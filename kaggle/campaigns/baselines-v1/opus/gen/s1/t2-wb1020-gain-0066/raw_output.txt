1. DIAGNOSIS
The binding failure is wideband stability, narrowly (mu_wide = 0.98). The output is still high-Q at the band edge (s22_max = -5.2 dB, delta_max = 0.26). The current-reuse drain is loaded by a series L2-C2 branch to ground. Above that branch's series resonance it is inductive, so at multi-GHz frequencies the drain sees an almost lossless inductive impedance. Through the combined Cgd of NM1/PM1 (gates AC-tied through C1), the Miller admittance jwCgd(1 + gm*Z_L) then has a negative real part, and the series gate inductor L1 adds resonant voltage gain at the gate. In that out-of-band region mu dips just below 1. No resistive element bounds the drain impedance where L2 dominates.

2. EDIT: add a damping resistor R6 across L2 (n2 to n4). C2 keeps it DC-isolated, so the bias is unchanged. At high frequency the L2-C2 branch then tends to a pure resistance to ground, a resistive HF termination that removes the inductive-load negative conductance and lowers the output Q. In-band, the sizer can choose R6 large enough that the 1-2 GHz gain is nearly unaffected.
```netlist
C C4 VIN1 n0
L L1 n0 n1
NMOS NM1 n2 n1 VSS VSS
C C1 n1 n3
PMOS PM1 n2 n3 VDD VDD
L L2 n2 n4
R R6 n2 n4
C C2 n4 VSS
C C3 n2 VOUT1
R R1 VDD n5
NMOS NM3 n5 n5 VSS VSS
R R2 n5 n1
PMOS PM2 n6 n6 VDD VDD
R R4 n6 VSS
R R5 n6 n3
```
3. PREDICTION: mu_min_wide_0.01-50GHz improves from 0.98 to about 1.15 (+0.15-0.2; it passes). s22_max improves by about 2 dB. s21 costs about 0.2 dB at most, and the in-band ripple gets flatter.
