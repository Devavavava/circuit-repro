1. DIAGNOSIS
The binding failure is wideband stability (mu_wide = 0.92). In-band mu_min is only 1.0006, and s22_max is -2.6 dB, so the output is very high-Q and nearly reflective at the band edge. The current-reuse drain sees a series L2-C2 branch to ground, which is inductive and almost lossless above its series resonance. That makes the drain load inductive at multi-GHz frequencies. Through the NM1 + PM1 Cgd (gates AC-tied via C1), it produces a negative-real Miller admittance jwCgd(1 + gm*Z_L) at the input, while L1 resonating with Cgs boosts the gate voltage. mu therefore falls below 1 out of band and only barely clears 1 in band. Nothing resistive damps the drain where L2 dominates.

2. EDIT: put a damping resistor R6 across L2 (n2 to n4). The bias is unchanged (C2 blocks DC). At high frequency the branch tends to R6 to ground, a resistive HF termination that bounds the drain impedance, removes the negative input conductance, and lowers the output Q. That improves s22 and flattens the 1-2 GHz response (ripple currently 0.93 dB).
```netlist
C C4 VIN1 n0
L L1 n0 n1
NMOS NM1 n2 n1 VSS VSS
C C1 n1 n3
PMOS PM1 n2 n3 VDD VDD
L L2 n2 n4
R R6 n2 n4
C C2 n4 VSS
C C3 n2 VOUT1
R R1 VDD n5
NMOS NM3 n5 n5 VSS VSS
R R2 n5 n1
PMOS PM2 n6 n6 VDD VDD
R R4 n6 VSS
R R5 n6 n3
```
3. PREDICTION: mu_min_wide_0.01-50GHz improves from 0.92 to about 1.1 (+0.2; it passes). In-band mu_min rises to about 1.15, s22_max improves by about 3 dB, and ripple drops by about 0.3 dB. Peak s21 costs about 0.3 dB, which the flatter band offsets for s21_min.
