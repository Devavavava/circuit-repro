1. DIAGNOSIS
The binding constraint is NF: 2.46 dB against 2.29 dB, while mu sits at exactly 1.00 in band and wide. The inverter input is a series gate inductor L1 into a purely capacitive gate, so the circuit has no noiseless mechanism for the 50-ohm real part. The match (S11 about -8.3 dB) instead relies on lossy real parts at the high-Q gate node: the bias feed resistors R2 and R5 (via C1) and the gate resistance. The Q of the series resonance amplifies their noise. The lack of resistive damping elsewhere is also why k and mu are pinned at 1.00. With no source degeneration, the optimizer cannot convert gm into a clean input resistance, and NF stays about 0.17 dB above the gate.

2. EDIT: add an inductive source-degeneration inductor Ls between the NM1 source and VSS. This gives Re{Zin} = gm_n*Ls/C_in, a noiseless real part, so the bias-feed resistors can be sized large and the lossy part of the match disappears. The series feedback also raises the input-side stability margin. DC bias is unchanged, since Ls is a DC short.
```netlist
C C4 VIN1 n0
L L1 n0 n1
NMOS NM1 n2 n1 ns VSS
L Ls ns VSS
C C1 n1 n3
PMOS PM1 n2 n3 VDD VDD
L L2 n2 n4
C C2 n4 VSS
C C3 n2 VOUT1
R R1 VDD n5
NMOS NM3 n5 n5 VSS VSS
R R2 n5 n1
PMOS PM2 n6 n6 VDD VDD
R R4 n6 VSS
R R5 n6 n3
```
3. PREDICTION: NF improves from 2.46 dB to about 2.15-2.2 dB (-0.25 to -0.3 dB). mu_min rises to about 1.05. S21 falls by about 0.3 dB at fixed sizing, which the optimizer can recover.
