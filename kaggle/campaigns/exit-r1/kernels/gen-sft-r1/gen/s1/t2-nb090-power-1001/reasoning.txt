The failure is primarily due to the noise figure (nf_db) being too high, exceeding the specification by ~0.23 dB. This is likely caused by the noise contributions from the biasing network and the current-reuse topology, particularly the diode-connected NMOS (NM3) and PMOS (PM2) used for biasing. These devices introduce additional noise due to their non-ideal behavior and the current mirroring mechanism, which degrades the noise performance of the LNA.

The original topology uses a current-reuse configuration with a shared DC path and a tuned load (L2+C2), which may lead to poor noise matching and increased noise contributions from the biasing network. The diode-connected NM3 and PM2 are directly tied to the DC path, potentially introducing noise and reducing the overall noise figure.

To address this, I propose a structural change that eliminates the direct biasing network from the main signal path and reconfigures the current-reuse topology to reduce noise contributions. The new topology uses a separate biasing network for the NMOS and PMOS transistors, decoupling the biasing from the main signal path. This reduces the noise contribution from the biasing network and improves noise matching.

The proposed netlist introduces a new NMOS transistor (NM3) connected to a different internal node (n5) and uses a separate resistor (R2) to ground, which helps in isolating the biasing network from the signal path. Additionally, the PMOS transistor (PM2) is connected to a different internal node (n6), allowing for a more stable and noise-friendly biasing configuration. This change reduces the noise contribution from the biasing network and improves the noise figure of the LNA.
