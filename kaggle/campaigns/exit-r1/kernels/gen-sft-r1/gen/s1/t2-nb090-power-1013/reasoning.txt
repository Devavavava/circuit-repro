The failure is primarily due to the noise figure (nf_db) being too high, exceeding the maximum allowed value of 2.1001 dB by a margin of -1.9035. This indicates that the noise performance of the LNA is insufficient, likely due to the noise contribution from the second stage and the biasing network. The current topology uses a current mirror for biasing, which introduces additional noise and may not provide optimal noise matching. Additionally, the lack of a source degeneration inductor in the first stage and the absence of a noise-canceling feedback path may contribute to the elevated noise floor.

To address this, I propose a structural change that introduces a source degeneration inductor (L2) in the first stage and a noise-canceling feedback path using a resistor (R2) and a capacitor (C2) to ground. This helps in reducing the noise contribution from the first stage and improves the noise matching. Additionally, the second stage is modified to include a source degeneration inductor (L3), which helps in stabilizing the second stage and reducing noise. The biasing network is also restructured to use a separate resistor (R1) for biasing the second stage, which reduces the noise contribution from the biasing network.
