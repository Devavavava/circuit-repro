The failure is primarily due to the poor stability margin (`mu_min_wide_0.01-50GHz`) of the two-stage LNA, which is insufficient to meet the specification. The original topology uses a current-mirror bias network (R1, NM3, R2, R3) that introduces parasitic feedback and increases the phase shift in the feedback loop, especially at higher frequencies. This degrades the stability margin, causing the circuit to oscillate or become unstable at the edges of the operating band.

To address this, the biasing network is restructured. Instead of using a current mirror with resistive biasing, the second stage (NM2) is directly biased through a series resistor (R2) from VDD to its gate, eliminating the current mirror and reducing the parasitic feedback path. This change simplifies the feedback loop, reduces phase shift, and improves the stability margin. Additionally, the drain load of the first stage is replaced with a simple inductor (L1) connected to VDD, and the second stage uses a different inductor (L2) for its drain load. The output capacitor (C2) is moved to the output node, ensuring a clean signal path without additional resonance effects that might destabilize the circuit.
