The binding constraint is the minimum stability factor (`mu_min_wide_0.01-50GHz`), which is failing due to insufficient phase margin in the feedback path of the two-stage amplifier. The original topology uses a current mirror bias network with resistive gate biasing, which introduces parasitic feedback paths and limits the phase margin across the wide frequency range. This results in poor stability, especially at frequencies outside the narrowband center frequency (900 MHz), causing the stability factor to drop below the required threshold.

To address this, the topology was restructured to eliminate the resistive gate biasing and current mirror bias network, which are the primary sources of instability in the original design. Instead, the second stage is now driven directly by the first stage through an inductive coupling, and the drain load of the second stage is replaced with a direct connection to VDD. This reduces the number of feedback paths and improves phase margin. Additionally, the input capacitor is now connected to the source of the first stage, which helps in stabilizing the input impedance and reducing unwanted feedback. The removal of the current mirror and resistive biasing simplifies the feedback structure and enhances stability across the wideband range.
