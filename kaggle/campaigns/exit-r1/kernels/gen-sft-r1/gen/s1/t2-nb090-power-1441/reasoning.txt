The failure is primarily due to poor input matching, as evidenced by the s11_max_db constraint being -2.33844 dB, which is significantly above the required -7.8774 dB. This indicates that the input impedance is not sufficiently matched to the source, leading to a poor return loss and limiting the overall performance of the LNA. The root cause lies in the structure of the first stage, where the input capacitor C1 is directly connected to the gate of NM1, and the drain is loaded with an inductor L1. This configuration may not provide sufficient impedance transformation at the desired frequency, especially when the second stage's interstage LC network introduces additional impedance mismatches.

To address this, I propose a structural change that introduces a series inductor at the input and modifies the drain load of the first stage to a resonant LC network. This reconfiguration enhances the input impedance matching by introducing a controlled inductive reactance at the input, allowing for better tuning at the target frequency. Additionally, the drain load is changed from a simple inductor to a resonant LC network, which improves the impedance transformation and stabilizes the gain of the first stage. These changes help improve the return loss (s11_max_db) and bring it within the required specification.
