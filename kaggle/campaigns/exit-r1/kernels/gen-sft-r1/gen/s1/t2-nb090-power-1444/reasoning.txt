The binding constraint is the input return loss (s11_max_db), which is failing by ~0.7 dB. This indicates that the input impedance is not sufficiently matched to the source, likely due to the lack of a dedicated input matching network and the reliance on the LC tank for both tuning and impedance transformation. The LC interstage network may be causing unwanted resonances or impedance mismatches at the input, especially when the second stage's gate is driven through a series inductor and capacitor. This configuration may not provide sufficient control over the input impedance, leading to a poor match and a higher-than-allowed s11_max_db.

To address this, I propose adding a series inductor (Lx) between the first stage's drain and the interstage LC network. This inductor helps decouple the first stage's output from the second stage's input, reducing the impact of the second stage's impedance on the first stage's output impedance. By doing so, the input impedance can be better controlled, improving the input match and reducing the s11_max_db. This change also helps in better isolation between the two stages, which can improve the overall stability and performance of the LNA.
