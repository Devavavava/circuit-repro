The failure is primarily due to the poor stability margin (`mu_min_wide_0.01-50GHz`) caused by the interstage LC network and the feedback paths in the original topology. The LC interstage (L2 and C2) creates a resonant path between the two stages, which can lead to unwanted feedback and instability, especially over a wide frequency range. The current mirror biasing (NM3, R1, R2, R3) also introduces additional feedback paths that may destabilize the system.

To address this, the topology is restructured to eliminate the interstage LC network and reduce feedback paths. The first stage is reconfigured to use a source-degeneration inductor (L2) instead of a drain load inductor, which helps stabilize the stage and improve the noise figure. The second stage is simplified to a common-source configuration with a direct connection to the output, avoiding the LC load that could cause instability. The biasing network is kept minimal, with NM3 connected to the source of NM2 instead of the gate, reducing feedback and improving stability.
