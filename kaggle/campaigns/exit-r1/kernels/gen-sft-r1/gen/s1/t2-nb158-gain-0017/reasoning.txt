The failure is due to the insufficient stability margin (mu_min_wide_0.01-50GHz) across the operating band. The anchor netlist uses a resistive shunt-feedback CS LNA with a shunt-peaked inductive load. While this topology provides good gain and noise figure, the stability margin is compromised over a wide frequency range, especially due to the feedback path and the inductive peaking network, which can lead to phase shifts that reduce the stability margin. The feedback resistor (R1) and the inductive load (L1) contribute to this instability, particularly at higher frequencies.

To address this, I propose a structural change to the topology by introducing a cascode configuration with an additional NMOS transistor (NM2) and a second inductor (L2) in the output path, forming a series-peaked inductive load. This change enhances the stability margin by reducing the feedback loop's phase shift over a wide frequency range. Additionally, a new node (n4) and a second capacitor (C2) are introduced to provide a more controlled impedance path and improve the stability. The addition of a second NMOS transistor (NM3) and a resistor (R1) in the bias path ensures proper DC biasing without affecting the stability.
