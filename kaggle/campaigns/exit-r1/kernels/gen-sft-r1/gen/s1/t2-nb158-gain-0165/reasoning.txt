The failure is due to insufficient gain in the LNA, as evidenced by the s21_db margin being -0.547203, which is below the required 24.5459 dB. The original topology employs a current-reuse push-pull configuration with a shared DC path and a tuned load. However, the push-pull structure introduces additional noise and impedance mismatches, which degrade the gain and noise figure. The NMOS and PMOS transistors are configured in a way that limits the voltage swing and amplification at the target frequency (1.575 GHz), resulting in a suboptimal gain.

To address this, the topology is restructured into a common-gate (CG) configuration for the NMOS transistor, which provides a low input impedance and high output impedance, improving the voltage gain. The PMOS transistor is reconfigured as a cascode stage, enhancing the output impedance and reducing the Miller effect, which helps in boosting the gain. The tuned load (L2+C2) is moved to a different node to ensure it is properly coupled to the output without introducing unnecessary feedback or impedance mismatches. Additionally, the DC biasing network is simplified to avoid parasitic effects that could degrade the gain at high frequencies.
