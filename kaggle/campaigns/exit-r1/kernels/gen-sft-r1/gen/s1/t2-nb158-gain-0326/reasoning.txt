The binding constraint `mu_min_wide_0.01-50GHz` failed due to poor stability across the wide frequency range, likely caused by the second-stage transistor (NM2) being too strongly coupled to the interstage LC network (L2 and C2). This coupling introduces a resonant peak in the feedback path at off-band frequencies, reducing the stability margin (`mu`) at lower frequencies. The structure of the anchor netlist allows for excessive feedback at these frequencies, especially due to the direct connection of the interstage LC network to the gate of NM2, which can lead to a narrow stability band and insufficient phase margin at the lower end of the band.

To address this, I modified the second-stage transistor's gate connection from the interstage LC network to a direct connection from the first-stage transistor's drain (n2). This change removes the LC interstage network from the feedback path of the second stage, reducing the resonant coupling that destabilizes the system at lower frequencies. By doing so, the feedback path becomes more predictable and less sensitive to off-band resonances, thereby improving the stability margin (`mu_min_wide_0.01-50GHz`) across the entire frequency range. The removal of the LC interstage network also simplifies the topology, reducing the number of inductors and improving the overall robustness of the LNA.
