The binding constraint is the insufficient gain (s21_db), which is below the required 25.4973 dB by approximately 10 dB. This is likely due to the two-stage structure's inability to achieve sufficient voltage gain at the target frequency, exacerbated by the interstage LC network and the biasing topology. The LC interstage (L2 and C2) may introduce a voltage drop or phase shift that reduces the overall gain. Additionally, the current-mirror biasing (R1, NM3, R2, R3) may not provide sufficient gate control over the transistors, limiting their transconductance and thus the gain.

To address this, I propose a structural change that replaces the two-stage tuned common-source topology with a single-stage common-gate (CG) LNA with an inductive source degeneration and a second stage using a common-source configuration with a tuned load. This change improves gain by leveraging the high-output impedance of the CG stage and the voltage gain of the CS stage. The inductive source degeneration (L2) in the CG stage helps stabilize the gain and reduce noise, while the tuned load (L3) in the CS stage enhances the voltage gain at the target frequency. The removal of the interstage LC network simplifies the signal path and reduces potential gain loss.
