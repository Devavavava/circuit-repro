The failure is due to the poor stability margin (`mu_min_wide_0.01-50GHz`) in the original two-stage LNA topology. The interstage LC network and the current-mirror biasing contribute to instability, especially over a wide frequency range. The second stage's gate is driven through a series resistor (R3) from the current mirror, which introduces phase shifts and reduces the stability margin at off-band frequencies. Additionally, the LC interstage network (L2 and C2) can resonate with parasitic capacitances and inductances, further degrading stability.

To address this, I propose a structural change that replaces the second stage's gate biasing and interstage LC network with a direct coupling through a capacitor, while adding a resistor in the source of the first stage to dampen oscillations and improve stability. This change reduces the number of inductors from 3 to 2 and modifies the biasing topology to avoid the series resistor from the current mirror to the second stage's gate, thereby reducing phase shifts and improving the stability margin.
