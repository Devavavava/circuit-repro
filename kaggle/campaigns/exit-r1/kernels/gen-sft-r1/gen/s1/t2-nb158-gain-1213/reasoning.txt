The original circuit uses a resistive shunt feedback and a shunt-peaked inductive load, which may not provide sufficient phase margin at higher frequencies, especially when the feedback is dominated by a resistor. This results in a lower stability factor, particularly in the wideband analysis.

To address this, I replaced the resistive shunt feedback with an active feedback topology using a PMOS transistor and additional inductive components. This change introduces a more controlled feedback path with a higher phase margin, improving stability across the frequency range. The addition of a second inductor and a PMOS transistor in the feedback path helps to stabilize the amplifier by providing a more predictable and controllable phase response, especially at higher frequencies.
