The binding constraint is the insufficient gain (s21_db), which is below the required 28.5304 dB by approximately 0.32 dB. The two-stage LNA's gain is limited by the second stage's performance, likely due to the lack of a direct feedback or impedance control mechanism between the stages. The current topology relies on LC resonant loads and DC-blocking capacitors, which may not provide sufficient impedance transformation or signal coupling at the operating frequency, leading to a suboptimal second-stage gain.

To address this, I propose a structural change that introduces a feedback path from the output to the second stage's gate via a resistor (Rrep1) and adds a direct coupling inductor (Lrep1) from the output to the second stage's source. This provides a controlled feedback mechanism and enhances the impedance matching between the stages, thereby improving the overall gain. Additionally, I replace the current mirror biasing with a simpler resistor-based biasing network (Rrep2 and Rrep3) to reduce complexity and potential noise contributions.
