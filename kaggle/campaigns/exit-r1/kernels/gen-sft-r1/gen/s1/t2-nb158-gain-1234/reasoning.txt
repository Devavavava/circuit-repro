The binding constraint is the stability metric `mu_min_wide_0.01-50GHz`, which measures the stability of the LNA over a wide frequency range. The failure indicates that the circuit is unstable at frequencies outside the narrowband design point, likely due to the interstage LC network and the biasing structure creating unintended feedback paths or resonances that reduce stability. The current mirror bias network introduces parasitic feedback paths that resonate with the inductors at off-band frequencies, degrading the stability margin.

To address this, I replaced the current mirror bias network with a simpler resistive biasing approach. Instead of using a current mirror (NM3) and resistive biasing (R2, R3) to the gates of NM1 and NM2, I connected the gates of NM1 and NM2 directly to a bias node (n5) via resistors (R2). This eliminates the current mirror and the associated feedback paths that were destabilizing the circuit at off-band frequencies. Additionally, I removed the LC interstage network (L2 and C2) and replaced it with a direct connection from the drain of NM1 to the gate of NM2, reducing the number of inductors and eliminating the resonant LC path that was causing instability. The output capacitor (C3) was reconnected to the drain of NM2, simplifying the output stage and reducing the number of inductors from three to two, which further improves stability.
