The binding constraint, `mu_min_wide_0.01-50GHz`, measures the stability margin of the LNA across a wide frequency range. The root cause lies in the feedback and impedance mismatch in the current-reuse topology. The original netlist uses a diode-connected NMOS (NM3) and PMOS (PM2) for biasing, which introduces a high-impedance path and potentially creates a feedback loop that destabilizes the amplifier at certain frequencies. Additionally, the shared DC path and the tuned load (L2+C2) may not provide sufficient impedance isolation, leading to a poor phase margin and reduced stability across the band.

This change reduces the feedback and phase shift that could destabilize the amplifier. Additionally, the PM2 transistor is now connected to a different node (n0) and its source is tied to VSS, which improves the impedance matching and isolates the feedback path.
