The failure is due to the insufficient stability margin (mu_min_wide_0.01-50GHz), which indicates that the circuit is unstable over a wide frequency range. This is likely caused by the interstage LC network (L2 and C2) creating a resonant feedback path that destabilizes the system, especially when the second-stage transistor (NM2) is driven by a signal that is not sufficiently isolated from the first stage. The current topology lacks a direct feedback path to the bias network or a mechanism to dampen the resonant feedback, leading to poor stability.

To address this, I added a resistor (Rrep1) between the bias node (n7) and the interstage LC network (n4). This introduces a small feedback path from the bias network to the interstage LC network, helping to dampen the resonant feedback and improve the stability margin. The resistor provides a low-impedance path at high frequencies, which suppresses unwanted oscillations and enhances the phase margin, directly addressing the instability.
