The failure is primarily due to insufficient gain (s21_db) in the current-reuse LNA topology. The binding constraint s21_db is at 9.67 dB, which is far below the required 22.86 dB. This indicates that the amplifier is not sufficiently amplifying the signal, likely due to the weak transconductance of the NMOS/PMOS push-pull configuration and the poor impedance matching caused by the current-reuse structure. The current-reuse topology introduces additional parasitic effects and reduces the effective transconductance of the amplifier, leading to a low gain. Additionally, the output impedance of the LNA is not properly matched to the load, further degrading the gain.

To address this, I propose a structural change to the LNA topology by replacing the push-pull current-reuse configuration with a single-stage NMOS amplifier with an inductive source degeneration and a resistive load. This configuration enhances the transconductance and improves the impedance matching, leading to a significant increase in gain. The inductive source degeneration helps in stabilizing the amplifier and improving the linearity, while the resistive load ensures a better match to the output impedance. This change also simplifies the biasing network and removes the current-reuse structure, which was causing the low gain and poor performance.
