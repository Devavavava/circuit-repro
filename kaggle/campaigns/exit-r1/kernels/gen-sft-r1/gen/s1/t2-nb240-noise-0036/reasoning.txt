The failure is due to the insufficient stability margin (`mu_min_wide_0.01-50GHz`) caused by the lack of a second-stage feedback or damping mechanism in the original topology. The single-stage resistive shunt feedback and shunt-peaked inductor load are insufficient to suppress oscillations across the wide frequency range, especially at higher frequencies. This leads to a reduction in the stability factor (`mu`) at the edges of the operating band, causing the circuit to fail the stability constraint.

To address this, I added a second-stage feedback path using a PMOS transistor (`PM1`) and a capacitor (`C2`) to provide additional damping and improve the stability margin. The PMOS transistor introduces a second feedback path from the output to the input, enhancing the phase margin and suppressing oscillations. The capacitor `C2` across the feedback resistor `R1` helps to further stabilize the circuit by reducing high-frequency gain variations.
