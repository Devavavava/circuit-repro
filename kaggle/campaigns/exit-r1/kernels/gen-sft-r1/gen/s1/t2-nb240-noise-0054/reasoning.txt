The failure is primarily due to insufficient gain in the LNA, as evidenced by the s21_db margin being -0.533, which indicates the achieved gain (10.6675 dB) is below the required minimum (22.842 dB). This suggests that the amplifier's transconductance and output impedance are not sufficiently optimized to achieve the desired gain at 2.4 GHz. The current-reuse topology, while effective in reducing noise, may not be providing enough signal amplification in this configuration. The use of a PMOS push-pull stage and the tuned load may be contributing to a lower gain due to poor impedance matching or insufficient transconductance.

To address this, I propose a structural change by replacing the PMOS push-pull stage with a NMOS cascode configuration. This topology improves gain by increasing the output impedance and enhancing the transconductance of the amplifier. The cascode configuration also helps in stabilizing the amplifier and improving its linearity. Additionally, the current mirror configuration is replaced with a simpler NMOS diode-connected transistor to provide a more stable current reference. The removal of the PMOS stage and the tuned load simplifies the circuit and reduces parasitic effects that may be degrading the gain.
