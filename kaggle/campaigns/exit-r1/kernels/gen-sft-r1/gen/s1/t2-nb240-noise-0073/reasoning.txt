The failure is primarily due to the insufficient gain (s21_db) in the current-reuse push-pull LNA topology. The anchor netlist uses a current-reuse configuration with a shared DC path and a tuned load, but the push-pull structure introduces significant signal cancellation and impedance mismatches at the output, leading to a poor gain and a low S21. The current-reuse approach also complicates the impedance matching, especially at the output node, which is critical for achieving high gain in a narrowband LNA.

To address this, I replaced the push-pull structure with a single-stage common-gate (CG) topology, which inherently provides better gain and improved impedance matching at the output. The CG configuration also simplifies the biasing and DC path, reducing the risk of signal cancellation and allowing for a more straightforward impedance transformation from the input to the output. The removal of the push-pull structure and the associated current mirror network reduces the complexity of the biasing and allows the output to be directly connected to the tuned inductor and capacitor, improving the overall gain and bandwidth.

The new netlist uses a single NMOS transistor as the amplifying element in a common-gate configuration, with a tuned inductor and capacitor at the output. This topology is more suitable for achieving the required gain and noise figure in a narrowband LNA.
