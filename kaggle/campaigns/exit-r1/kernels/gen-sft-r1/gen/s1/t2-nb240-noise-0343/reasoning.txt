The failure is due to the poor stability margin (mu_min_wide_0.01-50GHz) caused by the interstage LC network, which introduces resonances that destabilize the amplifier over a wide frequency range. The original topology's LC interstage (L2 and C2) creates a feedback path that reduces stability, especially when the second stage's output is directly coupled to the first stage's input through the LC network. This feedback, combined with the second stage's tuned load (L3), causes a reduction in the stability factor (mu) across the band, leading to the failure of the mu_min constraint.

To address this, I restructured the topology by removing the LC interstage and instead using a direct connection between the first and second stages, with a source inductance (L2) to improve stability. The second stage is now configured with a source inductance and a drain capacitor (C2), which helps in isolating the stages and reducing feedback paths that destabilize the amplifier. The bias network is kept minimal, with a current mirror (NM3 and R1) biasing the gates through resistors (R2 and R3). This change eliminates the resonant feedback path from the interstage LC network, improving the stability margin across the band.
