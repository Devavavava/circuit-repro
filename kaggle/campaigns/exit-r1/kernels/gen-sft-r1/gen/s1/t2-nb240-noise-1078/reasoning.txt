The binding constraint is the stability metric `mu_min_wide_0.01-50GHz`, which is failing due to insufficient phase margin across the operating band. The original topology features a two-stage common-source amplifier with LC-tuned loads and an interstage LC network. While this configuration enhances gain and noise performance, it introduces a resonant feedback path between the two stages via the interstage LC network (L2 and C2), which causes a phase dip at higher frequencies, reducing the stability margin. This is exacerbated by the lack of a direct feedback path or damping mechanism to suppress oscillatory behavior.

To address this, I added a resistor (ZR1) from the drain of NM1 (n2) to the interstage node (n4), and a capacitor (ZC1) from VDD to the bias node (n7). The resistor ZR1 provides a damping effect by introducing a controlled impedance in the interstage feedback path, which reduces the resonant peak and stabilizes the phase margin. The capacitor ZC1 decouples the bias network from the power supply, reducing noise coupling and improving the stability of the bias point, especially at higher frequencies. These changes directly address the phase instability caused by the LC interstage network and improve the stability margin across the band.
