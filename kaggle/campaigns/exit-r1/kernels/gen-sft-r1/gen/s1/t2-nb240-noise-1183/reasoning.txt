The binding constraint is the insufficient forward gain (s21_db), which is below the required minimum of 17.0744 dB. The original circuit features a current-reuse push-pull configuration with a tuned load, but the gain is compromised due to the complex feedback and impedance mismatches introduced by the current-reuse structure and the shared DC path. The PMOS and NMOS transistors are operating in a configuration that limits the voltage gain, especially at the target frequency of 2.4 GHz.

To address this, I propose a structural change to a simpler, single-stage NMOS common-gate (CG) LNA topology with an inductive source degeneration and a resistive load. This configuration inherently provides higher gain due to the absence of current reuse and the use of a direct voltage amplification path. The inductive source degeneration (L2) helps in improving the transconductance and reducing noise, while the resistive load (R2) ensures a good impedance match and stable gain. The addition of a second NMOS transistor (NM2) in a common-source configuration provides additional gain and helps in achieving the required forward gain. The removal of the PMOS and the current-reuse structure simplifies the circuit and reduces the feedback effects that were limiting the gain.
