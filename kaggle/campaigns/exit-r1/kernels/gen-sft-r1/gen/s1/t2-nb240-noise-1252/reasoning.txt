The binding constraint, `mu_min_wide_0.01-50GHz`, is failing due to poor stability across the wide frequency range. The original topology relies on a current mirror bias network with resistive feedback (R2 and R3) to bias the gates of NM1 and NM2. This configuration introduces a low-frequency pole and can lead to phase shifts that reduce the stability margin at off-band frequencies, especially when the interstage LC network resonates with the bias network's impedance. The lack of a direct feedback path from the output to the bias network limits the ability to stabilize the system across the entire 0.01–50 GHz range, causing the stability margin (`mu_min`) to dip below the required threshold.

To address this, the bias network is restructured to include a direct feedback path from the output (VOUT1) to the bias node (n7) via a capacitor (ZC1). This change introduces a high-frequency zero that counteracts the phase shift introduced by the bias resistors, thereby improving the stability margin across the wideband range. Additionally, the NM2 drain is connected to the interstage LC node (n4) instead of VDD, which reduces the parasitic feedback path from the output to the second stage and improves the impedance matching at the interstage node, helping to maintain a higher stability margin.
