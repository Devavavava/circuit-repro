The binding constraint is the stability factor `mu_min_wide_0.01-50GHz`, which is too low, indicating potential instability over a wide frequency range. This is likely due to the interstage LC network (L2 and C2) creating a resonant feedback path that reduces the stability margin at off-band frequencies. The current topology's interstage LC network, while beneficial for tuning and gain, introduces a resonant frequency that can interact poorly with the output stage, especially at frequencies outside the narrowband design point.

To address this, I restructured the LNA to eliminate the interstage LC network and instead use a direct connection between the two stages, with a single inductor in the output stage. This change reduces the number of inductors from three to two, simplifying the feedback path and improving stability across a broader frequency range. The second stage now uses a common-source configuration with its drain connected directly to VDD through an inductor (L2), and the output is DC-blocked by a capacitor (C2). The bias network remains similar, with a current mirror (NM3, R1, R2) providing bias to the first stage (NM1) and a separate resistor (R3) biasing the second stage (NM2). This topology reduces the risk of resonant feedback paths and improves the stability factor over the wide frequency range.
