The failure is due to the poor stability margin (mu_min_wide_0.01-50GHz) caused by the feedback configuration and the shunt-peaked load. The resistive feedback (R1) and the shunt inductor (L1) create a feedback path that destabilizes the amplifier over a wide frequency range, especially at higher frequencies beyond the operating band. The single-stage common-source (CS) topology with a resistive feedback and a shunt-peaked load lacks sufficient phase margin and stability compensation, leading to a negative stability margin.

To address this, I replace the single-stage resistive feedback CS LNA with a two-stage topology featuring a cascode structure and a more stable feedback configuration. The first stage uses a PMOS transistor (PM1) with a shunt inductor (L1) and a capacitor (C1) to provide a stable input impedance. The second stage is a NMOS transistor (NM1) with a cascode configuration (PM2) and additional inductors (L2) and capacitors (C2, C3, C4) to improve stability and bandwidth. The addition of a current source (NM3 and R1) and a bias network (R2, R4, R5) ensures proper biasing without explicitly defining values. This topology enhances phase margin and reduces feedback instability across the wide frequency range.
