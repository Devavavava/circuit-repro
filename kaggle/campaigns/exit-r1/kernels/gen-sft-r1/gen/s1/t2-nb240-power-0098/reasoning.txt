The failure is primarily due to the insufficient gain (s21_db) in the current-reuse push-pull LNA topology. The anchor netlist employs a push-pull configuration with NMOS and PMOS stages, but the gain is limited by the current-reuse structure and the shared DC path, which introduces parasitic effects and reduces the voltage swing. The tuned load (L2+C2) is off the main DC path, but the overall gain is still too low, as evidenced by the s21_db margin of -0.722726, indicating a 5.95898 dB gain, which is far below the required 21.4913 dB. The NMOS and PMOS stages are not effectively amplifying the signal, and the current reuse may be causing a reduction in the transconductance of the main amplifier stage.

To address this, I propose a structural change to a common-gate NMOS stage with a cascode configuration and a separate inductive load. This topology improves gain by providing a higher transconductance and reducing the Miller effect. The NMOS cascode configuration (NM1 and NM2) increases the output impedance, which boosts the voltage gain. The inductive load (L2) is placed directly on the output node, ensuring a high impedance at the operating frequency, which enhances the gain. The current source (NM3 and R2) provides a stable bias current, while the resistive divider (R1 and R4) ensures proper biasing. The input capacitor (C1) blocks DC while allowing the RF signal to pass. This configuration avoids the push-pull structure and the current-reuse complexity that may be limiting the gain.
