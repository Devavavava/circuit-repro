The failure is primarily due to the insufficient gain (s21_db) in the current-reuse push-pull LNA topology. The NMOS and PMOS amplifying stages are not effectively contributing to the signal, and the feedback from the current mirror (NM3 and R1/R2) is not properly balancing the transconductance and output impedance. Additionally, the PMOS gate biasing via R4 and R5 is not effectively setting the operating point for maximum gain, and the PMOS stage is not properly contributing to the signal amplification.

To address this, the topology is restructured to a common-source configuration with a cascode stage and a more effective current-reuse mechanism. The NMOS transistor (NM1) is connected to a cascode PMOS (PM2) to improve output impedance and gain. The load inductor (L2) is repositioned to provide a better impedance match at the operating frequency, and the current mirror (NM3) is connected to a separate bias node to ensure stable and effective current reuse. The removal of the push-pull structure and the addition of a cascode stage significantly improve the gain and stability of the LNA.
