The binding constraint, `mu_min_wide_0.01-50GHz`, is failing due to poor stability across the wide frequency range. The original topology relies on a current-mirror bias network with resistive feedback to both stages, which introduces parasitic feedback paths and weakens the stability margin at off-band frequencies. This is exacerbated by the LC interstage network, which, while beneficial for narrowband tuning, may resonate or couple with parasitic elements outside the target band, causing instability.

To address this, I replaced the current-mirror bias network with a direct biasing approach using a resistor from the VDD to the gate of the second stage (R1), and a resistor from the drain of the first stage to VDD (R2). This eliminates the current mirror and reduces the number of feedback paths, improving stability. Additionally, I moved the LC interstage network to be directly between the drain of the first stage and the gate of the second stage, ensuring a cleaner signal path and reducing the risk of parasitic coupling. The output capacitor (C2) is now placed directly between the output and VDD, simplifying the output stage and improving high-frequency response.
