The failure is due to the poor stability margin (mu_min_wide_0.01-50GHz) caused by the interstage LC network and the feedback paths introduced by the current mirror biasing network. The current mirror's feedback through R2 and R3 creates a low-impedance path that destabilizes the system, especially at higher frequencies. The LC interstage network (L2 and C2) also introduces resonant peaks that can cause instability when combined with the feedback paths.

To address this, I restructured the topology to eliminate the current mirror feedback and reduce the number of inductors. The new topology uses a single-stage common-source amplifier with a source inductor (L2) for noise reduction and a second-stage cascode configuration (NM2) with a load inductor (L3) for gain and stability. The input is directly coupled through a capacitor (C1), and the output is decoupled through another capacitor (C2). This configuration reduces the number of feedback paths and eliminates the resonant LC interstage network, leading to improved stability and meeting the mu_min constraint.
