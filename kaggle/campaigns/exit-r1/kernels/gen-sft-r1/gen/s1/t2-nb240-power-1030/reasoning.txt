The failure is due to the noise figure (nf_db) being too high, exceeding the specification by approximately 0.2 dB. This is likely caused by the noise contributions from the biasing network and the second stage, particularly the current mirror NM3 and the resistive biasing network (R1, R2, R3), which introduce additional noise and degrade the noise performance of the LNA. The second-stage transistor NM2 is also likely contributing more noise than desired due to the lack of a proper noise-shaping mechanism or impedance matching in the interstage network.

To address this, I propose a structural change that eliminates the resistive biasing network and replaces it with a direct gate biasing approach using a separate bias transistor, while also modifying the interstage network to improve noise performance. This involves removing the resistors R2 and R3, and instead biasing NM2 through a direct connection from a bias transistor (NM3) to its gate. Additionally, the interstage LC network is restructured to improve impedance matching and reduce noise coupling.
