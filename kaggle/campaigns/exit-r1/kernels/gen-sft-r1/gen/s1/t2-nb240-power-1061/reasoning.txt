This is likely due to the single-stage CS amplifier with resistive feedback and shunt-peaked load, which can introduce unwanted phase shifts and resonance peaks that reduce stability margins. The feedback resistor and the inductor in the shunt-peaked load may create a resonant frequency that interacts poorly with the amplifier's poles, leading to instability at certain frequencies.

To address this, I replaced the resistive feedback with a second NMOS transistor in a common-gate configuration, forming a cascode structure. This adds a second stage that helps isolate the output from the input, improving the phase margin and stability. The common-gate transistor (NM2) provides a low-impedance path to ground, reducing the feedback phase shift and stabilizing the circuit. The inductor L2 and capacitor C2 in the output stage help further stabilize the circuit by filtering out high-frequency noise and providing a more predictable impedance profile.
