The failure is due to the poor stability margin (mu_min_wide_0.01-50GHz) caused by the interstage LC network and the biasing structure. The original topology uses a current mirror with resistive biasing, which introduces parasitic feedback paths and reduces the stability margin across the operating band. The LC interstage also introduces resonant peaks that can cause phase shifts, further degrading stability.

To address this, I modified the biasing structure by replacing the resistive biasing with a direct current mirror configuration. This eliminates the resistive feedback paths and improves the phase margin. Additionally, I restructured the LC interstage to reduce the resonant effects and improve the stability across the band. The new topology uses a direct current mirror (NM3) connected to the source of NM2, which provides a more stable bias and reduces the impact of the interstage LC network on the stability margin.
