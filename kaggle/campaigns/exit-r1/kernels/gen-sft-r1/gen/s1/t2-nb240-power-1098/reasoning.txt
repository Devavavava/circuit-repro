The binding constraint is the insufficient gain (s21_db), which is limited by the poor impedance matching and the low transconductance of the current-reuse push-pull topology. The push-pull configuration introduces additional losses and phase shifts at the operating frequency, reducing the overall gain. Additionally, the shared DC path and the current-reuse structure may be causing signal degradation and poor power transfer.

To address this, I replaced the push-pull configuration with a single-stage common-source amplifier with an inductive source degeneration and a resistive load. This topology improves the transconductance and impedance matching at the operating frequency, leading to higher gain. The inductive source degeneration helps to increase the effective transconductance of the transistor by reducing the Miller effect and improving the voltage gain. The resistive load provides a better impedance match and improves the stability of the amplifier.

The netlist uses a common-source NMOS transistor with an inductive source degeneration and a resistive load, which is a more effective topology for achieving high gain and good impedance matching at the operating frequency.
