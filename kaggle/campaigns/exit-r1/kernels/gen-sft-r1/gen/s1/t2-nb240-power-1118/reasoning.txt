The binding constraint is the input return loss (s11_max_db), which is failing by approximately 0.3 dB. This suggests that the input impedance mismatch is too large at the center frequency (2.4 GHz), likely due to the lack of an explicit input matching network. In the original topology, the input is directly coupled through a capacitor (C1) to the gate of NM1, with no additional components to tune the input impedance. This results in a poor impedance match, especially at the narrowband frequency of operation.

To address this, I added an explicit input matching network using an inductor (L1) and capacitor (C1) in series, forming a resonant LC circuit at the target frequency. This helps to tune the input impedance to better match the source, thereby improving the return loss. Additionally, I modified the biasing structure to remove the series resistors (R2 and R3) that were feeding the gates of NM1 and NM2, as they can introduce parasitic effects and degrade the impedance match. Instead, I used a direct current mirror biasing structure (NM3 and R1) to bias the gates, which simplifies the topology and improves the high-frequency performance. Finally, I restructured the output coupling to use a capacitor (C2) directly from the second stage to the output, ensuring minimal loading and preserving the signal integrity.
