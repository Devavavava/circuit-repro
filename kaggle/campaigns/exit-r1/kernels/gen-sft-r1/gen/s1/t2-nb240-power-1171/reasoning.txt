The original topology uses a two-stage CS LNA with LC interstage tuning, which introduces resonant feedback paths that can lead to instability at off-band frequencies. The current mirror biasing and the LC interstage network may be creating parasitic feedback loops that reduce the stability margin, especially when the second stage's output is directly connected to the LC load, without sufficient isolation or damping.

To address this, the topology is revised to improve stability by introducing a source inductor (L2) in the second stage and reconfiguring the bias network to isolate the stages better. The first stage is now a common-gate (CG) configuration (NM1), which inherently provides better stability and lower noise compared to a common-source (CS) configuration. The second stage remains a CS configuration (NM2) but with a source inductor (L2) that adds inductive peaking and suppresses parasitic feedback. The LC interstage network is replaced with a direct inductive coupling (L1) from the first stage's drain to the second stage's gate, which simplifies the feedback path and improves stability. Additionally, the bias network is restructured to use a separate current mirror (NM3) with a resistor divider (R2, R3) to decouple the bias from the signal path, reducing noise and improving stability.
