The failure is due to the poor stability of the two-stage LNA across a wide frequency range, as indicated by the negative margin for `mu_min_wide_0.01-50GHz`. The original topology uses a current mirror biasing network with resistive feedback to both stages, which introduces parasitic feedback paths and weakens the stability margin at off-resonant frequencies. This is exacerbated by the LC interstage network, which, while effective for tuning, does not inherently suppress oscillatory behavior across the wide band.

To address this, I redesigned the biasing network to use a direct current mirror configuration (NM3's drain connected to VDD), which eliminates the resistive feedback path and reduces the parasitic coupling between stages. Additionally, I replaced the LC interstage network with a direct inductive coupling (L2) from the first stage’s drain to the second stage’s source, which improves stability by reducing the resonant feedback loop and simplifying the signal path. This change reduces the number of inductors from three to two, simplifying the topology and enhancing stability.
