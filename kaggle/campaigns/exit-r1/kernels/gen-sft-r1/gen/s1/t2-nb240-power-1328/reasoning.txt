The failure is due to the poor stability margin (`mu_min_wide_0.01-50GHz`) caused by the resonant LC interstage network (L2 and C2), which introduces a feedback path that destabilizes the amplifier across a wide frequency range. This feedback path, especially at frequencies near the interstage resonance, reduces the phase margin and causes the stability factor (`mu`) to dip below the required threshold. The current topology's LC interstage network is not sufficiently damped or isolated, leading to a negative stability margin at some frequencies within the 0.01–50 GHz band.

To address this, I introduce a damping capacitor (ZC1) across the interstage LC network (L2 and C2). This capacitor provides a low-impedance path at higher frequencies, reducing the feedback effect and improving the stability margin. The capacitor is placed between the interstage node (n4) and the second-stage gate node (n5), effectively shunting high-frequency oscillations and preventing unwanted resonance feedback. This change maintains the narrowband tuning at f0 = 2.4 GHz while ensuring a stable operation across the wide frequency range.
