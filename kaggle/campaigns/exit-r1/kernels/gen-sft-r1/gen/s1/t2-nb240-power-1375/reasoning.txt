The failure is due to the poor stability margin (`mu_min_wide_0.01-50GHz`) caused by the second-stage transistor (NM2) being too strongly coupled to the first stage through the interstage LC network. This coupling introduces a resonant peak in the feedback path, reducing the phase margin and destabilizing the amplifier at frequencies outside the narrowband design point (f0 = 2.4 GHz). The current topology lacks sufficient isolation between stages, especially at higher frequencies, which leads to a negative stability margin.

To address this, I added a capacitor (ZC1) between the bias node (n7) and the output node (VOUT1), and another capacitor (ZC2) between VDD and the drain of the first-stage transistor (n2). These capacitors provide additional feedback paths and decouple the bias network from the output, improving stability by reducing high-frequency coupling between stages. The capacitor ZC1 also helps to filter out high-frequency noise from the bias network, while ZC2 improves the power supply rejection and stabilizes the first stage's operating point.
