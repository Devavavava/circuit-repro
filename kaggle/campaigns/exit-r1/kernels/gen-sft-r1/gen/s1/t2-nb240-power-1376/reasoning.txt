The failure is primarily due to the poor stability margin (`mu_min_wide_0.01-50GHz`) caused by the interstage LC network and the feedback path through the bias network. The current structure introduces parasitic feedback paths and resonant peaks in the stability bandwidth, which reduce the phase margin and lead to instability at certain frequencies. This is exacerbated by the biasing network, which uses resistive paths from the bias reference to the gates of NM1 and NM2, creating a feedback path that degrades the stability margin.

To address this, the bias network is restructured to eliminate the direct resistive feedback path from the bias reference to the gates of the amplifying transistors. Instead, the bias is applied directly to the source of NM2, and an additional NMOS transistor (ZN1) is used to create a local feedback path from the drain of NM2 to the bias reference, ensuring a more controlled and stable biasing scheme. This change reduces the parasitic feedback and improves the phase margin, directly addressing the stability issue.
