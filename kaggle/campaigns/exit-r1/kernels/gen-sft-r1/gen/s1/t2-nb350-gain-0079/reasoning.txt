The binding constraint is the insufficient gain (s21_db), which is limited by the low transconductance and poor impedance matching in the current-reuse topology. The push-pull configuration introduces parasitic effects and reduces the effective transconductance of the amplifier, especially at high frequencies. The tuned load (L2+C2) is not effectively coupled to the output, and the current-reuse structure introduces additional noise and signal loss due to the multiple stages and feedback paths.

To address this, I replaced the push-pull current-reuse topology with a single-stage common-gate (CG) NMOS amplifier, which offers higher transconductance and better impedance matching at the desired frequency. The CG configuration provides a low input impedance and high output impedance, improving the voltage gain and reducing the signal loss. Additionally, the inductive load (L2) is directly connected to the output, ensuring a stronger coupling and better resonance at the target frequency. The biasing network is simplified to a single diode-connected NMOS transistor (NM3) and a resistor (R2), which ensures stable biasing without introducing unnecessary noise or signal degradation.
