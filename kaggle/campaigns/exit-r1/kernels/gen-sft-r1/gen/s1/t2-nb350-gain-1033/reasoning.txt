The binding constraint is the stability factor `mu_min_wide_0.01-50GHz`, which is negative, indicating the circuit is unstable over a wide frequency range. The original topology uses a two-stage common-source LNA with LC interstage tuning and a current-mirror bias network. While this topology achieves acceptable noise figure, gain, and power consumption, it lacks sufficient feedback and stability mechanisms to prevent oscillations at frequencies outside the narrowband center frequency (3.5 GHz). The LC interstage network and the current mirror provide some stability, but they are insufficient to maintain a positive stability factor across the 0.01–50 GHz range.

To address this, I replaced the two-stage CS LNA with a single-stage common-gate (CG) LNA with an inductive source degeneration and a second stage using a common-drain (CD) configuration. This topology inherently provides more stability due to the CG stage's inherent stability and the CD stage's low output impedance, which reduces feedback loops that could lead to instability. The inductive source degeneration (L2) in the CG stage helps control the phase shift and improve the stability factor. The bias network was simplified to a current mirror without resistive biasing, which reduces parasitic feedback paths and enhances stability.
