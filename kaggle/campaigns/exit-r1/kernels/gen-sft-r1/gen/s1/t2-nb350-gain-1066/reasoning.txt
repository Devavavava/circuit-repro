The binding constraint, `mu_min_wide_0.01-50GHz`, measures the stability of the LNA across a wide frequency range, and its failure indicates poor stability outside the narrowband center frequency (f0 = 3.5 GHz). The original two-stage LNA topology, while effective at f0, suffers from poor out-of-band stability due to the LC interstage network and the second-stage's tuned load. These components introduce resonances and phase shifts that reduce the stability margin (`mu`) at frequencies far from f0, particularly at lower frequencies (as seen in the wideband stability band from 3.43 GHz to 3.57 GHz). The current mirror biasing and the second-stage's tuned load exacerbate this by creating feedback paths that are unstable at off-band frequencies.

To address this, I restructured the LNA into a single-stage topology with a source-degeneration inductor and a direct coupling from the first stage to the second, eliminating the LC interstage network. This reduces the number of resonant components and feedback paths that destabilize the circuit at off-band frequencies. The new topology also uses a source-degeneration inductor (L2) to improve linearity and noise performance while maintaining a stable input impedance. The second stage is now a common-gate configuration (NM2), which inherently provides better stability and a flatter phase response over a wide frequency range. The removal of the LC interstage network and the use of a direct coupling via a capacitor (C2) simplifies the feedback paths and reduces the risk of instability at off-band frequencies. These changes improve the wideband stability margin (`mu_min_wide_0.01-50GHz`) and meet the required stability constraints.
