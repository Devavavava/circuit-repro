The failure is due to insufficient gain in the LNA, as evidenced by the s21_db margin being -0.275164, which indicates the achieved gain (10.3429 dB) is below the required 14.2693 dB. This is likely caused by the current-reuse topology and the shared DC path, which may introduce unwanted feedback or reduce the transconductance contribution of the amplifier stages. The push-pull configuration with NMOS and PMOS transistors may also be causing a reduction in gain due to phase and amplitude mismatches between the two stages.

To address this, I propose a structural change to the topology by replacing the push-pull configuration with a single-stage NMOS amplifier with a resistive load and an inductive load for improved gain and stability. The NMOS transistor is connected directly to the inductor, and the output is taken from the inductor node. This configuration avoids the current-reuse structure and simplifies the gain path, reducing potential losses and improving the overall gain of the amplifier. The resistive load (R2) and inductive load (L2) help in maintaining a stable and high-gain operation at the target frequency.
