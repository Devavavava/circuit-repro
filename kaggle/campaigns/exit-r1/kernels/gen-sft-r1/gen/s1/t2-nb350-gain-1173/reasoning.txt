The binding constraint, `mu_min_wide_0.01-50GHz`, measures the minimum stability factor across a wide frequency range, and its failure indicates poor stability in the LNA. The original two-stage topology with LC interstage coupling introduces resonances and feedback paths that destabilize the circuit at off-band frequencies. The current mirror biasing and the LC interstage network create parasitic feedback paths that reduce the stability margin, especially outside the narrowband center frequency (3.5 GHz). This is exacerbated by the lack of damping mechanisms in the interstage LC network, which allows for unwanted oscillations or resonances at higher frequencies.

To address this, I replaced the LC interstage coupling with a direct inductive connection between the two stages. This eliminates the resonant LC network that introduces parasitic feedback, thereby improving the stability margin across the wide frequency range. The new configuration uses a single inductor (L2) to couple the two stages directly, reducing the number of resonant elements and simplifying the feedback path. Additionally, the output capacitor (C2) is placed directly between the second stage and the output, ensuring a clean DC block without introducing additional resonances. The biasing network remains unchanged, as it was already sized to meet the current and noise constraints.
