The failure is due to the poor stability margin (mu_min_wide_0.01-50GHz) caused by the interstage LC network and the biasing configuration. The interstage LC (L2 and C2) introduces a resonant peak that can cause instability over a wide frequency range, especially when combined with the current mirror biasing network. The current mirror (NM3, R1, R2, R3) introduces feedback that interacts poorly with the LC network, reducing the stability margin.

To address this, I restructured the circuit to eliminate the interstage LC network and replace it with a direct connection between the two stages, using a source degeneration inductor (L2) in the second stage. This reduces the resonant feedback that causes instability. Additionally, I modified the biasing network to use a separate biasing inductor (L1) to isolate the biasing from the signal path, improving stability. The output capacitor (C2) was moved to the output node to ensure proper signal coupling without introducing unwanted resonances.
