The binding constraint, `mu_min_wide_0.01-50GHz`, is failing due to poor stability across the wide frequency range. The original topology uses a two-stage common-source configuration with LC-tuned loads and an interstage LC network. While this structure provides good gain and noise figure at the center frequency (3.5 GHz), the interstage LC network and the second stage's load configuration create resonances and phase shifts that reduce stability margins at off-band frequencies. This is exacerbated by the current mirror bias network, which introduces additional feedback paths that can destabilize the system over a wide bandwidth.

To address this, I replaced the second stage’s tuned load with a direct connection to VDD and added a shunt capacitor (C2) to the first stage's drain to suppress high-frequency oscillations. This reduces the resonant feedback effects between stages and stabilizes the amplifier over the wide frequency range. Additionally, I restructured the current mirror to directly bias the second stage's gate, eliminating unnecessary resistive elements that could contribute to phase shifts and instability.
