The failure is due to the poor stability margin (`mu_min_wide_0.01-50GHz`) caused by the lack of a proper feedback path and insufficient impedance control in the second stage. The second-stage NMOS (NM2) is directly driven by the LC interstage network, which lacks a controlled impedance path to the output, leading to a narrow stability band and poor out-of-band performance. This is exacerbated by the absence of a direct feedback or impedance-matching element between the second stage and the output, resulting in a lower stability factor at frequencies beyond the center frequency.

To address this, I added a resistor (ZR1) connecting the second-stage drain (n6) to the output node (VOUT1). This introduces a controlled feedback path and helps stabilize the amplifier by providing a defined impedance at the output, improving the stability margin without significantly degrading the gain or noise figure. This change also helps in shaping the output impedance and reducing the risk of oscillation at higher frequencies.
