The failure is due to insufficient gain in the LNA, as evidenced by the s21_db margin of -0.398332, which indicates the gain is below the required 15.9826 dB. The anchor netlist uses a push-pull current-reuse topology with a tuned load (L2+C2), but the gain is limited by the output impedance mismatch and the lack of a proper feedback mechanism to enhance the transconductance. The NMOS and PMOS stages are not optimally coupled, leading to a weaker signal amplification at the target frequency.

To address this, I replaced the push-pull topology with a common-gate (CG) stage for the NMOS transistor (NM2) and reconfigured the load network to improve impedance matching and increase the transconductance. The CG stage provides a lower input impedance and better high-frequency performance, which enhances the gain. Additionally, I replaced the current mirror biasing with a direct NMOS biasing (NM3) and adjusted the load inductors and capacitors to ensure a better resonance at the target frequency (3.5 GHz). The removal of the PMOS push-pull stage and the addition of the CG stage reduce the signal loss and improve the overall gain.
