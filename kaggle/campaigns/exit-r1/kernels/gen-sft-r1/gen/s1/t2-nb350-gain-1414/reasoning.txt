The failure is due to the poor stability margin (mu_min_wide_0.01-50GHz) caused by the resonant LC interstage and the current-mirror biasing network. The interstage LC network (L2 and C2) introduces a second resonance that interacts poorly with the biasing network (R1, NM3, R2, R3), leading to a narrow stability band and a low minimum stability factor across the wideband. This is exacerbated by the current mirror's feedback path, which introduces additional poles and zeros that destabilize the system at off-band frequencies.

To address this, the LC interstage network is replaced with a direct connection between the two stages, removing the resonant LC network (L2 and C2) and replacing it with a simple inductor (L2) that provides a low-impedance path at the operating frequency. This eliminates the second resonance and reduces the number of feedback paths that destabilize the system. Additionally, the biasing network is simplified by removing the current mirror and replacing it with a direct gate biasing resistor (R2) from the second stage to VDD, reducing the number of feedback loops and improving the stability margin.
