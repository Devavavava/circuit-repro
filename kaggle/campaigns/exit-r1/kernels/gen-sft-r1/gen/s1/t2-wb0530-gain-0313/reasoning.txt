The failure is due to noise figure (NF) being too high, exceeding the 2.619 dB specification. This is likely caused by the noise contributions from the biasing network and the current-reuse structure. The NMOS NM3 and R1/R2 bias network introduce noise that is not adequately suppressed in the current topology, especially at the target frequency of ~1.22 GHz. The current-reuse configuration may also contribute to noise due to the additional transistor and resistive elements in the bias path.

To address this, I redesigned the biasing structure to reduce noise contributions. The original NM3 diode-connected NMOS with R1 and R2 is replaced with a simpler NMOS NM3 connected to VDD instead of VSS, which allows for a more stable and lower-noise biasing path. Additionally, the R4 resistor in the original PMOS PM2 biasing path is removed, and the PM2 gate is directly connected to the output node n4, simplifying the feedback and reducing noise coupling. The shared DC path is restructured to avoid unnecessary resistive elements that could contribute to noise. These changes reduce the noise figure by eliminating noise-generating components and simplifying the signal path.
