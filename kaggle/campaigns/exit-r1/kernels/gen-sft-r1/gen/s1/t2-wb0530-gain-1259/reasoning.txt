The failure is primarily due to the noise figure (NF) being too high, exceeding the maximum allowable value of 2.5187 dB by approximately 0.25 dB. This is likely caused by the noise contributions from the biasing network and the diode-connected NMOS (NM3) and PMOS (PM2) transistors, which introduce additional noise into the signal path. The current-reuse topology, while effective for gain and power efficiency, may not be optimal for minimizing noise in a wideband configuration, especially when the biasing network is not carefully integrated into the signal path.

To address this, I replaced the diode-connected NM3 with a current mirror configuration (NM3 and NM4), which reduces the noise contribution from the biasing network by isolating it from the signal path. Additionally, I restructured the current-setting network by connecting the current mirror's output to a new internal node (n6), which allows for better control over the bias current and reduces noise coupling. The PMOS current-setting transistor (PM2) was moved to a new node (n7), and the load inductor (L2) was reconnected to ground (VSS) to ensure a stable and low-noise output path. These changes help to reduce the noise figure while maintaining the required gain and stability.
