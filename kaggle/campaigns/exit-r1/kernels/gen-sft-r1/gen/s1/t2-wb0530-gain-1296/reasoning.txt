The failure is due to the inadequate stability margin (mu_min_wide_0.01-50GHz) caused by the feedback path in the original topology. The current-reuse structure introduces a feedback loop through the diode-connected NM3 and the PM2 current source, which destabilizes the amplifier over a wide frequency range, especially at lower frequencies. This feedback reduces the phase margin, causing the stability factor (mu) to dip below the required minimum.

To address this, I modified the feedback path by replacing the diode-connected NM3 with a resistive load (Rrep1) connected to the gate of NM3. This change breaks the direct feedback path that was destabilizing the amplifier. The resistive load provides a more predictable and stable impedance at lower frequencies, improving the phase margin and ensuring the stability factor meets the required minimum across the entire band. This structural change eliminates the problematic feedback loop while maintaining the current-reuse topology's benefits in noise and gain performance.
