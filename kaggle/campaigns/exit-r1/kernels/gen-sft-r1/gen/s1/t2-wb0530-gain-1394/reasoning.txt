The failure is primarily due to the poor stability margin (mu_min_wide_0.01-50GHz), which is below the required minimum. This indicates that the circuit is unstable over a wide frequency range, likely due to the feedback path between the output and input through the inductive load and the current-reuse structure. The existing topology introduces a feedback loop that causes instability at lower frequencies, especially when the inductive load (L2) and capacitive load (C2) interact with the output node (n4). This interaction, combined with the current-reuse configuration, creates a phase shift that reduces the stability margin.

To address this, I restructured the feedback path by moving the inductive load (L2) to the output node (n2) and connecting it to the output node (VOUT1) directly, which helps in decoupling the feedback path from the input and reduces phase shift. Additionally, I replaced the PMOS current mirror (PM2) with an NMOS current mirror (NM2) to improve the stability by altering the feedback characteristics and reducing the phase shift at lower frequencies. This change helps in maintaining a higher stability margin across the wideband frequency range.
