The binding constraint is the minimum stability factor (mu_min_wide_0.01-50GHz), which is failing due to insufficient phase margin in the feedback loop across the wideband frequency range. The original topology uses a resistive shunt feedback with a shunt-peaked inductive load, which may introduce phase shifts that reduce stability at higher frequencies. This is exacerbated by the single-stage common-source (CS) configuration, which inherently has limited phase margin when combined with feedback and inductive peaking.

To address this, I propose a structural change to the topology by introducing a second stage with a common-gate (CG) configuration and an additional inductor in the feedback path. This adds a second pole-zero pair in the feedback loop, which helps to improve phase margin and stabilize the amplifier over the wideband range. The addition of a second NMOS transistor (NM2) in a CG configuration provides a low-output impedance and helps to isolate the feedback path from the load, reducing phase shifts. The new inductor (L2) in the feedback path helps to further control the phase response, while the additional capacitors (C3, C4) provide necessary coupling and decoupling at different frequency ranges.
