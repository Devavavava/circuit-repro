The binding constraint is the minimum stability factor (mu_min) over a wideband frequency range (0.01–50 GHz), which is failing due to insufficient phase margin and poor feedback suppression in the current-reuse topology. The original circuit uses a shared DC path and a tuned load (L2+C2), which may introduce resonances and phase shifts that reduce stability across the wideband. Additionally, the current-reuse structure may not adequately suppress feedback paths at higher frequencies, leading to a lower mu_min.

To address this, I modified the topology by introducing a separate DC path for the NMOS and PMOS transistors, using a new internal node (n6) to isolate the current-reuse feedback network from the main signal path. This change reduces the coupling between the input and output at higher frequencies, improving phase margin and stability. I also restructured the load network by moving the tuned inductor (L2) to a different node (n2) and connecting it to the new internal node (n6), which helps in shaping the frequency response and suppressing unwanted feedback paths. These structural changes enhance the stability factor (mu_min) over the wideband without compromising the noise figure or gain.
