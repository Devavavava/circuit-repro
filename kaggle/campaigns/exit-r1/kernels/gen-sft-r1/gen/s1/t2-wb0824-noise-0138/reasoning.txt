The failure is primarily due to the noise figure (NF) being too high, exceeding the maximum allowed value of 2.462 dB. The anchor netlist uses a current-reuse topology with a diode-connected NMOS (NM3) and PMOS (PM2) for biasing, but this configuration introduces parasitic noise contributions and insufficient noise isolation from the input. The noise is exacerbated by the lack of a proper noise-canceling structure and the absence of a dedicated noise-grounding path. Additionally, the shared DC path and the use of a single inductor (L2) for the load may not provide sufficient impedance matching and noise suppression across the wideband frequency range.

To address this, I modified the topology by introducing a second NMOS (NM3) connected to a new internal node (n5), which is tied to the gate of the original NMOS (NM1) through a resistor (R2) instead of directly to the DC path. This change decouples the noise contribution from the bias network and improves noise isolation. Additionally, I added a new capacitor (C2) between the new internal node (n5) and the input node (VIN1), which helps in filtering out high-frequency noise and improving the noise figure. The new NMOS (NM3) is connected to a new internal node (n6), which is used to bias the PMOS (PM2) through a resistor (R4), ensuring a more stable and noise-optimized biasing network.
