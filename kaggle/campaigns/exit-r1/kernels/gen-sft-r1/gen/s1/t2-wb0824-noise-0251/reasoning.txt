The noise figure (NF) is the binding constraint, failing by approximately 0.12 dB. This suggests that the noise contribution from the input transistor (NM1) or the biasing network is too high for the target specification. The current-reuse topology, while effective for gain and power efficiency, may be introducing noise due to the biasing network (R1, NM3, R2, etc.) or the load configuration (L2, C2). The noise from the biasing network is likely being amplified or not sufficiently suppressed in the current configuration, leading to a higher-than-allowed NF.

To address this, I propose a structural change to the biasing network and load configuration. The NM3 diode-connected NMOS is replaced with a PMOS diode (ZP1) to provide a more stable and lower-noise current reference. This change reduces the noise contribution from the biasing network by using a PMOS, which typically has lower noise in certain configurations. Additionally, the load network is modified: the inductor L2 is replaced with a capacitor C2 in series with a new inductor L2, and the output capacitor C3 is connected to a new internal node n6 instead of VOUT1. This change improves the impedance matching and noise performance by decoupling the output node from the load network, reducing noise coupling and improving stability.
