The failure is primarily due to the noise figure (NF) exceeding the specification. The noise performance is dominated by the input transistor NM1 and the biasing network, particularly the diode-connected NM3 and the resistive biasing elements. The noise contribution from NM1 is exacerbated by the high impedance at its source node, which is influenced by the resistive biasing network (R2, R1, R5, etc.). This configuration increases the noise contribution from the biasing network and reduces the overall noise immunity of the LNA.

To address this, the topology must be modified to reduce the noise contribution from the biasing network and improve the signal-to-noise ratio at the input. The proposed solution introduces a resistive divider (Rrep1) between the gate of NM1 and the output node (n4), which helps in reducing the noise contribution from the biasing network by providing a low-impedance path for noise currents. Additionally, a new NMOS transistor (NM2) is added between n5 and n4, creating a feedback path that helps in stabilizing the biasing and reducing the noise figure. This change also helps in improving the linearity and stability of the LNA by providing a more controlled biasing environment for NM1.
