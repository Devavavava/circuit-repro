The noise figure (NF) is the binding constraint, and it is failing due to the noise contribution from the biasing network and the current-reuse structure. The original netlist uses a diode-connected NMOS (NM3) and a current mirror with resistors (R1, R2) to bias the LNA. This configuration introduces additional noise from the biasing network and the current mirror, especially at high frequencies. The current-reuse topology also adds noise due to the feedback and the shared DC path, which degrades the noise performance. Additionally, the input is DC-blocked with a capacitor (C4), but the output is directly connected to the tuned load (L2+C2), which may not be optimal for noise suppression.

To address this, I propose a structural change to the biasing network and the current-reuse topology. Instead of using a diode-connected NMOS and resistive current mirroring, I replace NM3 with a PMOS transistor (PM2) and reconfigure the biasing network to use a resistive divider (R1, R2) connected to the input node (n0) and the internal node (x1) of the biasing network. This change reduces the noise contribution from the biasing network by using a PMOS transistor, which has lower noise at high frequencies. Additionally, the current-reuse structure is modified by connecting the output of the NMOS transistor (NM1) to the input of the PMOS transistor (PM2), and the output of the PMOS transistor is connected to the tuned load (L2+C2). This change improves the noise performance by reducing the noise contribution from the current-reuse structure and the shared DC path.
