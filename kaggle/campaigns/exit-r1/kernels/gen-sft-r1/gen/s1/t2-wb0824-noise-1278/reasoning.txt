The failure is due to the insufficient stability margin (mu_min_wide_0.01-50GHz) over the wideband frequency range. The original topology uses a resistive shunt feedback CS LNA with a shunt-peaked inductive load, which, while effective for broadband gain and input matching, does not inherently ensure sufficient stability across the entire band. The feedback resistor (R1) and the inductive load (L1) contribute to the gain and impedance transformation but may not provide enough phase margin or feedback suppression at higher frequencies, leading to a marginal stability condition.

To address this, I replaced the resistive shunt feedback with a current-reuse topology that includes a second NMOS transistor (NM2) and a PMOS transistor (PM1) to provide additional feedback and improve stability. This configuration enhances the phase margin by introducing a more controlled feedback path and reduces the risk of oscillation. The additional inductor (L2) and capacitors (C1, C2, C3) help shape the frequency response and ensure better impedance matching across the wideband range. The inclusion of a second NMOS transistor (NM3) and a PMOS transistor (PM2) in the biasing network ensures stable operation without the need for explicit biasing components, as the topology inherently self-biases.
