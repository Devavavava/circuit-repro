The failure is primarily due to the noise figure (nf_max_db) being too high, exceeding the specification by about 0.135 dB. This is likely caused by the noise contribution from the input transistor NM1 being too high, possibly due to the lack of a proper noise-shaping mechanism or insufficient impedance matching at the input. The current-reuse topology, while effective in boosting gain, may not be optimally configured for noise suppression in this wideband application. The input node n0 is directly connected to the gate of NM1 through a capacitor C4, which may not provide sufficient noise isolation or impedance control. Additionally, the shared DC path and the structure of the load network may contribute to noise degradation.

To address this, I propose a structural change that introduces a noise-shaping inductor (L3) and a capacitor (C3) to the output node, forming a resonant network that helps in shaping the noise response and improving the noise figure. This change modifies the output impedance and provides a more controlled noise path. Additionally, a new PMOS transistor (PM2) is connected to a new internal node (n7) and the output node (n2), which helps in balancing the current flow and reducing noise contributions from the input transistor. These changes improve the noise performance by isolating the input from noise sources and shaping the frequency response to meet the noise specification.
