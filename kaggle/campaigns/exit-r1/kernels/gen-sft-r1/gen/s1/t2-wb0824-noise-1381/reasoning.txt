The failure is due to the insufficient stability margin (mu_min_wide_0.01-50GHz) across the wideband frequency range, which is primarily caused by the feedback loop's inability to maintain sufficient phase margin at higher frequencies. The resistive shunt feedback in the anchor netlist introduces a phase lag that, combined with the inductive peaking, leads to a reduction in stability at the upper edge of the band. This is exacerbated by the single-stage topology, which lacks sufficient phase compensation mechanisms to maintain stability over the entire 0.01–50 GHz range.

To address this, I propose adding a second-stage amplifier with a cascode configuration and an additional inductor-capacitor (LC) peaking network to improve phase margin and stability. The second stage provides additional gain and helps isolate the input from the output, reducing feedback-induced instability. The addition of a PMOS cascode device and an LC peaking network at the output introduces a second resonant frequency that helps compensate for the phase lag caused by the inductive peaking and resistive feedback. This dual-stage topology with active peaking enhances stability over the wideband range.
