The failure is due to the noise figure (NF) being too high, exceeding the specification by approximately 0.17 dB. This is likely caused by the noise contribution from the input transistor NM1 being too large, possibly due to the lack of a proper noise-shaping mechanism or insufficient impedance transformation at the input. The current-reuse topology, while effective for gain and power efficiency, may not be optimally configured for noise performance in this wideband application.

To address this, I added a shunt capacitor (ZC1) between the output of the NMOS transistor (n2) and an internal node (x1), which helps in reducing the noise contribution by providing a low-impedance path for high-frequency noise. Additionally, I introduced a new NMOS transistor (ZN1) connected between the internal node (x1) and the output (VOUT1), which acts as a noise-canceling element by providing a feedback path for noise at the output. This change helps in shaping the noise characteristics of the LNA and lowers the overall noise figure without significantly affecting the gain or stability.
