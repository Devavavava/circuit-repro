The failure is due to the poor stability margin (mu_min_wide_0.01-50GHz) across the wideband frequency range. The original topology lacks sufficient feedback and impedance control to maintain stability at all frequencies, especially in the lower end of the band. The current-reuse configuration, while effective for gain and noise, does not inherently provide the necessary phase and magnitude stability across the entire 0.01–50 GHz range. The absence of a dedicated feedback path or impedance-matching component in the signal path exacerbates this issue.

To address this, I introduced a new capacitor (ZC1) between the internal node n5 (bias reference) and the signal node n3, which is part of the PMOS amplifier's output path. This addition provides a low-frequency feedback path that helps stabilize the amplifier at lower frequencies without significantly affecting the high-frequency performance. The capacitor acts as a frequency-dependent impedance that enhances phase margin at the lower end of the band, thus improving the stability metric (mu_min_wide_0.01-50GHz). This change does not interfere with the existing biasing or signal paths and is compatible with the current-reuse topology.
