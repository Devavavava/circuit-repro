The binding constraint is the minimum stability factor (mu_min_wide_0.01-50GHz), which is negative, indicating instability over part of the frequency range. This is likely caused by the feedback loop in the original circuit being too weak or poorly controlled at higher frequencies, leading to potential oscillation or poor phase margin. The resistive shunt feedback (R1) and the inductive peaking (L1) may not be sufficient to maintain stability across the entire 0.01–50 GHz range.

To address this, I introduced a second NMOS transistor (NM3) in a common-gate configuration, connected between VDD and the output node (n2), with its source tied to VSS and its gate connected to the drain of the main transistor (NM1). This addition provides additional feedback and phase compensation, improving the stability factor across the wideband range. The common-gate transistor (NM3) introduces a local feedback path that helps dampen oscillations and improve the phase margin, especially at higher frequencies.
