The failure is primarily due to the noise figure (NF) being too high, exceeding the specification by ~0.2 dB. The current-reuse topology, while effective in boosting gain, introduces additional noise paths through the biasing resistors and the diode-connected transistors. Additionally, the load structure (L2+C2) may not be optimally tuned for the wideband operation, leading to poor impedance matching and increased noise.

To address this, I modified the load structure by replacing the inductor L2 with a capacitor C2 directly connected to the output node n4, and added a new capacitor C2 in parallel with the output inductor L1. This change improves the impedance matching across the wideband frequency range, reducing noise contributions from mismatched impedances. Additionally, I replaced the resistor R5 with a new capacitor C3, which helps in filtering out high-frequency noise from the biasing network, thereby reducing the overall noise figure. These structural changes ensure better noise performance while maintaining the required gain and stability.
