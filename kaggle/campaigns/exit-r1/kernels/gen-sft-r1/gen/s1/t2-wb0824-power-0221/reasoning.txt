The failure is due to the insufficient stability margin (mu_min_wide_0.01-50GHz) in the original circuit. The resistive shunt feedback and the shunt-peaked inductive load, while beneficial for bandwidth and noise, do not provide sufficient phase margin across the wideband frequency range, leading to instability at certain frequencies. This is exacerbated by the lack of a dedicated stability-enhancing element in the feedback path.

To address this, I added a PMOS transistor (PM1) in a common-gate configuration, providing a low-impedance path to ground at the drain of the main NMOS transistor (NM1). This configuration introduces a phase lead at higher frequencies, improving the stability margin. Additionally, I introduced a new inductor (L2) and a capacitor (C2) to form a series peaking network at the output, which helps in shaping the frequency response and further enhancing stability. The addition of a resistor (R3) and a capacitor (C3) in the feedback path provides additional control over the phase and gain, ensuring a stable operation across the entire frequency range.
