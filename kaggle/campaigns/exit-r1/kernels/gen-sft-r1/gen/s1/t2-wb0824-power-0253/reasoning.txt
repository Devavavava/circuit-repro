The failure is due to the insufficient stability margin (mu_min_wide_0.01-50GHz) caused by the single-stage resistive shunt-feedback topology. The feedback resistor R1, while providing input matching, does not adequately suppress oscillations across the wideband frequency range, especially at higher frequencies. The shunt-peaked inductor L1 and resistor R2 load also contribute to a less stable phase response, leading to a negative stability margin.

To address this, I replace the single-stage resistive shunt-feedback topology with a two-stage inductive feedback configuration. The first stage uses an NMOS transistor NM1 with a series inductor L1 and a self-bias network (R1 and NM3), while the second stage employs a PMOS transistor PM1 with an inductive feedback path (L2 and R2). This topology enhances phase margin across the wideband by introducing additional phase shift and damping through the inductive feedback and the second stage's transconductance. The addition of the second stage and the inductive feedback path improves stability and reduces the risk of oscillation, directly addressing the insufficient stability margin.
