The failure is due to noise figure (NF) being too high, driven by the noise contribution from the biasing network and the current-reuse structure. The anchor netlist uses a diode-connected NMOS (NM3) and a current mirror (PM2, R4, R5) to bias the LNA, but this introduces additional noise into the signal path, especially at the input node. The noise from NM3 and the current mirror is not sufficiently suppressed, leading to an NF that exceeds the specification by approximately 0.18 dB.

To address this, the biasing network is restructured to reduce noise contributions. The NM3 diode is replaced with a NMOS transistor (NM2) connected to the output node (n4) instead of VSS, effectively isolating the biasing network from the signal path. This change ensures that the noise from the biasing network is not directly coupled into the input signal, reducing the overall noise figure. Additionally, the R2 resistor is connected to n4 instead of n1, further decoupling the biasing network from the signal path and reducing noise coupling.
