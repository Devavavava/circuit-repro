The failure is due to insufficient stability margin (`mu_min_wide_0.01-50GHz`) across the wideband frequency range, despite meeting other specifications like noise figure, gain, and power consumption. The original topology uses a resistive shunt-feedback CS LNA with a shunt-peaked inductive load. While this structure offers good input matching and broadband performance, it lacks sufficient feedback and phase compensation mechanisms to ensure stability over the entire 0.01–50 GHz range. The feedback resistor `R1` provides DC biasing but does not actively stabilize the amplifier at higher frequencies, leading to a marginal stability margin that fails the constraint.

To address this, I replaced the resistive shunt feedback with a more robust feedback network that includes a second NMOS transistor (`NM2`) and a series inductor (`L2`) to provide active feedback and phase compensation. This change introduces a second feedback path from the output to the source of the first transistor, enhancing the phase margin and ensuring stability over the wideband range. The addition of the inductor `L2` in the feedback path helps to tune the phase response, preventing instability at higher frequencies. The second NMOS transistor allows for more precise control of the feedback signal, improving both stability and linearity. This structural change directly addresses the insufficient stability margin and ensures the amplifier meets the `mu_min` constraint across the entire frequency range.
