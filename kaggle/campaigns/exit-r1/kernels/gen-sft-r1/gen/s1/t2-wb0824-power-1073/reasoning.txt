The failure is driven by the noise figure (nf_max_db) exceeding the specification, which is primarily due to the noise contribution from the input transistor NM1 being too high. This is likely caused by the lack of a proper noise-shaping mechanism in the current topology. The anchor netlist uses a simple current-reuse push-pull configuration, but it lacks a noise-canceling component or a more effective noise suppression structure at the input stage. The absence of a noise-canceling component leads to a higher noise floor, especially in a wideband configuration where noise contributions from all frequency components are integrated.

To address this, I introduced a noise-canceling capacitor (ZC1) between the drain of NM3 and the bias node (n5). This capacitor provides a low-impedance path for high-frequency noise at the bias node, effectively reducing the noise contribution from the input transistor by decoupling the bias network from the high-frequency signal path. This change improves the noise figure without significantly affecting the gain or stability of the amplifier.
