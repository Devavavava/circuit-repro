The failure is due to insufficient stability margin (`mu_min_wide_0.01-50GHz`) in the original resistive shunt-feedback LNA topology. The feedback resistor (R1) and shunt-peaked inductor (L1) create a narrow stability band, leading to instability at lower frequencies. This is exacerbated by the single-stage CS amplifier, which cannot provide sufficient phase margin across the wideband frequency range.

To address this, I introduce a second-stage common-gate (CG) amplifier with an inductive source degeneration (L3) to enhance phase margin and improve stability. This topology adds a second amplification stage that provides additional phase shift and improves the stability of the system across the wideband range. The CG stage also helps in reducing the feedback loop's phase lag, thereby increasing the stability margin. Additionally, the inductive source degeneration (L3) in the CG stage helps in peaking the output impedance, further enhancing the stability and bandwidth of the LNA.
