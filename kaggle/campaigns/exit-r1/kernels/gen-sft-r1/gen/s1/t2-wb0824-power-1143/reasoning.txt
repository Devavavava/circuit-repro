The failure is primarily due to the noise figure (NF) being too high, exceeding the specified maximum of 2.1089 dB. This is likely caused by the noise contributions from the biasing network and the current-reuse structure, particularly the NMOS diode (NM3) and the resistive biasing elements (R1, R2, R4, R5). These components introduce unnecessary noise into the signal path, especially at the input stage, and the current-reuse configuration may be amplifying this noise due to the feedback and gain mechanisms involved.

To address this, I propose a structural change that replaces the current-reuse configuration with a simpler cascode topology. This reduces the number of noise-generating components in the signal path and improves the noise performance. The NMOS and PMOS transistors are reconnected in a cascode configuration (NM1 and PM1), with a direct connection from the input to the gate of NM1 and a tuned load (L2 and C2) at the output. This simplifies the signal path, reduces noise contributions from the bias network, and improves the noise figure by minimizing the impact of the current-reuse structure.
