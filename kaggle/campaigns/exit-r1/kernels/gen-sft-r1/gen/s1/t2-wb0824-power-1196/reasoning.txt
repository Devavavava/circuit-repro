The failure is primarily due to the noise figure (NF) being too high, exceeding the maximum allowed value of 1.9622 dB. The noise figure is dominated by the noise contributions from the input transistor (NM1) and the biasing network, particularly the NMOS diode (NM3) and the associated resistors (R1, R2). The current-reuse structure, while beneficial for gain and power efficiency, introduces additional noise paths and parasitic effects that degrade the noise performance, especially at the high frequency of 1.385 GHz. Additionally, the shared DC path and the use of a tuned load (L2+C2) may contribute to poor impedance matching and increased noise at the input.

To address this, I propose a structural change that eliminates the NMOS diode (NM3) and its associated resistor (R2), and instead connects the gate of NM1 directly to the input through a capacitor (C1), while using a different biasing approach with a PMOS diode (PM2) and a resistor (R4) to set the current. This simplifies the biasing network and reduces the noise contributions from the diode and resistors. Additionally, the load is modified to use a capacitor (C2) directly from the output to ground, which improves the impedance match and reduces noise at the input. The removal of the shared DC path and the use of a simpler biasing network reduce the overall noise contribution and improve the noise figure.
