The failure is due to the noise figure (NF) being too high, exceeding the maximum allowed value of 2.0872 dB. The noise contribution from the input transistor NM1 and the biasing network is likely the main culprit. The current-reuse topology, while effective for gain and power efficiency, introduces additional noise paths through the biasing network (NM3, R1, R2, R4, R5, PM2), which are not properly suppressed in the wideband operation at f0 = 1.385 GHz. This leads to an elevated noise floor, particularly at the higher end of the band, causing the NF to exceed the specification.

To address this, I propose a structural change that eliminates the biasing network from the signal path and replaces the current-reuse configuration with a simpler cascode topology. This reduces the noise contribution from the biasing network and improves the noise matching at the input. The new topology uses a single NMOS transistor (NM1) in a common-source configuration with a resonant inductor (L2) and capacitor (C2) as the load. The second transistor (NM2) is used in a cascode configuration to improve the output impedance and bandwidth. This change simplifies the signal path, reduces noise contributions, and improves the noise figure while maintaining the required gain and stability.
