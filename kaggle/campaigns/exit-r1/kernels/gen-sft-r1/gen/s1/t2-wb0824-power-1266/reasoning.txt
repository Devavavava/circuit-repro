The failure is primarily due to the noise figure (NF) being too high, exceeding the maximum allowed value of 2.3169 dB. The anchor netlist uses a current-reuse topology with a diode-connected NMOS (NM3) and PMOS (PM2) for biasing, but this configuration introduces significant noise contributions from the biasing network and the current mirror. The noise from the current mirror and the biasing resistors (R1, R2, R4, R5) adds to the overall noise figure, especially at the high-frequency operating point (f0 = 1.385 GHz). The noise from the biasing network is not well suppressed, and the current mirror introduces additional noise due to the mismatch and non-ideal behavior of the transistors. This results in a higher-than-allowed noise figure, causing the binding constraint to fail.

To address this, I propose a structural change that eliminates the current mirror and diode-connected transistors used for biasing and instead uses a simpler and more noise-efficient configuration. The new topology replaces the NM3 and PM2 biasing network with a direct connection from the gate of NM1 to VDD, and the PM1 gate is connected to VDD via a resistor (Rrep1). This eliminates the current mirror and reduces the noise contributions from the biasing network. The resistor Rrep1 is added to provide a controlled gate bias to PM1, ensuring stable operation without introducing additional noise. The load inductor L2 is connected to the output node n2, and the output capacitor C2 is connected to VSS, forming a resonant load that improves the gain and bandwidth while reducing noise.
