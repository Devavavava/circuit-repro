The failure is due to the poor stability margin (mu_min_wide_0.01-50GHz) across the wideband frequency range. The original topology uses a resistive shunt feedback and a shunt-peaked inductive load, which may lead to insufficient phase margin at higher frequencies, especially when the feedback loop interacts with the inductive peaking. This causes the stability factor (mu) to dip below the required minimum, particularly at the edges of the band, leading to instability or oscillation.

To address this, I propose a structural change by introducing a second NMOS transistor (NM2) in a cascode configuration, connected to the inductor (L1) and the output node (VOUT1). This addition provides additional phase compensation and improves the stability margin by isolating the feedback loop from the inductive peaking at higher frequencies. The new NMOS transistor also helps in managing the output impedance and ensures better control over the feedback path, thus enhancing the stability across the entire band.
