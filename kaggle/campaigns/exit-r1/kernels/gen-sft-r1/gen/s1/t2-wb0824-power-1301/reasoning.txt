The failure is due to the insufficient stability margin (mu_min_wide_0.01-50GHz) over the wideband range, which is likely caused by the feedback configuration and the inductive peaking in the original design. The resistive shunt feedback and the shunt-peaked inductor load may not provide sufficient phase margin across the entire band, leading to instability at certain frequencies.

To address this, I restructured the feedback and load network to enhance stability. I replaced the resistive shunt feedback with a current source feedback configuration using a PMOS transistor (PM1) and a current mirror (PM2, NM3, NM4), which provides a more predictable and stable feedback path. Additionally, I modified the load network to use a series inductor (L2) and a capacitor (C2) in parallel with the output, which helps in shaping the frequency response and improving the phase margin. The addition of a resistor (R1) and a capacitor (C3) in the biasing network ensures a stable DC operating point without introducing unnecessary noise or instability. These changes collectively improve the stability margin across the wideband range.
