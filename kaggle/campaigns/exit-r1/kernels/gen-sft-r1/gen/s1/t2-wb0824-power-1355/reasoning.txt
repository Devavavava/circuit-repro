The failure is primarily due to the noise figure (NF) being too high, exceeding the maximum allowed value of 1.8991 dB. This is likely caused by the noise contributions from the biasing network and the current-reuse structure in the original topology. The NMOS diode (NM3) and the resistor (R1) in the bias network introduce additional noise, especially at high frequencies, which degrades the noise performance of the LNA. Additionally, the current-reuse configuration may not be optimally suppressing the noise from the output stage.

To address this, the biasing network is restructured to reduce noise contributions. The NM3 diode is replaced with a direct connection to the internal node n2, and the resistor R2 is connected to n2 instead of n1. This reduces the noise path from the bias network to the signal path. The PMOS gate biasing is also adjusted by connecting the PM2 diode to n4 (the tuned load node) instead of VDD, ensuring a more stable and noise-friendly biasing path. These changes simplify the biasing structure and reduce noise coupling from the bias network to the signal path, directly improving the noise figure.
