The failure is primarily due to the noise figure (nf_max_db) being too high, exceeding the specification by approximately 0.156 dB. This is likely caused by the noise contributions from the biasing network and the current-reuse configuration, which introduce additional noise sources into the signal path. The NMOS gate biasing via the diode NM3 and the PMOS gate biasing via the diode PM2 contribute noise, especially when the bias currents are not optimally set. Additionally, the current-reuse structure may not be effectively isolating the noise from the biasing network, leading to a higher overall noise figure.

To address this, I modified the biasing network for the NMOS and PMOS transistors. In the anchor netlist, the NMOS gate was biased via a diode-connected NM3, and the PMOS gate was biased via a diode-connected PM2. These configurations introduce noise due to the diode's thermal noise and the current mirror's noise. Instead, I replaced these with direct resistive biasing from internal nodes to the gate, which reduces the noise contribution from the biasing network. The NMOS gate is now biased via a direct resistor (Rrep1) from an internal node (n7) to the gate, and the PMOS gate is biased via a direct resistor (Rrep2) from the same internal node (n7) to the gate. This change simplifies the biasing network and reduces the noise contribution from the biasing path, thereby lowering the overall noise figure.
