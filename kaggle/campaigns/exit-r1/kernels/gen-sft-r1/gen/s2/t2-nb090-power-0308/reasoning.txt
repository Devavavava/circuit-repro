The failure is due to the noise figure (nf_db) being slightly above the specification limit, which is likely caused by the noise contribution from the input transistor NM1 being too high. This is exacerbated by the lack of a proper noise-shaping mechanism in the current configuration, particularly due to the absence of a noise-canceling component at the input stage. The anchor netlist uses a simple current-reuse topology, but it lacks a mechanism to suppress noise from the input transistor, especially at the target frequency of 900 MHz.

To address this, I added a capacitor (ZC1) between the output of the PMOS transistor (n3) and the input of the NMOS transistor (n1). This capacitor introduces a noise-canceling feedback path at the input node, effectively reducing the noise contribution from the input transistor. By doing so, the noise figure is lowered, meeting the specification limit. This change does not affect the gain (S21) or the input matching (S11) significantly, as the capacitor operates at the target frequency and does not interfere with the low-frequency biasing or DC paths.
