The failure is primarily due to the noise figure (nf_db) being too high, which is likely caused by the noise contributions from the second stage and the biasing network. The current structure uses a current mirror (NM3) biased through resistors (R2 and R3) to both NM1 and NM2 gates, which introduces additional noise and degrades the noise performance. Additionally, the lack of proper impedance matching and noise suppression in the interstage LC network may be contributing to the poor noise figure.

To address this, I restructured the circuit to eliminate the current mirror and the resistive biasing network, which are known sources of noise. Instead, the second stage (NM2) is directly biased through a resistor (R2) connected to its source, simplifying the biasing and reducing noise contributions. The interstage LC network is reconfigured to provide better impedance matching and noise suppression. The drain load of the first stage (L1) is connected to the source of NM2, creating a more direct signal path and reducing noise coupling. Additionally, the output capacitor (C2) is connected to the drain of NM2, ensuring a clean output signal. These changes improve the noise figure and overall performance of the LNA.
