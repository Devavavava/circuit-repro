The failure is due to poor input impedance matching, as evidenced by the s11_max_db constraint failing by ~0.58 dB. The original topology uses a simple DC-blocked capacitor (C1) at the input, which does not provide sufficient impedance transformation or tuning to achieve the required -7.99 dB minimum return loss at 900 MHz. The LC interstage network (L2 and C2) and the second-stage tuned load (L3) are not directly involved in input impedance shaping, leading to a mismatch.

To address this, the input network is restructured to include a series inductor (L1) and a shunt capacitor (C1), forming a resonant circuit that enhances impedance matching at f0. The first-stage transistor (NM1) is reconnected to a new internal node (n3) to allow for better control over the input impedance. A new inductor (L2) is added in series with the second-stage transistor (NM2), and a new capacitor (C2) is used to provide a low-impedance path to ground at f0. The bias network is simplified by removing the current mirror and resistive biasing, relying instead on a direct connection from the bias reference (NM3) to the gate of the first-stage transistor (NM2), which reduces parasitic effects and improves stability.
