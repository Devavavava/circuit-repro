The binding constraint is the stability factor `mu_min_wide_0.01-50GHz`, which is slightly negative, indicating instability over a wide frequency range. This is likely due to the current-reuse topology and the shared DC path in the anchor netlist, which introduces feedback paths that reduce stability margins. The NMOS and PMOS transistors in the push-pull configuration, along with the tuned load and DC-blocking capacitors, may be contributing to parasitic feedback and phase shifts that destabilize the amplifier at higher frequencies.

To address this, I propose a structural change to the topology by replacing the push-pull configuration with a cascode structure. This reduces the Miller effect and minimizes feedback paths, improving stability. Additionally, the tuned load is moved to a separate DC path, and the biasing network is restructured to avoid shared nodes that could introduce unwanted coupling. The NMOS and PMOS transistors are reconnected in a way that isolates the input and output nodes more effectively, reducing the risk of instability.
