The failure is due to the poor stability margin (mu_min_wide_0.01-50GHz), which is critical for ensuring the LNA remains unconditionally stable across a wide frequency range. The current topology, with its two-stage CS structure and LC interstage, introduces a feedback path through the interstage LC network, which causes a reduction in the stability factor, especially at frequencies outside the narrowband center frequency (f0). This is exacerbated by the shared biasing network, which may not provide sufficient isolation between the stages.

To address this, I propose a structural change that eliminates the interstage LC network and reconfigures the second stage to be a common-gate (CG) configuration instead of common-source (CS). This change reduces the feedback path between stages and improves the stability margin. Additionally, the first stage is reconfigured to use a source inductor for improved input matching and noise performance. The biasing network is restructured to use a separate current mirror for each stage, improving isolation and stability.
