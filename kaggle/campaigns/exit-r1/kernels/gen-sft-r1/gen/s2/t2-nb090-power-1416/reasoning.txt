The binding constraint is the input return loss (s11_max_db), which is too high (only -2.2858 dB, failing the -7.8472 dB target). This indicates poor input impedance matching at the center frequency, likely due to the input capacitor C1 and the first-stage transistor NM1 not forming a sufficiently resonant or well-matched network at 900 MHz. The LC tank L1 at the drain of NM1 may be causing unwanted resonances or reflections that interfere with the input matching.

To address this, I replaced the original structure with a single-stage common-source LNA using a different topology. The new design uses a series inductor L1 between the input and the gate of NM1, along with a shunt capacitor C1 to ground. This configuration improves input impedance matching by allowing the inductor to resonate with the input capacitance at the desired frequency, thereby enhancing the return loss. The second stage is replaced with a simple NM2 transistor whose source is connected to the drain of NM1, and its gate is biased through a current mirror. This reduces the number of inductors and simplifies the structure, improving stability and reducing parasitic effects that may have been degrading the input matching in the original two-stage design.
