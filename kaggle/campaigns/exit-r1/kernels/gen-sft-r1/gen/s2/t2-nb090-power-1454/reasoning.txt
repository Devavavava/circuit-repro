The failure is due to the low stability margin (`mu_min_wide_0.01-50GHz`) caused by poor feedback and phase compensation in the current-reuse LNA topology. The original circuit uses a shared DC path with a diode-connected NMOS (NM3) and PMOS (PM2) for biasing, which introduces parasitic feedback and degrades stability across a wide frequency range. This is exacerbated by the lack of explicit phase compensation elements and the shared inductive load (L2+C2), which can resonate and destabilize the amplifier at higher frequencies.

To address this, I redesigned the biasing network to isolate the DC path from the signal path, reducing unwanted feedback. I replaced the shared diode-connected NM3 with a separate NMOS (NM2) connected to a local inductor (L3), ensuring a stable bias without interfering with the signal flow. Additionally, I introduced a new inductor (L3) and capacitor (C2) to provide explicit phase compensation, improving stability across the wideband range. The PMOS biasing was also restructured to use a direct resistor (R4) to VSS, simplifying the feedback path and reducing parasitic interactions.
