The failure is due to the poor stability margin (mu_min_wide_0.01-50GHz) caused by the current-reuse topology and the shared DC path, which introduces unwanted feedback and phase shifts across the frequency band. The shared DC path between the NMOS and PMOS stages, along with the resonant load (L2+C2), creates a narrow stability band and insufficient phase margin at higher frequencies. This is exacerbated by the current mirror (NM3 and R1) and the biasing network (PM2, R4, R5), which add parasitic effects and complicate the feedback loop.

To address this, I restructured the circuit to eliminate the shared DC path and decouple the NMOS and PMOS stages. The new topology uses a fully differential configuration with separate DC paths for each stage, reducing feedback and phase shifts. A new NMOS (NM2) is added to form a cascode stage with NM1, improving the output impedance and stability. The inductor L3 is added to provide a low-impedance path for AC signals, further stabilizing the circuit. The capacitor C2 is moved to a position that decouples the output from the resonant load, and the resistor R2 is connected to the new internal node n6 to isolate the biasing network from the signal path.
