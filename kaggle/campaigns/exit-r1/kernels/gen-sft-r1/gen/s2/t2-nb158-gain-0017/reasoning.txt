The failure is due to the insufficient stability margin (mu_min_wide_0.01-50GHz) caused by the feedback topology and the inductive peaking network. The resistive shunt feedback and the inductive peaking load create a resonant structure that destabilizes the amplifier over a wide frequency range, especially at frequencies beyond the target band. This results in a negative stability margin, violating the mu_min constraint.

To address this, the topology is restructured to use a common-gate (CG) stage in parallel with the main common-source (CS) stage. This configuration inherently improves stability by reducing feedback at higher frequencies. The CG stage provides a low-impedance path to ground at high frequencies, which suppresses unwanted feedback loops that cause instability. Additionally, the inductive peaking is replaced with a capacitive peaking network, which avoids the resonant behavior that destabilizes the circuit. The addition of a second NMOS transistor in a CG configuration, along with a PMOS transistor, allows for better control of the output impedance and improves the stability margin over a wider frequency range.
