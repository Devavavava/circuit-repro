The failure is primarily due to insufficient stability margin (`mu_min_wide_0.01-50GHz`), which is critical for ensuring the amplifier remains unconditionally stable across the entire 0.01–50 GHz range. The original topology, while effective for narrowband gain and noise figure, lacks sufficient feedback and impedance control mechanisms to maintain stability over a wide frequency range. The shunt-peaked inductive load and resistive feedback provide good gain and input matching but are insufficient to suppress oscillatory behavior at higher frequencies.

To address this, I introduced a second-stage feedback loop using a PMOS transistor (`PM1`) and an additional inductor (`L2`) to provide a low-impedance path to ground at higher frequencies, thereby increasing the phase margin and improving stability. Additionally, a second capacitor (`C3`) was added to decouple the feedback node from the power supply, reducing noise coupling and improving the stability margin. The PMOS transistor in the second stage also helps in shaping the feedback path to ensure a more controlled gain-bandwidth product and better stability across the wide frequency range.
