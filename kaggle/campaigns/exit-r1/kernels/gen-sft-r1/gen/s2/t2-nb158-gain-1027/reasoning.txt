The failure is primarily due to insufficient gain in the second stage, as evidenced by the s21_db margin being -0.3948, indicating the achieved gain (17.7169 dB) is below the required 29.2744 dB. This is likely caused by the second-stage transistor (NM2) being under-driven or its load not providing sufficient impedance for amplification. The LC interstage network (L2 and C2) may be causing excessive signal loss or phase shift, reducing the overall gain. Additionally, the biasing network may not be providing sufficient gate drive to NM2, limiting its transconductance.

To address this, I propose a structural change that replaces the second-stage transistor (NM2) with a common-gate configuration (NM2 with its source connected to the drain of NM1) and adds a direct coupling inductor (L2) from the drain of NM1 to the gate of NM2. This configuration enhances the second-stage gain by reducing the Miller effect and improving the impedance transformation from the first to the second stage. The direct inductor coupling ensures minimal signal loss and better phase alignment at the operating frequency. This topology change is expected to increase the s21_db by approximately 10–12 dB, meeting the gain requirement.
