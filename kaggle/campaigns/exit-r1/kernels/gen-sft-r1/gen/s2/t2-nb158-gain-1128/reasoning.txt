The binding constraint is the stability factor `mu_min_wide_0.01-50GHz`, which is negative, indicating instability over a wide frequency range. The original topology uses a two-stage CS amplifier with LC resonators at each stage and a current mirror biasing network. This configuration leads to poor stability due to the feedback paths between the stages, especially when the interstage LC network resonates at frequencies near the operating point. The current mirror introduces additional feedback and parasitic effects that reduce the stability margin across the band.

To address this, I modified the topology by replacing the second stage with a common-gate (CG) configuration. The CG stage inherently provides better stability due to its lower output impedance and reduced feedback effects between stages. I also restructured the inductor connections to decouple the stages more effectively and reduce parasitic coupling. The addition of a new NMOS transistor (NM2) in the CG configuration and the reorganization of inductors (L2 and L3) help to isolate the stages and improve the stability margin. The biasing network was simplified by removing the current mirror and using a direct resistor (R2) to bias the second stage, which reduces the complexity and parasitic interactions.
