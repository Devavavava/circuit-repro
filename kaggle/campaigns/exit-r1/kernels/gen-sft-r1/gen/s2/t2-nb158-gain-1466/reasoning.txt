The binding constraint is the gain (s21_db), which is below the target by ~0.27 dB. The two-stage LNA with LC interstage tuning is inherently limited in gain due to the voltage division caused by the LC tank at the interstage node. The second stage's gate is driven through a series inductor (L2), which introduces a voltage drop and reduces the signal transfer efficiency from the first stage to the second. This is exacerbated by the fact that the second stage's drain is loaded with another inductor (L3), which does not provide a low-impedance path to ground at the operating frequency, further reducing the gain.

To address this, I propose a structural change to the topology by replacing the LC interstage configuration with a direct coupling between the two stages, using a capacitor (C2) to block DC while allowing AC signals to pass. This eliminates the voltage drop across the inductor (L2) and improves signal transfer from the first stage to the second. Additionally, the second stage's drain load is replaced with a resistor (R1) in parallel with an inductor (L3), which provides a more stable and lower impedance path to ground at the operating frequency, thereby improving gain and reducing signal loss.
