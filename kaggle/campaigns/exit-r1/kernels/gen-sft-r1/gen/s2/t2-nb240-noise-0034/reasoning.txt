The failure is primarily due to the insufficient gain (s21_db) in the current-reuse push-pull LNA topology. The anchor circuit uses a PMOS and NMOS cascode configuration with a shared DC path and a tuned load (L2+C2), but the gain is limited by the poor impedance transformation and the parasitic effects of the shared nodes, especially at the output. The output node (n2) is shared between the NMOS and PMOS stages, leading to a degradation in the output impedance and a reduction in the voltage gain. Additionally, the current-reuse structure may not be effectively amplifying the signal due to the lack of proper impedance matching and the presence of multiple internal nodes that introduce noise and signal loss.

To address this, the topology was restructured to use a two-stage NMOS amplifier with a separate inductor (L2) connected to the output of the first stage (n3), which helps in improving the gain and isolating the output from the input. The second stage (NM2) is connected to a separate inductor (L3) and the output node (VOUT1), providing a better impedance match and higher voltage gain. The biasing network is restructured to use a separate NMOS transistor (NM3) and resistor (R2) to provide a stable bias point for the first stage, while the second stage uses a different biasing structure to ensure proper operation. The removal of the PMOS stage and the tuned load from the shared DC path helps in reducing the noise and improving the gain. These changes lead to a significant improvement in the s21_db metric, which is the binding constraint in the original design.
