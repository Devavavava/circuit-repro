The failure is primarily due to insufficient stability and poor noise performance in the original topology, as evidenced by the negative margin for the `mu_min_wide_0.01-50GHz` constraint. The resistive shunt feedback and shunt-peaked inductor load, while effective for broadband matching and gain, are inadequate for maintaining stability over a wide frequency range. The noise figure is also higher than desired, likely due to the feedback resistor and lack of proper noise suppression in the output stage.

To address this, I propose a structural change that introduces a cascode configuration with a second NMOS transistor and a series inductor in the output path. This configuration improves stability by increasing the output impedance and reducing feedback at higher frequencies. The addition of a second NMOS transistor (NM2) in a cascode configuration with an inductor (L2) in the output path enhances the stability and reduces noise contributions from the output stage. The resistive feedback is replaced with a more controlled biasing network using an additional PMOS transistor (PM1) and resistors (R1, R2, R4, R5) to ensure proper DC biasing and reduce noise. This topology also includes a direct DC coupling from the input to the gate of the first transistor (NM1) to ensure a proper DC path without the need for an additional capacitor.
