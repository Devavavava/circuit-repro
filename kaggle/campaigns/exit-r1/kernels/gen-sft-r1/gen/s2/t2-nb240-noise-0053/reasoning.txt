The failure is primarily due to the low forward gain (s21_db) of the amplifier, which is insufficient to meet the specification. The current-reuse push-pull configuration, while effective for noise and power efficiency, introduces significant signal loss at the output stage due to the voltage divider effect between the NMOS and PMOS transistors. This is exacerbated by the tuned load (L2+C2), which may not be optimally resonant at f0, leading to a mismatch in impedance and reduced gain. Additionally, the shared DC path and the diode-connected transistors (NM3 and PM2) may contribute to signal degradation and instability in the gain path.

To address this, I propose replacing the push-pull configuration with a common-source topology for the output stage. This eliminates the voltage divider effect and allows for higher voltage swing and gain. The NMOS transistor (NM1) is connected directly to the tuned load (L2+C2), which is optimized for resonance at f0, ensuring maximum power transfer and gain. The PMOS transistor (PM1) is reconfigured to act as a current source, driving the NMOS transistor and improving the overall gain. The input is decoupled via a capacitor (C1), and the output is taken from the tuned load (C2), ensuring minimal signal loss and optimal impedance matching.
