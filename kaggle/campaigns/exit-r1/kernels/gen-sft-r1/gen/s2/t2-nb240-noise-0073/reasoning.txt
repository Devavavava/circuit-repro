The failure is primarily due to the insufficient gain (s21_db) in the current-reuse push-pull LNA topology. The anchor circuit uses a shared DC path with a tuned load (L2+C2), which may introduce parasitic effects and reduce the overall gain at the target frequency. Additionally, the current-reuse configuration, while effective in reducing noise, may not be optimally configured for maximum gain in this specific layout. The low s21_db value (7.45 dB) is far below the required 19.58 dB, indicating a fundamental gain limitation in the topology.

To address this, I propose a structural change that replaces the push-pull configuration with a single-stage common-gate (CG) topology, which is known for its high gain and good noise performance in low-noise amplifiers. The CG topology provides a low input impedance and high output impedance, which helps in achieving a higher voltage gain. Additionally, the use of a separate inductor (L3) for the output stage ensures a more stable and higher gain at the operating frequency. The removal of the shared DC path and the tuned load (L2+C2) simplifies the circuit and reduces parasitic interactions that may have been degrading the gain. The addition of a capacitor (C2) between the output of the CG stage and ground provides a low-impedance path for AC signals, further enhancing the gain and stability.
