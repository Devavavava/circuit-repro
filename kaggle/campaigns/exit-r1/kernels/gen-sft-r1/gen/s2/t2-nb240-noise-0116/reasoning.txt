The failure is primarily due to the insufficient gain (s21_db) of the LNA, which is below the required minimum of 19.8504 dB. The current topology uses a push-pull configuration with a current-reuse structure, but the gain is limited by the configuration of the NMOS and PMOS stages, as well as the load network. The tuned load (L2+C2) may not be optimally contributing to the gain, and the shared DC path may be introducing unwanted losses or impedance mismatches.

To address this, I propose a structural change that reconfigures the amplifier to a single-stage NMOS common-gate (CG) topology with an inductive source degeneration and a second NMOS stage for gain boosting. This configuration improves the gain by leveraging the inductive source degeneration to increase the transconductance and reduce noise, while the second NMOS stage further amplifies the signal. The removal of the PMOS push-pull structure and the tuned load simplifies the impedance matching and reduces the complexity of the DC path, allowing for better signal transmission and higher gain.
