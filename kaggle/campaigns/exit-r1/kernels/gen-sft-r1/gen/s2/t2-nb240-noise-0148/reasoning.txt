The failure is primarily due to the poor gain (s21_db) of the LNA, which is too low by approximately 13.6 dB (from 8.5 dB to the required 22.1 dB). This is likely caused by the push-pull structure of the amplifier, where the NMOS and PMOS transistors are competing for signal amplification, leading to a reduction in overall gain. Additionally, the current-reuse configuration may be inefficient in this topology, limiting the achievable gain. The shared DC path and the complex biasing network may also introduce unwanted signal losses and noise contributions, further degrading performance.

To address this, I propose a structural change to a single-stage common-gate (CG) LNA topology with an inductive source degeneration and a resistive load. This configuration enhances gain by leveraging the inductor's ability to provide a low impedance at the operating frequency, while the resistive load helps to increase the output voltage swing and improve stability. The removal of the push-pull structure and current-reuse network simplifies the topology, reducing signal loss and improving gain. The inductive source degeneration also helps to increase the transconductance of the NMOS transistor, further boosting the gain.
