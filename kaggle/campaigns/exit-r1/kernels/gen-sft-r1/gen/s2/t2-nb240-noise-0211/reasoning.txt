The binding constraint is the stability factor (mu_min_wide_0.01-50GHz), which is failing due to insufficient phase margin in the feedback loop of the LNA. The original topology uses a current-reuse push-pull configuration with a shared DC path, which may lead to parasitic feedback paths and reduced stability, especially over a wide frequency range. The lack of a dedicated feedback control mechanism or additional phase compensation elements contributes to this instability.

To address this, I introduce a dedicated feedback network with an additional NMOS transistor (ZN1) and a resistor (Rrep1) to provide a controlled feedback path from the output to the input. This feedback network helps stabilize the amplifier by introducing a phase shift that counteracts the instability caused by the parasitic feedback paths. The resistor Rrep1 provides a controlled impedance for the feedback, while ZN1 acts as a low-impedance path to ground, helping to dampen oscillations and improve phase margin over a wide frequency range.

These changes improve the stability factor (mu_min_wide_0.01-50GHz) by ensuring a more controlled and stable feedback mechanism, which directly addresses the binding constraint.
