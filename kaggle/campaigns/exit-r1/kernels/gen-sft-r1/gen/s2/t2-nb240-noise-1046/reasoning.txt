The failure is due to the insufficient stability margin (mu_min_wide_0.01-50GHz), which is critical for ensuring the LNA remains unconditionally stable across the entire frequency range of interest. The original topology, while meeting most performance metrics, lacks sufficient feedback or damping mechanisms to prevent oscillations or instability at off-band frequencies. The current mirror bias network and the LC-tuned loads provide some feedback, but they are not sufficient to guarantee a positive stability factor across the wide band from 10 MHz to 50 GHz.

To address this, I added a resistor (ZR2) between VOUT1 and VSS, which introduces a small amount of feedback at higher frequencies, helping to dampen potential oscillations and improve the stability margin. This change is minimal in terms of performance impact but crucial for meeting the stability constraint. The resistor provides a low-impedance path to ground at high frequencies, effectively adding a small amount of negative feedback that stabilizes the system without significantly degrading the noise figure or gain.
