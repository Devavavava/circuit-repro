The failure is due to the poor stability margin (`mu_min_wide_0.01-50GHz`) caused by the interstage LC network and the feedback paths in the two-stage topology. The LC interstage (L2 and C2) introduces a resonant path that can create a feedback loop between the two stages, reducing the stability margin at frequencies beyond the operating band. This is exacerbated by the current-mirror biasing network, which adds additional feedback paths and increases the risk of instability.

To address this, I replaced the LC interstage with a direct connection between the two stages, using a capacitor (C2) to block DC while allowing AC coupling. This eliminates the resonant feedback path that was destabilizing the circuit. Additionally, I changed the second stage from a common-source configuration to a common-gate configuration (NM2 connected with source to VDD and drain to output), which inherently improves stability by reducing the feedback from the output to the input. This change also helps in achieving a higher gain and better noise figure by reducing the output impedance of the second stage.
