The binding constraint `mu_min_wide_0.01-50GHz` is failing due to insufficient stability margin across a wide frequency range. The original topology, while effective at the center frequency (f0=2.4 GHz), lacks sufficient feedback or damping mechanisms to maintain stability at off-band frequencies. This is likely due to the shunt-peaked inductive load (L1+R2), which enhances gain at f0 but can introduce resonances or phase shifts that reduce stability at other frequencies. The feedback resistor (R1) provides DC biasing but does not contribute to wideband stability.

To address this, I added a PMOS transistor (PM1) in a common-gate configuration, connected between the feedback node (n2) and the output (VOUT1), with its source tied to VDD and body to VSS. This PMOS acts as a wideband stabilizing element, providing additional feedback at higher frequencies and helping to dampen resonances that could reduce the stability margin. The PMOS also helps to increase the phase margin across the band, ensuring that the stability factor (mu) remains above the required minimum over the full 0.01–50 GHz range. This structural change directly addresses the instability issue without compromising gain or noise performance.
