The failure is due to the poor stability margin (mu_min_wide_0.01-50GHz) across the frequency band, which is caused by the resonant LC interstage network (L2 and C2) creating a feedback path that destabilizes the amplifier at frequencies away from the center frequency (f0=2.4 GHz). This resonant network introduces a phase shift that reduces the stability margin, especially at the edges of the band.

To address this, I replaced the LC interstage network with a series inductor (L2) directly connecting the drain of NM1 to the gate of NM2, eliminating the resonant feedback path. This change reduces the phase shift introduced by the interstage network, improving the stability margin. Additionally, I replaced the second-stage tuned load (L3) with a direct connection to VDD, simplifying the topology and reducing the number of inductors, which helps in achieving a more stable and predictable frequency response.

I also reconfigured the bias network by connecting the current mirror reference (NM3) directly to the source of NM2 and removed the series resistors (R2 and R3) that were biasing the gates of NM1 and NM2. This change reduces the gate resistance and improves the signal integrity at the input and output stages.
