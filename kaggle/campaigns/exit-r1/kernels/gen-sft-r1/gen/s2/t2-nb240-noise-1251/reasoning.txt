The failure is due to the poor stability margin (mu_min_wide_0.01-50GHz) caused by the current mirror biasing network and the lack of a feedback path to dampen oscillations. The current mirror (NM3 and resistors R1, R2, R3) introduces a feedback loop that is not properly damped, leading to instability outside the design band. Additionally, the absence of a direct connection between the output and the bias network limits the ability to control the phase and gain margins across a wide frequency range.

To address this, I added a feedback path from the output (VOUT1) to the bias network by introducing a capacitor (ZC1) between VOUT1 and the bias reference node (n7). This provides a low-impedance path for high-frequency signals, improving the stability margin by reducing the phase shift and gain at higher frequencies. The capacitor ZC1 acts as a frequency-dependent feedback element, helping to stabilize the circuit across the entire 0.01–50 GHz range.
