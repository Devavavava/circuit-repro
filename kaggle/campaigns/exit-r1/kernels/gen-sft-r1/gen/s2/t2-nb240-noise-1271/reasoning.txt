The failure is due to insufficient stability margin (mu_min_wide_0.01-50GHz) across the band, which is a critical constraint for the LNA's robustness. The original topology relies on a current-mirror bias network with resistive feedback (R2, R3) to the gates of NM1 and NM2. This configuration introduces a feedback path that can destabilize the system, especially at off-band frequencies, leading to a negative stability margin. The LC interstage network (L2, C2) and tuned drain loads (L1, L3) are optimized for performance at f0=2.4 GHz, but they do not inherently suppress oscillations outside this frequency range, exacerbating the stability issue.

To address this, I replaced the current-mirror bias network with a direct gate biasing approach using a resistor (R2) from VDD to the gate of NM1, and a separate resistor (R1) from VDD to the gate of NM2. This eliminates the feedback path from the current mirror, reducing the risk of instability. Additionally, I restructured the LC interstage network to use a series inductor (L2) from VDD to the source of NM2, which helps in decoupling the second stage from the first, thereby improving the stability margin. The drain load of NM2 is now directly connected to VDD through a capacitor (C2), simplifying the topology and reducing parasitic feedback paths.
