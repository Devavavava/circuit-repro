The binding constraint `mu_min_wide_0.01-50GHz` failed due to insufficient stability margin across a broad frequency range. The original topology, while effective at the center frequency (f0 = 2.4 GHz), lacks sufficient feedback and impedance control mechanisms to maintain stability at off-band frequencies. The LC interstage and tuned drain loads provide strong resonance at f0 but do not inherently suppress oscillatory behavior outside this band. The current mirror bias network, while stable at f0, does not contribute to wideband stability.

To address this, I restructured the LNA into a single-stage common-gate topology with a source inductor and a feedback capacitor. This configuration enhances wideband stability by introducing a low-impedance path to ground at high frequencies and reducing the gain at off-band frequencies. The source inductor (L2) provides a high-impedance path at f0, improving noise performance, while the feedback capacitor (C2) introduces a controlled phase shift that stabilizes the amplifier over a broad range. The common-gate configuration inherently improves stability by reducing the Miller effect and providing a lower input impedance, which minimizes the risk of oscillation. This topology also simplifies the bias network, removing the need for a current mirror and reducing the number of inductors, which helps in achieving a more predictable and stable behavior across the entire 0.01–50 GHz range.
