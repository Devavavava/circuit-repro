The anchor circuit features a current-reuse push-pull configuration with a shared DC path and a tuned load. However, the push-pull topology may be causing signal cancellation or phase mismatches, especially at the operating frequency of 2.4 GHz, which degrades the gain. The use of a diode-connected NMOS (NM3) and PMOS (PM2) for biasing introduces nonlinearity and may not provide sufficient control over the transconductance of the main amplifying stages (NM1 and PM1), further limiting the achievable gain.

To address this, I replaced the push-pull configuration with a single-stage NMOS common-source amplifier (NM1) driven by a resonant inductor (L1) and a second inductor (L2) to provide a high-impedance load at the operating frequency. This topology ensures that the output voltage is amplified without signal cancellation, while the resonant inductors help to boost the gain and stabilize the circuit. The addition of a second NMOS transistor (NM2) as a current source, along with a resistor (R3) to ground, provides a stable bias current and enhances the transconductance of the main amplifier. This structural change improves the gain and ensures that the amplifier meets the required specifications.
