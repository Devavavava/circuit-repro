The failure is primarily due to the insufficient gain of the second stage, as evidenced by the s21_db margin being negative by ~0.3 dB. This indicates that the second-stage transistor (NM2) is not contributing enough gain, likely due to its drain being loaded by a resonant inductor (L3) that is too lossy or not sufficiently tuned at the operating frequency. The LC tank at the drain of NM1 (L1 and C1) and the interstage LC network (L2 and C2) may also be contributing to signal loss or phase mismatch that degrades the overall gain.

To address this, I modified the topology to improve the second-stage gain by replacing the resonant load on NM2 with a series inductor (L2) connected to the source of NM2, and a capacitor (C2) from the source of NM2 to VDD. This creates a source-degeneration inductor that enhances the transconductance of NM2 at the operating frequency, thereby boosting the second-stage gain. Additionally, I replaced the original LC tank on the first stage with a direct connection from the input to the gate of NM1, simplifying the input matching and reducing potential signal loss. The bias network was also restructured to ensure stable gate biasing without introducing unnecessary losses.
