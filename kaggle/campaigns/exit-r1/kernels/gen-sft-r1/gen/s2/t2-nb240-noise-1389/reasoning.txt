The failure is due to the poor stability margin (mu_min_wide_0.01-50GHz) caused by the resonant LC interstage network (L2 and C2), which introduces a feedback path that destabilizes the amplifier across a wide frequency range. This resonant feedback reduces the phase margin at frequencies near and above the operating point, causing the stability factor (mu) to dip below the required minimum. The original topology's interstage LC network creates a narrowband resonance that interacts poorly with the second stage, leading to instability outside the narrowband operating point.

To address this, I replaced the LC interstage network with a direct inductive coupling between the two stages. This eliminates the resonant feedback path that destabilizes the amplifier and improves the phase margin across a broader frequency range. The new topology uses a single inductor (L2) to couple the first and second stages, rather than an LC resonant network, which reduces the feedback and improves stability.
