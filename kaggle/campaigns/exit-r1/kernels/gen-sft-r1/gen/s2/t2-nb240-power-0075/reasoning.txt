The failure is primarily due to the insufficient gain (s21_db) of the LNA, which is below the required minimum of 23.4697 dB. The current topology, while well-structured, suffers from a poor voltage gain due to the shared DC path and the load configuration. The tuned load (L2+C2) is disconnected from the DC path, which limits the available voltage swing and amplification. Additionally, the push-pull configuration may introduce signal cancellation or phase mismatches that degrade the gain at the operating frequency.

To address this, I restructured the circuit by connecting the load inductors (L2 and L3) directly to the power supply (VDD) and ground (VSS), forming a more effective voltage gain stage. This change allows the signal to swing across a larger voltage range, improving the overall gain. I also modified the biasing network by connecting the reference NMOS (NM3) to the output node (n2) instead of ground, ensuring a more stable and efficient current reference. These changes enhance the signal amplification and reduce signal loss, directly addressing the low s21_db issue.
