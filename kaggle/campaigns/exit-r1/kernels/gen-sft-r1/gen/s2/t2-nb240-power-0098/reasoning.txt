The binding constraint is the insufficient gain (s21_db), which is too low by ~0.72 dB. This is likely due to the current-reuse topology in the anchor netlist, where the NMOS and PMOS stages are configured in a push-pull configuration. This topology may lead to poor signal amplification at the target frequency due to the opposing phase and current paths, which can cause signal cancellation and reduced gain. Additionally, the presence of multiple passive components and the shared DC path may introduce unwanted losses and impedance mismatches that further degrade the gain.

To address this, I propose a structural change by replacing the push-pull configuration with a single-stage NMOS amplifier with an inductive load. This configuration is known to provide higher gain and better impedance matching at a specific frequency, which is critical for the narrowband LNA. By removing the PMOS stage and the associated passive components, the circuit becomes simpler and more efficient, allowing for better signal amplification without the complications of current reuse and opposing phases.

The inductive load (L2) is retained but repositioned to provide a high-impedance path at the target frequency, enhancing the gain. The NMOS transistor is configured in a common-source configuration with a direct connection to the output, which is more effective for amplification. This change simplifies the circuit, reduces parasitic effects, and improves the overall gain performance.
