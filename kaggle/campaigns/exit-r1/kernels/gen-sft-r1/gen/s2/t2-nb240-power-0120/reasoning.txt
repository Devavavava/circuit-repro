The failure is primarily due to the poor stability and poor noise figure performance, as indicated by the binding constraint `mu_min_wide_0.01-50GHz` being far below the required minimum. The original topology features a current-reuse push-pull configuration with a shared DC path and a tuned load (L2+C2), but this setup introduces instability and poor noise characteristics over a wide frequency range. The NMOS and PMOS transistors are arranged in a way that causes strong feedback and poor impedance matching at higher frequencies, leading to a low stability factor (`mu`) and a degraded noise figure.

To address this, I propose a structural change that replaces the push-pull configuration with a single-stage NMOS amplifier with an inductive source degeneration and a separate tuned load. This topology improves stability by reducing feedback and enhancing impedance matching. The inductive source degeneration (L3) helps in stabilizing the amplifier and reducing noise, while the separate tuned load (L2+C2) ensures better frequency response and stability over a wide band. The removal of the PMOS stage and the shared DC path simplifies the circuit and reduces parasitic effects that contribute to instability.
