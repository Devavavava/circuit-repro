The failure is primarily due to the low forward gain (s21_db) of the LNA, which is insufficient to meet the required minimum of 22.4785 dB. This is likely caused by the poor impedance matching and the low transconductance of the active devices in the current-reuse topology. The push-pull configuration, while effective in some aspects, may introduce parasitic effects and impedance mismatches that degrade the gain at the operating frequency.

To address this, I replaced the push-pull configuration with a single-stage NMOS common-gate (CG) topology, which is known for its high input impedance and good gain characteristics at RF frequencies. The CG topology also allows for better impedance matching at the input and output ports, which is critical for achieving the required s21_db and s11_max_db. Additionally, the inductive source degeneration (L2) and the capacitive load (C2) are retained to provide a tunable load and improve the stability and gain of the amplifier.
