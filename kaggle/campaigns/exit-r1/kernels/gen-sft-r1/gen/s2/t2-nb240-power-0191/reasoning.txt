The failure is primarily due to the low forward gain (s21_db) of the amplifier, which is insufficient to meet the specification. The anchor netlist uses a push-pull configuration with a shared DC path and a tuned load, but the topology lacks sufficient gain boosting mechanisms and has a complex biasing structure that may introduce parasitic effects or reduce the signal path efficiency. The current-reuse approach, while effective in reducing power, may not be optimal for gain in this configuration, especially at the specified frequency of 2.4 GHz. The shared DC path and the use of multiple resistive and capacitive elements in the bias network may also contribute to signal degradation and poor impedance matching.

To address this, I propose a structural change that simplifies the topology and enhances the signal path. The revised netlist replaces the push-pull configuration with a single-stage common-source amplifier with an inductive source degeneration and a capacitive load. This configuration improves the transconductance and gain while maintaining a low noise figure. The use of an inductor (L3) in the source path of the NMOS transistor (NM2) helps to increase the effective transconductance and reduce noise, while the capacitive load (C2) ensures a high output impedance and better signal integrity. The removal of the PMOS stage and the shared DC path simplifies the biasing and reduces parasitic effects, leading to improved gain and stability.
