The binding constraint is the insufficient gain (s21_db), which is too low by ~0.74 dB. This is likely due to the push-pull configuration in the anchor netlist, where the NMOS and PMOS transistors are operating in a way that limits the overall voltage gain. The current-reuse topology, while beneficial for noise and power, may not be optimally configured for maximum gain in this narrowband application. The shared DC path and the tuned load (L2+C2) may also be causing signal attenuation or phase mismatch, reducing the effective gain at f0.

To address this, I replaced the push-pull configuration with a single-stage common-gate (CG) topology. In the revised netlist, NM1 is connected as a CG stage (its source is tied to VDD), allowing for higher voltage gain due to the low input impedance at the gate and the high output impedance at the drain. The addition of PM2 as a current source (with R4 and R5) provides a stable bias and helps maintain linearity, while the inductor L3 and capacitor C2 form a tuned load that enhances gain at f0. The input capacitor C1 and inductor L1 ensure proper signal coupling and impedance matching. This topology is more suitable for achieving high gain in a narrowband LNA without the push-pull limitations of the anchor netlist.
