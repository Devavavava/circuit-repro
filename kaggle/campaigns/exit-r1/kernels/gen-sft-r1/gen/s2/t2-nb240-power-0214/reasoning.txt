The binding constraint, `mu_min_wide_0.01-50GHz`, is failing due to poor stability in the wideband range. The original topology features a two-stage CS LNA with LC interstage matching, which, while effective at the design frequency (2.4 GHz), lacks sufficient feedback and impedance control across the wideband range (0.01–50 GHz). This results in a low stability factor (`mu`) at off-band frequencies, particularly due to the lack of a direct feedback path from the output to the input stage, and insufficient impedance isolation between stages.

The proposed netlist introduces a new topology that addresses these issues. It replaces the LC interstage matching with a direct inductive coupling between the stages, using a single inductor (L2) to couple the drain of NM1 to the gate of NM2, while also connecting the source of NM2 to VDD through an inductor (L3). This configuration enhances impedance matching and provides a more stable feedback path from the output to the input. Additionally, the output is now connected to VOUT1 through a capacitor (C2), which helps in filtering out high-frequency noise and ensuring a cleaner output signal.

The bias network is restructured to use a resistor (R2) from the drain of NM1 to VDD, and a resistor (R1) from the gate of NM2 to VSS, which provides a more stable biasing mechanism. The current mirror (NM3 and R1) is retained but repositioned to ensure more consistent current flow through the stages. These changes collectively improve the stability factor (`mu`) across the wideband range, addressing the binding constraint failure.
