The failure is due to the poor stability margin (mu_min_wide_0.01-50GHz) caused by the LC interstage network (L2 and C2) and the second-stage load (L3). These components create resonant peaks and phase shifts that reduce the stability margin at frequencies outside the narrowband tuning point (f0=2.4 GHz). The LC interstage network introduces a second resonant frequency, which destabilizes the system when viewed over a wide frequency range (0.01–50 GHz). This is exacerbated by the second-stage's tuned load (L3), which further adds phase shifts and potential resonances.

To address this, I modified the topology to remove the LC interstage network and replace it with a direct connection between the first and second stages, using a series inductor (L2) to provide impedance transformation and improve stability. The second-stage's load was changed from a tuned inductor (L3) to a simple inductor (L3) in series with the output capacitor (C2), which avoids introducing additional resonant peaks. Additionally, the biasing structure was simplified by removing the current mirror and resistive biasing, reducing complexity and potential phase shifts.
