The failure is due to the poor stability margin (mu_min_wide_0.01-50GHz) caused by the LC interstage network (L2 and C2). This network introduces a resonant peak that destabilizes the amplifier across a wide frequency range, particularly near the operating frequency (2.4 GHz). The interstage LC network creates a feedback path that reduces the phase margin, leading to a negative stability margin. This is exacerbated by the second-stage NMOS (NM2) being driven through this resonant network, which amplifies the instability.

To address this, I replaced the LC interstage network with an inductor (L2) directly connecting the first-stage drain (n2) to the second-stage gate (n5), bypassing the resonant LC network. This change eliminates the resonant feedback path and improves the phase margin. Additionally, I restructured the biasing network to use a single resistor (R2) from the second-stage gate (n5) to VDD, simplifying the biasing and reducing the number of nodes involved in the feedback loop. This simplification reduces the likelihood of unintended resonances and improves stability.
