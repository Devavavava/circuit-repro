The binding constraint `mu_min_wide_0.01-50GHz` is failing due to insufficient stability across the wide frequency range. The original topology uses a two-stage CS LNA with LC-tuned loads and interstage LC coupling, which limits the stability bandwidth due to the narrowband nature of the LC resonances. The stability factor `mu` is particularly sensitive to frequency variations, and the current structure does not provide enough inherent phase margin or feedback to maintain stability over the full 0.01–50 GHz range.

To address this, I propose a structural change that replaces the second-stage LC-tuned load with a source-degenerated NMOS transistor with a series inductor. This change introduces a more robust feedback path and increases the phase margin at higher frequencies. Additionally, the input DC-blocking capacitor is moved to a different node to avoid unintended resonances that might destabilize the circuit. The biasing network is retained but restructured to ensure consistent gate biasing without introducing additional instability.
