The binding constraint `mu_min_wide_0.01-50GHz` is failing due to poor stability across a wide frequency range. The original topology uses a two-stage common-source configuration with LC-tuned loads and an interstage LC network. This structure, while effective for narrowband gain and noise figure, introduces resonances and phase shifts that degrade stability at frequencies beyond the design band. The current mirror biasing and the LC interstage network likely contribute to phase shifts that reduce the stability margin at off-band frequencies, causing the `mu_min_wide` constraint to fail.

To address this, I replaced the two-stage LC-tuned common-source structure with a single-stage common-gate configuration, which inherently provides better stability due to its lower phase shift and higher output impedance. The common-gate stage is driven by a series inductor and a capacitive divider, which helps in isolating the input from the output and reduces feedback paths that could destabilize the circuit. The use of a common-gate stage also simplifies the biasing network, removing the need for a current mirror and reducing the number of inductors, which minimizes resonant effects and phase shifts across the wide frequency range.

Additionally, the output is now DC-blocked using a capacitor, and the bias network is simplified to a single NMOS diode and resistor, ensuring a stable and predictable bias point without introducing additional phase shifts or resonances.
