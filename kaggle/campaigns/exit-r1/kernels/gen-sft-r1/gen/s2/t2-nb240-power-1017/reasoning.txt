The failure is due to the insufficient stability margin (mu_min_wide_0.01-50GHz) across the frequency band, which is critical for ensuring unconditional stability in the LNA. The original topology uses a current mirror bias network with resistors R2 and R3 feeding the gates of NM1 and NM2, respectively. This configuration introduces a feedback path that can lead to instability, especially when the second stage's output is directly connected to the first stage's input through the LC interstage network. The lack of a direct feedback path from the output to the bias network limits the phase margin, resulting in a lower stability factor (mu) at off-band frequencies.

To address this, I added a direct feedback path from the output (VOUT1) to the bias network by introducing a capacitor ZC1 between VOUT1 and VSS, and a resistor ZR2 between VOUT1 and the bias reference node n7. This change enhances the phase margin by providing a low-impedance path for feedback at higher frequencies, thus improving the stability factor (mu_min_wide_0.01-50GHz) without significantly affecting the gain or noise figure.
