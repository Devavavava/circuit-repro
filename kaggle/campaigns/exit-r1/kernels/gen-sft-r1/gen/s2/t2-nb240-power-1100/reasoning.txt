The binding constraint `mu_min_wide_0.01-50GHz` is failing due to poor stability across the wide frequency range, likely caused by the LC interstage network (L2 and C2) and the second-stage tuned drain load (L3). These components create resonant peaks and phase shifts that reduce the stability margin at frequencies outside the narrowband center frequency (f0 = 2.4 GHz). The current topology lacks sufficient feedback or damping to suppress these unwanted oscillations, especially in the interstage and output networks.

To address this, I restructured the topology to eliminate the LC interstage network and instead used a direct connection between the first and second stages, reducing the number of inductors from 3 to 2. This minimizes the resonant peaks and phase shifts that destabilize the circuit at off-center frequencies. Additionally, the second-stage drain load was changed from an inductor (L3) to a capacitor (C2), which helps dampen high-frequency oscillations. The bias network was also simplified by using a single resistor (R2) to bias the first stage and a direct connection from the second stage to the output, reducing parasitic feedback paths that could destabilize the system.
