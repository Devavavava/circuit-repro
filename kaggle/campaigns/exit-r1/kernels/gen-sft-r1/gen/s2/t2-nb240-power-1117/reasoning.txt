The failure is primarily due to the insufficient gain (s21_db) in the current-reuse push-pull LNA topology. The binding constraint of s21_db is failing by ~0.32 dB, which is likely caused by the poor transconductance-to-noise figure trade-off in the current-reuse configuration, especially at the target frequency of 2.4 GHz. The push-pull structure may also be introducing unwanted phase and amplitude imbalances, reducing the overall gain and stability.

To address this, I replaced the original push-pull current-reuse configuration with a single-stage NMOS common-gate (CG) LNA topology. This structure offers better gain and noise performance at high frequencies due to its low input impedance and high output impedance, which helps in achieving a higher voltage gain. The CG topology also simplifies the biasing and reduces the number of parasitic elements affecting the gain and stability. The addition of a resonant inductor (L2) and capacitor (C2) in parallel with the output provides a tuned load, enhancing the gain at the target frequency. The removal of the PMOS push-pull stage and the current mirror structure reduces complexity and potential noise contributions, leading to a more stable and higher gain performance.
