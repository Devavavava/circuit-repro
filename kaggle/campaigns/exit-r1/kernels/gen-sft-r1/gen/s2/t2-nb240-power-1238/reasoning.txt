The failure is due to the poor stability margin (mu_min_wide_0.01-50GHz) caused by the resonant LC interstage network and the second-stage's tuned drain load. These components create parasitic feedback paths that destabilize the amplifier over a wide frequency range, especially at frequencies near the resonant points of L1, L2, and L3. The second-stage's gate is driven by a resonant LC network, which introduces phase shifts that reduce the phase margin, leading to instability.

To address this, I replaced the resonant LC interstage network with a direct connection between the first and second stages, using a capacitor (C2) to block DC while allowing AC signals to pass. This eliminates the resonant feedback path that was destabilizing the circuit. Additionally, I restructured the second-stage's drain load from an inductor (L3) to a direct connection to VDD, which simplifies the impedance and reduces the risk of parasitic oscillations. The biasing network was also simplified by removing the resistive biasing of the second stage and using a direct connection from the current mirror to the second stage's gate, ensuring a more stable and predictable bias point.
