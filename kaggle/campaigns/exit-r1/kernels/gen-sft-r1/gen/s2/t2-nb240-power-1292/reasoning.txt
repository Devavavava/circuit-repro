The failure is due to insufficient gain in the second stage, as evidenced by the binding constraint s21_db being 18.6447 dB, which is below the required 25.0075 dB. This indicates that the second-stage transistor (NM2) is not contributing enough gain, likely due to poor transconductance or improper load impedance. The current topology relies on a tuned inductor (L3) as the drain load for NM2, which may not provide sufficient impedance for high gain at the target frequency. Additionally, the biasing network may not be optimally driving the second stage.

To address this, I propose a structural change that replaces the second-stage tuned inductor (L3) with a common-source configuration using a resistor (R1) and a second NMOS transistor (NM3) as a current mirror. This change enhances the second-stage transconductance and provides a more stable and higher impedance load, thereby boosting the gain. The addition of a resistor (ZR1) between the output and the second stage also helps in improving the gain by reducing the Miller effect and ensuring a more controlled signal flow.
