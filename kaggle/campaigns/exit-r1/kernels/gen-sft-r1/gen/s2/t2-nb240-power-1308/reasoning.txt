The failure is due to insufficient stability margin (mu_min_wide_0.01-50GHz) over a wide frequency range, which is critical for robustness in the LNA. The original topology, while achieving good noise figure, gain, and power consumption, lacks sufficient phase and amplitude stability across the band. The interstage LC network and the current-mirror biasing contribute to a narrow stability band, leading to a negative margin at the binding constraint.

To address this, I restructured the circuit to include an additional inductor (L2) in the biasing network, forming a feedback path from the current mirror to the ground, which enhances phase margin and suppresses oscillatory behavior. This change introduces a new feedback loop that stabilizes the amplifier over a broader frequency range without significantly affecting the gain or noise figure. The addition of L2 between the current mirror's bias node (n7) and ground (VSS) provides a low-impedance path at high frequencies, reducing unwanted phase shifts and improving stability.
