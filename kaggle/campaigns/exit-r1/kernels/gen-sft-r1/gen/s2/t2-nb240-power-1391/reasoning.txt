The binding constraint `mu_min_wide_0.01-50GHz` is failing due to the poor stability of the two-stage LNA across a wide frequency range. The original topology uses a current mirror with resistive biasing, which introduces parasitic feedback paths and increases the risk of instability at off-band frequencies. The interstage LC network, while effective at the center frequency, does not sufficiently suppress unwanted resonances or feedback loops that can destabilize the amplifier over a wide band.

To address this, I modified the biasing structure by replacing the resistive biasing of the second stage with a direct connection to a grounded node, reducing the parasitic feedback path and improving stability. Additionally, I changed the topology of the second stage from a common-source configuration to a common-gate configuration, which inherently improves stability by reducing the Miller effect and feedback. This change also allows for better impedance matching and reduces the risk of oscillation at off-band frequencies. The removal of the interstage LC network and its replacement with a direct coupling through an inductor simplifies the topology and reduces the number of resonant nodes that could cause instability.
