The failure is due to the insufficient stability margin (mu_min_wide_0.01-50GHz) caused by the lack of feedback suppression in the interstage LC network. The original topology lacks a direct feedback path from the output to the input, allowing for potential instability across the band. This is exacerbated by the LC interstage resonating at the operating frequency, which may introduce phase shifts that reduce the stability margin.

To address this, I added a feedback path from the output (VOUT1) to the bias network (n7) using a capacitor (ZC1) and a resistor (ZR2) from the bias network to ground. This introduces a low-frequency feedback path that suppresses unwanted oscillations and stabilizes the amplifier over the wide frequency range. The capacitor ZC1 blocks DC while allowing AC feedback, and ZR2 provides a controlled path for the feedback current, ensuring stability without degrading the gain or noise figure.
