The binding constraint is the insufficient gain (s21_db), which is limited by the poor transconductance and poor impedance matching in the current-reuse topology. The original anchor netlist uses a push-pull configuration with a tuned load, but the transconductance of the NMOS and PMOS stages is not sufficiently high to achieve the required gain. Additionally, the current-reuse configuration may be causing some signal degradation due to the additional stages and feedback paths. The impedance mismatch at the input and output also contributes to the low gain, as the input is DC-blocked with a capacitor, and the output is loaded with a capacitor, which may not provide the optimal impedance transformation.

To address this, I propose replacing the push-pull configuration with a single-stage NMOS amplifier with a resistive load. This simplifies the topology and improves the transconductance by eliminating the second stage and feedback paths. The resistive load provides a better impedance match and helps in achieving higher gain. Additionally, the tuned load is replaced with a simple inductor and capacitor to ensure proper impedance matching at the desired frequency. The biasing network is simplified by using a single NMOS transistor for biasing, which reduces complexity and improves stability.
