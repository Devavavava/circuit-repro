The failure is primarily due to the insufficient gain of the LNA, as evidenced by the s21_db margin being -0.726569, which indicates the achieved gain (6.85897 dB) is below the target (25.0848 dB). The structure of the anchor netlist uses a push-pull current-reuse topology with a tuned load (L2+C2) and a shared DC path. However, this configuration likely leads to a poor voltage gain due to the low impedance of the PMOS output stage and the limited voltage swing of the NMOS stage. The current-reuse topology, while effective for noise and power, may not be optimal for achieving the required gain in this narrowband application.

To address this, I propose replacing the push-pull current-reuse topology with a cascode configuration that enhances the voltage gain. By using a cascode NMOS (NM2) driven by a resonant inductor (L3) and a second NMOS (NM1) in a common-source configuration, the overall gain is increased. The addition of a resonant inductor (L3) and a second NMOS stage provides a higher transconductance and better impedance matching, which boosts the gain. Additionally, the use of a separate inductor (L2) to ground helps in stabilizing the output and improving the impedance match at the output node.
