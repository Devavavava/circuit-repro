The failure is due to the insufficient gain of the LNA, as evidenced by the s21_db margin being -0.694324, which indicates the gain is below the required 24.8292 dB. The original topology uses a current-reuse push-pull configuration with a tuned load (L2+C2), but the gain is limited by the low transconductance of the NMOS and PMOS stages and the poor impedance matching at the output. The output is loaded by a simple capacitor (C3), which may not provide sufficient impedance transformation or phase control for optimal gain at 3.5 GHz.

To address this, I propose a structural change that replaces the push-pull configuration with a common-gate NMOS stage (NM2) driven by a cascode NMOS (NM1) and a current source formed by a diode-connected NMOS (NM3) and resistor (R2). This configuration improves the transconductance and provides better impedance matching. Additionally, a series inductor (L3) is added between the output and the load capacitor (C2), which helps in tuning the output impedance and enhancing the gain at the desired frequency. The current mirror (NM3 and R2) ensures a stable bias current, while the inductor L3 helps in achieving a higher gain by improving the output impedance and reducing the Miller effect.
