The failure is due to the poor stability margin (mu_min_wide_0.01-50GHz) caused by the feedback paths in the original topology. The interstage LC network and the current mirror biasing create parasitic feedback paths that reduce the stability of the circuit across the wide frequency range. This is exacerbated by the direct connection between the second stage's drain and VDD through L3, which can introduce unwanted resonances and phase shifts that lower the stability factor.

To address this, I restructured the topology to eliminate the direct LC load on the second stage and instead used a series inductor and capacitor to decouple the second stage from VDD. This helps to suppress parasitic feedback paths and improves the stability margin. Additionally, the current mirror biasing was reconnected to avoid creating low-impedance paths that could contribute to instability. The revised netlist ensures that the second stage is more isolated from the power supply and reduces the number of inductors that could resonate with parasitic capacitances.
