The binding constraint is the stability factor `mu_min_wide_0.01-50GHz`, which measures the stability of the LNA across a wide frequency range. The failure indicates that the circuit is unstable at some frequency outside the design band, likely due to the interstage LC network and the second-stage configuration. The LC interstage (L2 and C2) introduces a resonant frequency that may interact poorly with the second stage (NM2), causing a stability issue at off-band frequencies.

To address this, I changed the topology of the second stage from a common-source configuration with a tuned drain load (L3) to a common-gate configuration with a source inductor (L3). This change improves the stability by reducing the feedback path between the output and input at off-band frequencies. The common-gate configuration inherently provides better stability due to its lower input impedance and reduced Miller effect. Additionally, the source inductor (L3) helps to suppress unwanted feedback at higher frequencies.

The output capacitor (C3) is now connected to the drain of NM2, and the output is taken from the drain of NM2, which simplifies the output network and reduces parasitic effects. The bias network remains unchanged, ensuring that the gate voltages of NM1 and NM2 are still controlled by the current mirror (NM3, R1, R2, R3).
