The binding constraint `mu_min_wide_0.01-50GHz` is failing due to poor stability across the wide frequency range, particularly at frequencies outside the narrowband center frequency (f0 = 3.5 GHz). The original topology's two-stage structure with LC-tuned loads and interstage LC coupling introduces resonances and phase shifts that destabilize the amplifier at off-band frequencies. This is exacerbated by the shared biasing network, which couples the stages and reduces isolation, further degrading stability.

To address this, the topology is restructured to reduce interstage coupling and improve stability. The first stage is reconfigured to use a source-degeneration inductor (L1) instead of a drain-tuned inductor, which helps control the gain-bandwidth product and suppress oscillations. The second stage is restructured as a common-gate configuration (NM2), which inherently provides better stability and isolation compared to a common-source stage. The output is now directly coupled through a capacitor (C2), eliminating the second LC tank and reducing resonant peaks that destabilize the amplifier at off-band frequencies. The biasing network is simplified by removing the shared bias resistors and using a direct current mirror (NM3, R1, R2) to decouple the stages, improving stability and reducing noise contributions from the bias network.
