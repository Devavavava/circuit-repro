The failure is due to the poor stability margin (mu_min_wide_0.01-50GHz) caused by the interstage LC network and the feedback paths in the two-stage topology. The LC interstage (L2 and C2) introduces a resonant frequency that can create a feedback path from the second stage back to the first, reducing the stability margin. This is exacerbated by the current mirror bias network (NM3, R1, R2, R3), which adds additional feedback paths and increases the risk of instability.

To address this, I restructured the topology to eliminate the interstage LC network and simplify the feedback paths. The second stage is now directly connected to the first stage, and the drain load of the first stage is replaced with a simple inductor (L1) to VDD. The second stage uses a common-source configuration with a direct connection to the output through a capacitor (C2), removing the LC interstage and reducing feedback paths. The bias network is simplified to a single current mirror (NM3, R1) with a resistor divider (R2, R3) to bias the first stage, reducing complexity and feedback loops.
