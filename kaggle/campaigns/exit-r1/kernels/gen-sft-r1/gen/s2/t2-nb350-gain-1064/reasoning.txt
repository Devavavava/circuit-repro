The failure is due to insufficient gain, as the achieved s21_db of 8.79853 dB is far below the required minimum of 26.1318 dB. The low gain is likely caused by poor impedance matching and inadequate signal amplification in the current-reuse topology, particularly due to the weak signal path from the input to the output through the shared DC path and the tuned load. The current-reuse configuration may also introduce unnecessary noise and phase shifts that degrade the gain.

To address this, I propose a structural change that improves the signal path and enhances impedance matching. The new topology replaces the current-reuse push-pull configuration with a single-stage NMOS common-gate amplifier with a series inductor and a cascode configuration, which improves gain and stability. The addition of a second NMOS transistor in a cascode configuration helps to increase the output impedance and reduce the Miller effect, thereby boosting the overall gain. The inductor in the signal path ensures better impedance matching at the operating frequency, and the removal of the PMOS push-pull structure simplifies the signal path and reduces noise.
