The binding constraint `mu_min_wide_0.01-50GHz` is failing due to poor stability across the wide frequency range, particularly at frequencies beyond the center frequency (f0 = 3.5 GHz). The original topology has a two-stage CS structure with LC tank loads and a current mirror biasing network. While this configuration provides good gain and noise performance at f0, it lacks sufficient feedback and phase margin across the wide band, leading to instability and a low stability factor (mu_min) at off-band frequencies.

The root issue is the absence of a direct feedback path between the output and the bias network, which limits the system's ability to maintain stability over a wide range of frequencies. The current mirror biasing network (NM3, R1, R2, R3) is not actively involved in the feedback loop, and the LC tank loads (L1, L3) provide only narrowband impedance matching, not wideband stability.

To address this, I added a direct feedback path from the output (VOUT1) to the bias network via a new NMOS transistor (ZN1) and a resistor (ZR2). This creates a low-impedance path from the output to the bias network, enabling active feedback that enhances phase margin and stability across the wide frequency range. Additionally, a resistor (ZR1) was added between VDD and the interstage LC network (n4) to provide a DC path and improve impedance matching, further stabilizing the system at off-band frequencies.
