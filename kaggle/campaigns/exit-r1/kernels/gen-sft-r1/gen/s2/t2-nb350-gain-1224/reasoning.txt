The binding constraint is the insufficient gain (s21_db), which is limited by the poor impedance matching and the inadequate transconductance of the amplifier stage. The current-reuse topology in the anchor netlist introduces parasitic effects and non-ideal impedance transformations that degrade the gain at the target frequency. The push-pull configuration may also be causing signal cancellation or phase mismatches, further reducing the achievable gain.

To address this, I propose a structural change to the topology by replacing the push-pull configuration with a single-stage common-gate (CG) LNA with a resistive load and a shunt inductor. This configuration improves the input impedance match and enhances the transconductance contribution, leading to better gain performance. The resistive load provides a stable output impedance, while the shunt inductor helps to tune the output node for optimal performance at the target frequency. This change simplifies the signal path, reduces parasitic effects, and ensures better gain and stability at f0 = 3.5 GHz.
