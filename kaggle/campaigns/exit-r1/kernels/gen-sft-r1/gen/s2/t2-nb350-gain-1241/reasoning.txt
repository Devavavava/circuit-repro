The failure is due to the poor stability margin (`mu_min_wide_0.01-50GHz`) caused by the resonant LC tank in the interstage network (L2 and C2), which introduces a narrowband resonance that destabilizes the amplifier over a wider frequency range. This resonance causes a phase shift that reduces the stability margin, especially outside the narrowband tuning frequency (f0 = 3.5 GHz). The current structure, with its LC interstage and tuned drain loads, creates a feedback path that reduces the phase margin at off-band frequencies, leading to instability.

To address this, the LC interstage network (L2 and C2) is replaced with a series inductor (L2) and a capacitor (C2) connected directly to the drain of NM1, which eliminates the resonant feedback path and improves stability. Additionally, the second stage is restructured to use a common-gate configuration (NM2 with source connected to VDD), which inherently provides better stability and reduces the phase shift at higher frequencies. The output capacitor (C3) is moved to the output node, ensuring proper DC blocking without introducing additional resonances.
