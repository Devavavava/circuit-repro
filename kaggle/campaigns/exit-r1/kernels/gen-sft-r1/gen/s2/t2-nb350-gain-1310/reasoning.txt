The failure is due to the poor stability margin (mu_min_wide_0.01-50GHz) caused by the second-stage transistor (NM2) being too strongly coupled to the first stage through the interstage LC network. This coupling creates a feedback path that destabilizes the system at frequencies beyond the design band, leading to a negative stability margin. The current topology lacks sufficient isolation between stages, especially at higher frequencies, which is exacerbated by the direct connection of NM2's gate to the LC interstage network.

To address this, I replaced the second-stage transistor (NM2) with a PMOS transistor (PM2) and reconfigured the circuit to use a common-gate topology for the second stage. This change introduces a high-impedance output from the first stage and isolates the second stage from the first via a capacitive coupling (C2), reducing feedback and improving stability. The PMOS transistor in the second stage provides a higher output impedance, which helps in maintaining a stable feedback loop. Additionally, the removal of the interstage inductor (L2) and the reconfiguration of the drain load (L1) to be connected to the second stage's drain instead of the first stage's drain reduces the resonant feedback path that was destabilizing the circuit.
