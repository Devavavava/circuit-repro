The failure is due to the poor stability margin (mu_min_wide_0.01-50GHz) caused by the resonant LC interstage network (L2 and C2), which introduces a feedback path that destabilizes the system over a wide frequency range. The two-stage topology with LC interstage coupling creates a resonant peak that reduces the phase margin, especially when the second stage is driven by a resonant network. This is exacerbated by the lack of damping in the interstage LC network, leading to a narrow stability band and a low stability factor (mu) at off-band frequencies.

To address this, I modified the topology by removing the LC interstage network and replacing it with a direct connection between the two stages. This eliminates the resonant feedback path that destabilizes the system. Additionally, I restructured the second stage to use a common-gate configuration (NM2) with a source inductor (L2) to improve stability and reduce phase lag. The first stage was modified to use a source inductor (L1) instead of a drain inductor, which helps in improving the input matching and reducing the impact of parasitic capacitances. The output capacitor (C3) was retained to block DC while allowing AC signal passage. These changes improve the stability margin by removing the resonant feedback path and reducing phase lag at critical frequencies.
