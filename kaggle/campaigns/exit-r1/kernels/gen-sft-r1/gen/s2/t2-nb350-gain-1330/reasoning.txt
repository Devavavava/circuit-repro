The failure is due to the poor stability margin (mu_min_wide_0.01-50GHz) caused by the LC interstage network and the current mirror biasing network. The LC interstage (L2 and C2) introduces resonant peaks and phase shifts that interact poorly with the current mirror (NM3, R1, R2, R3), leading to instability across a wide frequency range. The current mirror's feedback path through R2 and R3 introduces parasitic feedback and phase shifts that reduce the stability margin at off-band frequencies.

To address this, I replaced the LC interstage network with a direct inductive coupling between the two stages. This eliminates the resonant LC tank that causes phase shifts and instability, while maintaining the inductive peaking needed for gain and impedance matching. The new topology uses a single inductor (L2) to couple the two stages directly, and the second stage's drain load is now a simple inductor (L3) to VDD, eliminating the second LC tank. This change improves the stability margin by reducing the number of resonant elements and feedback paths that can destabilize the circuit at off-band frequencies.
