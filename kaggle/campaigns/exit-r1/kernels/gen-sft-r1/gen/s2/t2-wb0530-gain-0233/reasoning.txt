The failure is primarily due to the insufficient stability margin (mu_min_wide_0.01-50GHz) across the wideband frequency range. The anchor netlist uses a resistive shunt-feedback configuration with a shunt-peaked inductor and resistor load. While this topology provides good gain and noise figure, it lacks sufficient phase margin and stability at higher frequencies, leading to a margin that falls below the required threshold. The single-stage CS amplifier with feedback is inherently limited in its ability to maintain stability over a wideband, especially when the feedback network is resistive, as it introduces phase lag at higher frequencies.

To address this, I propose a structural change to the topology by introducing a second-stage amplifier with a cascode configuration and additional inductive peaking. This allows for better control over the phase response and improves the stability margin. The addition of a PMOS cascode device and an inductor in the second stage helps to increase the output impedance and reduce the Miller effect, thereby improving the phase margin. The resistive feedback is replaced with a more controlled current feedback mechanism, which helps in maintaining stability over a wider frequency range.
