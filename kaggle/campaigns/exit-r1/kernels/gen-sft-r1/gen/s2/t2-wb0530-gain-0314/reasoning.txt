The failure is due to the insufficient stability margin (`mu_min_wide_0.01-50GHz`) in the original circuit, which is limited by the feedback topology and the inductive peaking network. The resistive shunt feedback and the single inductor in the load network do not sufficiently suppress oscillations across the wideband frequency range, especially at lower frequencies. This is exacerbated by the self-biasing configuration, which lacks explicit control over the feedback loop’s phase and gain behavior over the entire band.

To address this, I propose a structural change that introduces a second inductor in the feedback path and adds a PMOS transistor in the output stage, forming a cascode configuration. This improves the stability margin by introducing additional phase shift and damping at lower frequencies, while also enhancing the output impedance and reducing the impact of parasitic capacitances. The addition of the PMOS cascode stage helps isolate the output from the load, improving both stability and gain flatness over the wideband. The second inductor in the feedback path provides additional peaking control and helps balance the phase response across the band.
