The failure is due to the insufficient stability margin (mu_min_wide_0.01-50GHz) across the wideband frequency range. The original topology, while achieving good noise figure and gain, lacks sufficient feedback and impedance control to ensure stability over the entire band. The resistive shunt feedback and inductive peaking provide some stability, but they are not sufficient to prevent oscillations or instability at certain frequencies within the 0.01–50 GHz range.

To address this, I introduce a second stage with a common-gate (CG) configuration, which provides additional phase shift and impedance transformation to enhance stability. The CG stage (PMOS ZP1) is connected to the output of the original amplifier, and its source is tied to an inductor (L2) that provides a low-impedance path to ground at high frequencies, further stabilizing the circuit. The addition of a PMOS current source (PM2) and its associated bias network (R4, R5, R6) ensures a stable bias point for the CG stage, while the inductor L2 helps to suppress high-frequency oscillations. The resistor R1 is moved to a new position to provide additional feedback and control over the output impedance.
