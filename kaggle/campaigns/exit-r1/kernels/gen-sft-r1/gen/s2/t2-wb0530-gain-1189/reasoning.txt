The failure is primarily due to the insufficient stability margin (mu_min_wide_0.01-50GHz) across the wideband frequency range, which is caused by the feedback and load configuration in the original circuit. The resistive shunt feedback and shunt-peaked inductive load, while effective for broadband gain and input matching, do not provide sufficient phase margin across the entire band, especially at the lower end of the frequency range. This leads to instability and a margin deficit in the mu_min constraint.

To address this, I restructured the circuit to include a cascode configuration with an additional NMOS transistor (NM3) and a feedback resistor (R2) to the gate of the cascode transistor. This introduces an additional stage that helps stabilize the amplifier by increasing the output impedance and providing better phase control. Additionally, a second inductor (L2) is introduced to further enhance the phase margin and improve stability over the wideband range. The addition of a PMOS transistor (PM1) and a second resistor (R1) in a biasing configuration ensures proper DC operating points without affecting the AC performance. These changes collectively improve the stability margin while maintaining the required gain and input match.
