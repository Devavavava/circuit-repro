The failure is due to the insufficient stability margin (mu_min_wide_0.01-50GHz) caused by the feedback loop's inability to maintain a stable phase and gain margin across the wideband frequency range. The resistive shunt feedback and the shunt-peaked inductive load, while beneficial for bandwidth and gain, do not provide sufficient phase compensation at higher frequencies, leading to instability. This is exacerbated by the lack of a dedicated stability-enhancing element in the feedback path.

To address this, I added a PMOS transistor (ZP1) in a feedback configuration from the output to the input, creating an additional feedback path that helps stabilize the amplifier across the wideband range. This PMOS transistor acts as a phase-compensating element, improving the phase margin and ensuring stability at all frequencies within the specified range. Additionally, a capacitor (ZC1) was added between the output and ground to provide a low-impedance path at high frequencies, further aiding in phase compensation and reducing the risk of oscillation. These structural changes enhance the stability margin without significantly affecting the gain or noise figure.
