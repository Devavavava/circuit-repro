The failure is due to the noise figure (NF) being too high, which is primarily caused by the noise contribution from the NMOS transistor NM1 and the biasing network. The current-reuse topology in the anchor netlist introduces additional noise from the biasing resistors and diode-connected transistors, which degrades the NF. The noise is exacerbated by the lack of a direct DC path to the input, leading to poor noise matching and higher noise contributions from the biasing network.

To address this, I propose a structural change that removes the current-reuse biasing network and replaces it with a simpler, more noise-friendly configuration. The diode-connected NM3 and PM2 transistors, along with their associated resistors, are removed, and the gate of PM1 is directly connected to the input node n1. This eliminates the noise contribution from the biasing network and improves noise matching at the input. Additionally, a new NMOS transistor (NM2) is added to provide a direct DC path from the input to the gate of PM1, ensuring proper biasing and reducing noise contributions from the input node. The inductor L2 and capacitor C2 are reconnected to ensure proper load impedance and stability.
