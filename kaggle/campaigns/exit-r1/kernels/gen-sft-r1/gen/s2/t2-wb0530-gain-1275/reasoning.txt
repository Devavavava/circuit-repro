The failure is primarily due to the noise figure (NF) being too high, exceeding the maximum allowed by 0.174233 dB. This is likely caused by the noise contributions from the biasing network and the current-reuse structure, particularly from the NMOS diode NM3 and the resistive biasing network (R1, R2, R4, R5). These components introduce additional noise into the signal path, especially at high frequencies, which degrades the NF.

The proposed structural change addresses this by modifying the biasing configuration and reducing the noise contribution from the bias network. The NMOS diode NM3 is connected to a different node (n4 instead of VSS), which helps in decoupling the biasing network from the signal path. Additionally, a new NMOS transistor NM2 is added to provide a more stable and noise-friendly biasing current, improving the overall noise performance of the LNA. The inductor L3 is added to further decouple the biasing network from the signal path, ensuring minimal noise coupling at high frequencies.
