The failure is due to noise figure (nf_max_db) being too high, exceeding the specification by ~0.33 dB. This is likely caused by the noise contribution from the biasing network and the current mirror NM3, which adds noise to the signal path. The NMOS transistor NM3 is biased via a diode connection to VSS, which may not provide sufficient noise immunity or stability at the high frequency (1.22 GHz). Additionally, the PMOS current mirror PM2 is biased through R4 to VSS, which may introduce noise and instability in the signal path.

To address this, I replaced the NM3 diode connection with a resistor Rrep1 connecting n5 to VDD, which provides a more stable and lower-noise biasing path for the current mirror. This change reduces the noise contribution from the biasing network and improves the noise figure. The resistor Rrep1 helps in stabilizing the current mirror and reducing noise injection into the signal path, thereby improving the noise figure and meeting the specification.
