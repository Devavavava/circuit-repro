The failure is due to the poor stability margin (`mu_min_wide_0.01-50GHz`) caused by the current-reuse topology and the shared DC path in the anchor netlist. The shared DC path between the NMOS and PMOS stages introduces parasitic feedback and weakens the phase margin across the wideband frequency range. This is exacerbated by the tuned load (L2+C2) and the diode-connected NM3, which may not provide sufficient impedance variation to stabilize the feedback loop over the entire band. The current-reuse configuration, while beneficial for gain and power efficiency, can introduce instability if not carefully balanced with proper impedance matching and feedback control.

To address this, the topology was restructured to isolate the DC paths of the NMOS and PMOS stages, reducing parasitic feedback. The NMOS stage was reconnected to a separate DC node (n4), and the PMOS stage was connected to the output node (VOUT1) directly, ensuring a more stable and predictable feedback path. The removal of the diode-connected NM3 and the reconfiguration of the load (L2) to connect to the NMOS source (n1) instead of the output node (n4) improved impedance matching and reduced unwanted feedback paths. This change isolates the gain stages and enhances the stability margin across the wideband frequency range.
