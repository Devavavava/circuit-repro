While this topology provides good noise figure and input matching, it lacks sufficient phase margin at higher frequencies, leading to instability in the wideband range. This is likely due to the feedback loop not being sufficiently damped, especially at the higher end of the operating band.

To address this, I replaced the resistive shunt feedback with a current-reuse topology that includes a second transistor stage for improved phase margin and stability. The new configuration introduces a PMOS transistor (PM1) in a current mirror configuration with NMOS (NM3), which helps regulate the current and improve stability. Additionally, an inductor (L2) and capacitor (C2) are added to the output stage to provide additional phase compensation and ensure stable operation across the wideband. The inclusion of a second stage and additional passive components helps maintain a higher stability factor over the entire frequency range, addressing the `mu_min_wide_0.01-50GHz` constraint.
