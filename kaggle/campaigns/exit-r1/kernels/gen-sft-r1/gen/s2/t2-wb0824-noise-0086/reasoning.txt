The failure is due to the insufficient stability margin (mu_min_wide_0.01-50GHz) across the wideband operation, which is likely caused by the single-stage CS amplifier with resistive feedback and shunt-peaked inductive load. The stability is compromised due to the feedback loop and the inductive peaking, which may introduce phase shifts and resonate with parasitic capacitances, leading to instability at certain frequencies within the band. The single-stage topology lacks sufficient phase margin and feedback control over the wideband frequency range, especially when the inductive peaking interacts with the feedback network.

To address this, I replaced the resistive feedback with a second-stage amplifier and added a PMOS transistor for active feedback and improved phase margin. The second stage (PMOS PM1) provides additional gain and control over the feedback path, improving stability. The addition of a second inductor (L2) and a capacitor (C2) in the second stage helps in shaping the frequency response and reducing phase shifts. The resistive network (R4, R5) and additional PMOS (PM2) provide a more controlled biasing and feedback path, ensuring a stable operation across the wideband.
