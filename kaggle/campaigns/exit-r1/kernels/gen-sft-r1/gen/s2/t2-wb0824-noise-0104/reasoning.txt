The binding constraint is the stability margin (`mu_min_wide_0.01-50GHz`), which is negative, indicating instability over part of the band. The original circuit uses a resistive shunt-feedback CS LNA with a shunt-peaked inductive load. While this topology provides good noise and gain, it has limited stability due to the feedback path and the resonant peaking in the load. The stability margin is insufficient because the feedback and inductive peaking create a resonant peak that can cause oscillations at certain frequencies within the band.

To address this, I propose a structural change to the topology by introducing a second NMOS transistor in a common-gate configuration, which enhances the stability of the circuit. This configuration provides additional phase margin and reduces the risk of oscillation. Additionally, the inductive load is replaced with a series inductor and a resistor, which helps in shaping the frequency response and improving the stability margin. The addition of a second NMOS transistor and the modification of the inductive load to a series configuration provide better control over the phase and gain characteristics of the LNA, leading to an improved stability margin over the entire band.
