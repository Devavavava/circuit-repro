The failure is due to the insufficient stability margin (mu_min_wide_0.01-50GHz) across the wideband frequency range, which is critical for ensuring unconditional stability of the LNA. The original topology, while effective in achieving low noise and high gain, lacks sufficient feedback mechanisms to suppress oscillations at higher frequencies. The current-reuse structure and the tuned load (L2+C2) may not provide enough phase margin across the entire band, especially at the edges of the operating range.

To address this, I introduce a new feedback path by adding a capacitor (ZC1) between the internal node n2 and the node n6, which is part of the current-setting network. This capacitor provides an additional low-frequency feedback path, enhancing the phase margin and improving the stability factor (mu) across the wideband range. The capacitor helps dampen any potential oscillatory modes by introducing a controlled phase shift at higher frequencies, thus ensuring the stability margin meets the required specification.
