The failure is primarily due to the poor stability margin (`mu_min_wide_0.01-50GHz`) caused by the current-reuse topology and the shared DC path between the NMOS and PMOS stages. This configuration introduces a feedback path that reduces the stability margin, especially over a wide frequency range. The shared DC path and the use of a diode-connected NMOS for biasing also contribute to a less predictable and less stable gain structure, which limits the minimum stability factor (`mu_min`) across the band.

To address this, I restructured the circuit to eliminate the shared DC path and reduce the feedback coupling between stages. The new topology separates the DC paths of the NMOS and PMOS stages, improving the stability margin. Additionally, I replaced the diode-connected NMOS biasing with a resistor-based biasing network to reduce the nonlinearity and feedback effects that degrade stability. The inductive load is now connected directly to the output node, and the output capacitor is placed to ensure a clean signal path without introducing parasitic feedback.
