The shared DC path introduces a low-frequency feedback loop that destabilizes the amplifier over a wide frequency range, particularly affecting the phase margin at lower frequencies. This is exacerbated by the current-reuse configuration, which can introduce additional phase shifts and reduce the phase margin.

To address this, I propose a structural change that eliminates the shared DC path and reconfigures the current-reuse topology to isolate the feedback loops. This is achieved by moving the diode-connected NMOS (NM3) and its associated resistor (R2) to a separate DC path, and by reconfiguring the load network to avoid shared nodes. This change ensures that the feedback paths are isolated, improving the stability margin over the entire frequency range.
