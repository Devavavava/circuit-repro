The failure is due to the insufficient stability margin (mu_min_wide_0.01-50GHz) over the wideband frequency range. The original circuit, while meeting most performance metrics, lacks sufficient feedback and stabilization mechanisms to maintain stability across the entire band. The shunt-peaked inductive load and resistive feedback provide some stability, but the single-stage common-source topology with a single feedback resistor is insufficient to suppress oscillations and ensure a robust stability margin over the wideband.

To address this, I introduced a second-stage common-gate topology with an additional inductive peaking element and a second feedback path. This adds a second feedback loop and introduces additional phase compensation, which improves the stability margin across the entire frequency range. The addition of a second NMOS transistor (NM2) in a common-gate configuration, along with an inductor (L2) and additional capacitors (C1, C2, C3), enhances the phase margin and suppresses potential oscillations. The resistive feedback network (R1, R2) is retained but augmented with additional resistive elements (R4, R5) to provide more controlled feedback and improve stability.
