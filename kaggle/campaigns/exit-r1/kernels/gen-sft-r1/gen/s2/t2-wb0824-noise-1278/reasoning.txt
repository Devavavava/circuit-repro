The binding constraint `mu_min_wide_0.01-50GHz` is failing due to insufficient stability margin across the wideband frequency range. The original circuit employs a resistive shunt-feedback topology with a shunt-peaked inductive load, which, while effective in broadening the bandwidth, introduces a narrow stability window. The feedback resistor R1 sets the DC bias, but it may not provide sufficient phase margin over the entire 0.01–50 GHz range, especially when the inductive load (L1) and resistive peaking (R2) interact with the amplifier’s output impedance, leading to a drop in stability margin (`mu_min`) at certain frequencies.

To address this, I propose a structural change by introducing a second NMOS transistor (NM2) and a PMOS transistor (PM1) to form a current mirror configuration, which enhances the output impedance and provides better phase compensation. This configuration helps to increase the stability margin by improving the feedback loop's phase characteristics across the wideband range. Additionally, the addition of a PMOS transistor (PM1) and a capacitor (C1) in the input stage improves the input impedance matching and reduces the impact of parasitic capacitances, further contributing to a more stable and robust amplifier design.
