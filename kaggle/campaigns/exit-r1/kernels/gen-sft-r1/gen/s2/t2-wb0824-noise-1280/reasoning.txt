The failure is primarily due to the noise figure (NF) being too high, exceeding the maximum allowed value of 1.8796 dB by approximately 0.25 dB. This is likely caused by the noise contribution from the biasing network and the current mirror, which are not properly isolated from the signal path. The NMOS biasing network (NM3, R1, R2) and the PMOS current mirror (PM2, R4, R5) introduce noise that is not adequately suppressed, especially at the operating frequency of 1.385 GHz. Additionally, the lack of proper impedance matching and noise suppression in the output stage (C2, L2) may contribute to the elevated noise floor.

To address this, I modified the topology by adding a capacitor (ZC1) between the output node (n4) and the input node (n0). This change helps in decoupling the noise from the signal path and provides better noise suppression by creating a low-impedance path for high-frequency noise to ground. This structural change effectively isolates the noise sources from the signal path, thereby reducing the overall noise figure of the LNA.
