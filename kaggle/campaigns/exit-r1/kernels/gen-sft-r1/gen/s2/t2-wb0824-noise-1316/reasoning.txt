The failure is primarily due to the noise figure (NF) being too high, exceeding the maximum allowed value of 1.7783 dB by approximately 0.32 dB. This suggests that the noise contribution from the transistor NM1 and the biasing network is too large for the given frequency and topology. The current-reuse structure, while beneficial for gain and power efficiency, introduces additional noise paths through the biasing resistors and the diode-connected transistors (NM3 and PM2). These noise contributions are amplified at the input node, degrading the overall noise performance.

To address this, I modified the biasing network to reduce the noise contribution from the biasing path. Specifically, I replaced the original biasing structure involving NM3 and R1/R2 with a simpler configuration using a resistor Rrep1 connecting the bias node n5 to the input node n1. This change reduces the noise coupling from the bias network to the input signal path, improving the noise figure. Additionally, I introduced a new resistor Rrep1 and adjusted the connections of the diode-connected transistor NM3 to ensure that the biasing path is isolated from the signal path, minimizing noise injection.
