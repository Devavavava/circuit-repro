The failure is primarily due to the poor input matching, as evidenced by the s11_max_db constraint being violated by ~0.39 dB. The original circuit uses a simple NMOS/PMOS push-pull configuration with a tuned load, but the input impedance is not sufficiently controlled, leading to a mismatch at the desired frequency. The lack of a dedicated input matching network and the absence of a second-stage impedance transformation mechanism contribute to this issue. Additionally, the current-reuse topology, while beneficial for noise and gain, may not be optimally configured for wideband matching without additional impedance control.

To address this, I introduced a second-stage NMOS transistor (NM2) and a resistive feedback network (R3) to provide additional impedance control and improve input matching. The NM2 transistor is connected between VDD and n3, with its source tied to n2, allowing for a more controlled input impedance. The resistive feedback (R3) from n0 to VSS helps in stabilizing the input node and improving the reflection coefficient. These changes enable better impedance matching at the input, directly addressing the s11_max_db constraint.
