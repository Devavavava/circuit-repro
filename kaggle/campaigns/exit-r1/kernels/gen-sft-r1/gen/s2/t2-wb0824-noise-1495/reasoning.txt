The failure is due to the poor stability margin (mu_min_wide_0.01-50GHz) caused by the current-reuse topology and the shared DC path between the NMOS and PMOS stages. The shared DC path introduces parasitic feedback and phase shifts that reduce the stability margin, especially over a wideband. The NMOS and PMOS stages are not sufficiently decoupled, leading to a lower stability factor (mu) at the operating frequency and across the band.

To address this, I restructured the circuit to decouple the NMOS and PMOS stages by removing the shared DC path and reconfiguring the current-reuse mechanism. The PMOS stage is now driven by a separate DC path, and the NMOS stage is directly connected to the input through a series inductor, reducing parasitic feedback. The load network is also restructured to use a series inductor and capacitor, improving impedance matching and stability. This change isolates the NMOS and PMOS stages, reducing phase shifts and improving the stability margin over the wideband.
