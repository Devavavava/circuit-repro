The failure is due to poor stability margin (mu_min_wide_0.01-50GHz) caused by the current-reuse topology and the shared DC path. The current-reuse configuration introduces feedback paths that reduce stability, especially over a wide frequency range. The shared DC path between the NMOS and PMOS stages creates parasitic feedback, leading to a lower stability margin. Additionally, the tuned load (L2+C2) may resonate with parasitic capacitances, exacerbating instability at higher frequencies.

To address this, I replaced the current-reuse topology with a cascode configuration, which inherently improves stability by isolating the input from the output through a high-impedance node. The cascode structure (NM2 and L3) helps suppress unwanted feedback paths and enhances the stability margin. The removal of the shared DC path and the reconfiguration of the load network (L2 and C2) to be connected to a separate node (n6) reduces parasitic interactions. This structural change ensures a more predictable and stable operation across the wideband frequency range, directly improving the mu_min_wide_0.01-50GHz metric.
