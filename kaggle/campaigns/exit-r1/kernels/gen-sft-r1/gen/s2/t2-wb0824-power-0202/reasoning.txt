The failure is primarily due to the noise figure (NF) being too high, exceeding the specification by approximately 0.2 dB. This is likely caused by the noise contributions from the biasing network and the current-reuse structure. The NMOS diode NM3 and the resistive biasing network (R1, R2) introduce noise that is amplified by the LNA's gain structure, especially at the high frequency of operation (1.38 GHz). Additionally, the current-reuse topology may not be optimally configured for noise suppression at this frequency, leading to a higher-than-acceptable noise floor.

To address this, I restructured the biasing network to reduce noise contributions and improve the signal-to-noise ratio. Specifically, I replaced the NMOS diode NM3 with a PMOS diode connected to a different internal node (n4), which helps in reducing the noise contribution from the biasing network. Additionally, I changed the connection of the resistive biasing network (R2) to a different internal node (n2), which helps in stabilizing the bias point and reducing noise. These changes improve the noise figure by reducing the noise contributions from the biasing network and improving the signal path.
