The binding constraint is the minimum stability factor (mu_min_wide_0.01-50GHz), which is failing due to insufficient phase margin in the feedback loop across the wideband frequency range. The anchor netlist uses a resistive shunt feedback topology with a shunt-peaked inductive load, which, while effective for broadband gain and noise figure, lacks sufficient phase compensation to maintain stability over the entire 0.01–50 GHz range. The feedback network is too simple and does not provide enough phase shift to prevent oscillation at higher frequencies, especially when the inductive peaking interacts with the feedback path.

To address this, I replace the resistive shunt feedback with a more robust feedback structure that includes an active feedback path using a PMOS transistor and additional inductors and capacitors to provide phase compensation. This new topology introduces a second-stage feedback loop with a PMOS transistor (PM1) and an inductor (L2) to the ground, which helps to stabilize the circuit by adding a controlled phase shift at higher frequencies. The addition of a PMOS transistor and an inductive feedback path provides better control over the phase margin and ensures stability across the entire frequency range. The new inductor (L3) and capacitor (C2) in the output path also help to smooth the frequency response and reduce unwanted resonances that could destabilize the circuit.
