The failure is due to insufficient stability margin (`mu_min_wide_0.01-50GHz`) across the wideband frequency range. The original topology relies on a resistive shunt feedback and a shunt-peaked inductive load, which may not provide enough phase margin over the entire band, especially at lower frequencies where the feedback loop becomes less effective. This results in a negative stability margin, indicating potential instability or oscillation.

To address this, I introduce a second-stage common-gate (CG) amplifier with a capacitive coupling to the output. This adds a second pole-zero pair that enhances the phase margin across the band. The CG stage also provides additional gain and helps stabilize the feedback loop. The addition of a second NMOS transistor (`NM2`) and a capacitive coupling (`C2`) between the output and the second stage creates a more robust feedback path, improving stability without sacrificing bandwidth or noise performance.
