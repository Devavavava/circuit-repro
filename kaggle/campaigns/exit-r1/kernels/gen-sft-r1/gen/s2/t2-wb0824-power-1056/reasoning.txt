The failure is due to the noise figure (NF) being too high, exceeding the specified maximum of 2.2064 dB. The noise contribution is primarily dominated by the input transistor NM1 and the biasing network. The current-reuse topology in the anchor netlist introduces additional noise paths through the biasing resistors (R1, R2, R4, R5) and the diode-connected transistors (NM3, PM2), which increase the overall noise floor. The noise from the bias network and the diode-connected transistors is not adequately suppressed, leading to a higher-than-allowed NF.

To address this, I introduce a new PMOS transistor (ZP1) and a resistor (Rrep1) to create a feedback path from the output (VOUT1) to the bias node (n5). This feedback path helps to stabilize the biasing network and reduce the noise contribution from the biasing resistors and diode-connected transistors. The new PMOS (ZP1) acts as a noise-canceling element by providing a low-noise path from the output to the bias node, reducing the noise injected into the input transistor NM1. The resistor Rrep1 provides a controlled feedback path that helps to maintain a stable bias point and reduce noise coupling from the output to the input.
