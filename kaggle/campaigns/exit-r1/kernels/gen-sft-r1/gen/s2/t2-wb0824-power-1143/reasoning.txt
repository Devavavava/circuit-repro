The failure is primarily due to the noise figure (nf_max_db) exceeding the specification. This is likely caused by the noise contributions from the biasing network and the current-reuse structure. The diode-connected NMOS (NM3) and PMOS (PM2) in the bias network introduce additional noise, especially at high frequencies, which degrades the noise performance. Additionally, the current-reuse configuration may not be optimally suppressing the noise from the input transistor (NM1) due to the shared DC path and the load configuration (L2+C2), which may not be optimally tuned for the wideband operation.

To address this, I propose modifying the biasing network to reduce noise contributions. By replacing the diode-connected NM3 with a resistor (Rrep1) connected to an internal node (n6), we decouple the noise from the biasing network and reduce its impact on the noise figure. This change simplifies the biasing path and reduces the noise contribution from the diode-connected transistor. The resistor Rrep1 provides a more stable and predictable biasing point without introducing additional noise sources.
