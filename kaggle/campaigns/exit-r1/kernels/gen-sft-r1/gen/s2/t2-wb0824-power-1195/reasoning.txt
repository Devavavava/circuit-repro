The failure is primarily due to the inadequate stability margin (`mu_min_wide_0.01-50GHz`) in the original circuit. The resistive shunt-feedback topology, while effective for noise and input matching, lacks sufficient phase margin across the wideband frequency range, especially at the lower end of the band. The shunt-peaked inductor (L1) and resistive load (R2) contribute to a phase shift that reduces the stability margin, causing the circuit to fail the stability constraint.

To address this, I redesigned the topology to include a cascode structure with an additional NMOS transistor (NM3) and a resistive feedback network (R1, R2, R4, R5) to improve phase margin. This change introduces a second stage that helps control the phase response over the wideband range, ensuring sufficient stability. The addition of a second inductor (L2) in the load network further enhances the phase characteristics and helps maintain stability across the entire band. The resistive feedback network (R4, R5, R2) also helps regulate the gain and improve the stability margin by providing a more controlled feedback path.
