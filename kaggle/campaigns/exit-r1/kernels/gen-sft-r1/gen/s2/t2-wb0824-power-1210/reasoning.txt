The failure is due to the insufficient stability margin (mu_min_wide_0.01-50GHz) over the wideband frequency range. The anchor circuit uses a resistive shunt-feedback configuration with a shunt-peaked inductive load, which, while effective for broadband matching and gain, lacks sufficient phase margin across the wideband. The feedback network and load peaking may not provide enough phase shift to ensure stability at all frequencies within the 0.01–50 GHz range, leading to a negative stability margin.

To address this, I replaced the resistive shunt-feedback configuration with a cascode topology that introduces additional phase margin through the cascode device. The NMOS cascode transistor (NM3) adds a second stage that improves the output impedance and provides better phase characteristics, enhancing stability. Additionally, the input network was restructured to include a series inductor (L2) and a capacitor (C1) to improve the input impedance match and reduce unwanted feedback paths. The addition of a second NMOS transistor (NM2) in a current mirror configuration helps regulate the bias current and improve linearity. Finally, a PMOS transistor (PM1) was added to the bias network to provide a more stable and controllable current source, further improving stability and reducing noise contributions.
