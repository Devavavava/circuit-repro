The failure is due to noise figure (NF) being too high, exceeding the maximum allowed value of 2.0976 dB. This is likely caused by the noise contributions from the biasing network and the current mirror, which are not adequately suppressed in the original topology. The NMOS NM3 and PMOS PM2 biasing structures introduce noise that affects the overall noise performance, especially at the target frequency of ~1.386 GHz. Additionally, the current mirror configuration and the load network may not be optimally designed for noise suppression in this wideband application.

To address this, I replaced the original current mirror and biasing structure with a simpler and more noise-efficient configuration. The NMOS NM3 is now connected to the output node (n2) instead of being tied to VSS, which reduces noise coupling from the bias network. The PMOS PM2 is connected to a new internal node (n6) and its source is connected to VDD, allowing for a more stable and noise-clean current reference. The resistors R4 and R5 are reconnected to the new node n6, simplifying the current reference and reducing noise contribution. These changes help lower the noise figure by reducing the impact of the biasing network on the signal path, and by providing a more stable and noise-clean current reference for the amplifier.
