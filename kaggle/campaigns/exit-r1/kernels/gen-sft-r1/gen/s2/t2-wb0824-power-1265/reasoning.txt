The failure is due to poor stability margin (`mu_min_wide_0.01-50GHz`) caused by insufficient feedback and phase compensation in the current-reuse LNA topology. The original design lacks a direct feedback path from the output to the input, leading to phase shifts that reduce stability across the wideband frequency range. This is exacerbated by the shared DC path and the lack of a dedicated stability-enhancing component.

To address this, I added a feedback path using a resistor `Rrep1` connecting the gate of NM3 (the diode-connected NMOS) to VDD and a capacitor `ZC1` connecting the source of PM1 to VSS. This creates a low-frequency feedback loop that stabilizes the amplifier and improves phase margin across the band. The resistor `Rrep1` provides a controlled feedback path, while the capacitor `ZC1` helps manage high-frequency phase shifts, ensuring the stability factor (`mu`) meets the required minimum across the entire 0.01–50 GHz range.
