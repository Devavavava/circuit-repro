The failure is due to the noise figure (NF) being too high, exceeding the maximum allowed value of 1.9963 dB. The noise performance is likely dominated by the input transistor NM1 and the biasing network, particularly the diode-connected NMOS NM3 and the resistive biasing elements R1, R2, R4, and R5. The current-reuse topology, while beneficial for gain and power efficiency, may have introduced noise contributions from the biasing network that are not adequately suppressed in the current configuration.

To address this, I modified the biasing network to reduce its impact on the noise figure. Specifically, I replaced the resistive biasing of the NMOS gate with a direct connection from the diode-connected NM3 to the input node n1, eliminating the resistive divider R1 and R2. This change reduces the noise contribution from the biasing network and improves the signal-to-noise ratio at the input. Additionally, I replaced the PMOS biasing network with a simpler configuration that avoids unnecessary noise-generating components. These structural changes directly address the noise issue without compromising the gain or stability of the LNA.
