The binding constraint is the minimum stability factor (mu_min_wide_0.01-50GHz), which is failing due to insufficient stability in the wideband range. The original circuit uses a resistive shunt feedback topology with a shunt-peaked inductive load, which, while effective for broadband matching and gain, may not provide sufficient phase margin across the entire 0.01–50 GHz range. This is likely due to the feedback network and load configuration not adequately suppressing oscillatory modes at the lower end of the band.

To address this, I propose a structural change to the topology by introducing a second inductor (L2) in series with the output capacitor (C2), and adding a second NMOS transistor (NM3) along with a resistor (R3) to create a current mirror-like structure. This change enhances the phase margin and provides better impedance control over the wideband range. The addition of a second inductor and transistor helps to stabilize the circuit by introducing additional phase shift and impedance buffering, which improves the stability factor across the entire frequency range.
