The original circuit relies on a resistive shunt feedback and a shunt-peaked inductive load, which may not provide sufficient phase margin at higher frequencies, leading to instability. This is exacerbated by the lack of a dedicated stability-enhancing component in the feedback path, especially at the high end of the band.

To address this, I introduced a PMOS transistor (PM1) in the feedback path, creating a second-stage feedback loop that enhances phase margin and stabilizes the amplifier over the entire frequency range. This PMOS transistor is connected between the output node (n3) and the input node (n1), with its source tied to VSS and gate connected to the node after the inductor (n2). This addition introduces a second feedback path that helps maintain stability without significantly degrading gain or noise performance.
