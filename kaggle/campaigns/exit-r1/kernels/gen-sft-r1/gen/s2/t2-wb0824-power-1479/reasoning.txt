The failure is due to the insufficient stability margin (mu_min_wide_0.01-50GHz) across the wideband frequency range, which is likely caused by the single-stage CS LNA topology with shunt feedback and shunt-peaked load. This configuration may introduce resonances or phase shifts that reduce stability, especially at higher frequencies. The single NMOS transistor and the resistive feedback loop may not provide sufficient phase margin over the entire band, leading to instability at the edges of the operating range.

To address this, I propose a structural change to the topology by introducing a second NMOS transistor in a common-gate configuration, which enhances the phase margin and improves stability. Additionally, a capacitive coupling from the input to the gate of the second transistor helps in broadening the bandwidth and reducing unwanted feedback effects. The inclusion of an inductor in series with the output capacitor provides better impedance matching and enhances the output swing, while the resistive feedback is restructured to ensure a more stable DC operating point. These changes collectively improve the stability margin and ensure the LNA meets the required specifications across the entire frequency range.
