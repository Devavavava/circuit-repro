The binding constraint is the stability margin (`mu_min_wide_0.01-50GHz`), which is negative, indicating instability over a wide frequency range. The failure is due to insufficient phase margin in the feedback loop, likely caused by the single-stage CS amplifier with shunt feedback and a shunt-peaked load. The feedback network, while helping with input matching, introduces phase lag at higher frequencies, and the inductive peaking may not provide enough phase boost to maintain stability across the entire band. This is exacerbated by the single-stage topology, which has limited gain and phase control.

To address this, I propose a dual-stage topology with a cascode structure and a more controlled feedback path. The first stage uses a cascode NMOS to improve output impedance and stability, while the second stage provides additional gain and phase control. An additional inductor and capacitor are used to shape the frequency response and enhance phase margin. The feedback resistor is repositioned to provide better DC biasing and stability. This topology increases the phase margin by introducing additional poles and zeros that can be tuned to ensure stability over the entire band.
