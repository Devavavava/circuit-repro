The failure is due to the poor stability margin (mu_min_wide_0.01-50GHz), which indicates instability over a wide frequency range. This is likely caused by the shared DC path between the NMOS and PMOS stages, which introduces a feedback loop that degrades stability, especially at higher frequencies. The current-reuse topology in the anchor netlist, with the PMOS and NMOS stages sharing a common DC node, creates a parasitic feedback path that reduces the phase margin and causes the mu_min to fall below the required threshold.

To address this, I restructure the circuit to isolate the DC paths of the NMOS and PMOS stages. This is achieved by using a separate DC path for the PMOS stage and connecting the NMOS stage directly to ground. This change eliminates the parasitic feedback path, improving the stability margin. Additionally, the load inductor (L2) is connected to the output node (VOUT1) instead of an internal node, which helps in maintaining a stable output impedance and improves the overall stability of the amplifier.
