The binding constraint is the stability metric `mu_min_wide_0.01-50GHz`, which measures the stability of the LNA across a wide frequency range. The failure indicates that the circuit is unstable outside the narrowband tuning frequency (f0 = 1.575 GHz), likely due to the feedback path from the output to the input through the second stage and the LC interstage network. This feedback, combined with the current-mirror biasing and the LC resonant loads, creates a phase shift that reduces the stability margin at off-band frequencies.

To address this, I modified the topology to reduce the feedback path and improve stability. I replaced the second stage's source connection from VSS to the drain of the first stage (NM1), effectively creating a cascode configuration. This change reduces the Miller effect and feedback from the second stage to the first, improving stability. Additionally, I restructured the LC interstage network to connect the first stage's drain to the second stage's source via an inductor (L2), which helps decouple the stages and suppress unwanted feedback. The current-mirror biasing was simplified to reduce complexity and potential instability sources.
