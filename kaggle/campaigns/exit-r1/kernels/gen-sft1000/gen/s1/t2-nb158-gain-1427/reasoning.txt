The binding constraint is the stability margin (mu_min_wide_0.01-50GHz), which is negative, indicating instability over a wide frequency range. This is likely due to the LC interstage network (L2 and C2) creating a resonant feedback path that destabilizes the amplifier at frequencies outside the narrowband center frequency (f0). The two-stage structure with LC loads and interstage coupling introduces phase shifts that reduce the stability margin, especially at higher frequencies.

To address this, I replaced the LC interstage network with a direct connection between the two stages using a series inductor (L2) and a capacitor (C2) to ground, which reduces the feedback path and improves stability. Additionally, I reconfigured the second stage to use a common-gate configuration (NM2) with its source connected to the drain of NM1, which increases the output impedance and reduces the Miller effect, further enhancing stability. The bias network was also simplified by removing the resistor R1 and NM3, and using a direct connection from VDD to the bias node (n7), which reduces complexity and potential instability sources.
