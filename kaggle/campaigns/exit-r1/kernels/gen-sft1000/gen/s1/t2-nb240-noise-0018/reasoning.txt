The failure is due to insufficient gain (s21_db) caused by the limited impedance transformation and poor signal delivery from the active device to the output. The current-reuse topology in the anchor netlist has a shared DC path and a tuned load (L2+C2), which may not provide sufficient voltage gain at the operating frequency due to impedance mismatches and signal loss through the current reuse structure. The push-pull configuration also introduces additional losses and phase shifts that degrade the gain.

To address this, the topology is restructured to use a common-gate NMOS transistor (NM2) in parallel with the main NMOS transistor (NM1), forming a cascode-like configuration. This configuration improves the output impedance and enhances gain by reducing the Miller effect and increasing the transconductance. The removal of the tuned load (L2+C2) and the addition of a direct inductive load (L3) on the output node (n2) ensures a higher voltage gain at the target frequency. The PMOS transistor (PM1) is repositioned to provide a higher impedance path to the output, while the NMOS transistor (NM3) is used to set the bias point for the main amplifier. These changes lead to a more efficient signal transfer and higher gain, directly addressing the s21_db constraint.
