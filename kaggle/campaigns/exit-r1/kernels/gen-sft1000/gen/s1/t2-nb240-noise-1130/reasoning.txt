The failure is due to insufficient stability and poor noise performance in the interstage LC network, leading to a negative margin in the mu_min_wide_0.01-50GHz constraint. The original topology has an LC interstage (L2 and C2) that introduces resonant peaks and phase shifts, which destabilize the amplifier over a wide frequency range. This causes the stability factor (mu) to dip below the required minimum at frequencies outside the narrowband center frequency (f0), leading to oscillation risk or poor noise behavior.

To address this, I replaced the LC interstage with a series inductor (L2) directly coupling the output of the first stage (NM1) to the gate of the second stage (NM2), while adding a capacitor (C2) in parallel with the source of NM1. This change reduces the resonant effects of the interstage network and improves the phase margin. Additionally, I removed the DC-blocking capacitor (C1) and replaced it with a direct connection from the input (VIN1) to the gate of NM1, simplifying the input path and reducing parasitic effects that contribute to noise and instability.

These structural changes improve the stability factor (mu) over a wider frequency range and reduce noise contributions from the interstage network, addressing the binding constraint.
