The binding constraint `mu_min_wide_0.01-50GHz` is failing due to insufficient stability across the wide frequency band, likely caused by the LC resonant structures and the feedback paths in the two-stage topology. The LC interstage and tuned drain loads may introduce resonances or phase shifts that reduce the stability margin at frequencies outside the narrowband center frequency (f0 = 2.4 GHz). The current mirror biasing and the resistive gate biasing also contribute to feedback and noise coupling, which may destabilize the circuit at off-center frequencies.

To address this, I propose a structural change by reconfiguring the second stage and the interstage network. Instead of a second common-source stage with a tuned drain load, I replace it with a cascode configuration that enhances stability and suppresses unwanted feedback. The interstage LC network is modified to decouple the stages more effectively, while the drain load is replaced with a direct connection to VDD, reducing resonant feedback. Additionally, the biasing network is simplified and integrated into the cascode structure to minimize noise and feedback coupling.
