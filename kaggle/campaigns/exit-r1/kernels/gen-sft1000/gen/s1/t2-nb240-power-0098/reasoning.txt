The failure in the binding constraint is due to the insufficient gain (s21_db) of the LNA, which is limited by the poor transconductance and impedance matching in the current-reuse push-pull configuration. The structure relies on a shared DC path with a tuned load (L2+C2), which may introduce parasitic effects and degrade the gain at the operating frequency. Additionally, the current-reuse topology may not be effectively amplifying the signal due to the limited gain contribution from the NMOS and PMOS stages, especially at the narrowband frequency of 2.4 GHz.

To address this, I propose replacing the current-reuse push-pull configuration with a single-stage NMOS common-gate (CG) amplifier with an inductive source degeneration and a second NMOS stage for gain boosting. This topology improves the transconductance and impedance matching, while the inductive source degeneration enhances the gain and stability at the target frequency. The removal of the PMOS stage and the tuned load (L2+C2) reduces parasitic effects and simplifies the DC path, leading to better gain and noise performance.
