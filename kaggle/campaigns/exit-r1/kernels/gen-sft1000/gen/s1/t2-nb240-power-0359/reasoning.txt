The failure is primarily due to insufficient gain (s21_db), which is limited by the shunt-peaked load configuration and the resistive feedback topology. The resistive shunt feedback and the inductor-based shunt peaking in the anchor netlist create a trade-off between input matching and gain, especially at the target frequency of 2.4 GHz. The feedback resistor (R1) and the shunt inductor (L1) are not optimally configured to maintain both high gain and stable impedance matching, resulting in a marginal gain that fails the specification.

To address this, I propose a structural change that replaces the resistive shunt feedback with a current-reuse topology, which enhances gain while maintaining a good input match. This is achieved by introducing a second NMOS transistor (NM2) in a cascode configuration, along with additional inductors (L2) and capacitors (C1, C2, C3) to improve impedance transformation and frequency response. The current-reuse topology allows for higher transconductance and better power efficiency, directly improving the gain (s21_db) and meeting the required specifications.
