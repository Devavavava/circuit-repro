The binding constraint is the stability factor `mu_min_wide_0.01-50GHz`, which is negative, indicating instability over a wide frequency range. This is likely due to the interstage LC network (L2 and C2) creating a resonant feedback path that destabilizes the second stage, especially at frequencies outside the narrowband center frequency (2.4 GHz). The LC interstage network introduces a phase shift that, when combined with the second-stage amplifier, reduces the phase margin and causes instability.

To address this, I restructured the LC interstage network to eliminate the direct feedback path between the stages. Instead of using an LC network from the drain of the first stage to the gate of the second stage, I replaced it with a capacitive coupling (C2) directly from the drain of the first stage to the source of the second stage. This change removes the resonant feedback path and improves stability by reducing the phase shift between stages. The second-stage drain load is now a direct inductor (L3) to VDD, simplifying the load and reducing parasitic interactions. Additionally, I removed the resistor R3 from the second-stage gate biasing and replaced it with a direct connection from the bias node (n7) to the gate of the second stage, reducing the number of passive components and improving signal integrity.
