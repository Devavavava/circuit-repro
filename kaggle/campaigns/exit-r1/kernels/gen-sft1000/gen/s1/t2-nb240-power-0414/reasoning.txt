The binding constraint `mu_min_wide_0.01-50GHz` is failing due to poor stability across the wide frequency band. The original topology uses an LC interstage (L2 and C2) between the two stages, which introduces a resonant peak at the operating frequency. While this improves gain and noise figure at f0, it also creates a narrow stability band and a strong resonance that degrades stability at off-band frequencies. The current mirror biasing and lack of damping in the interstage network exacerbate this issue, leading to a negative stability margin over the wide band.

To address this, I replaced the LC interstage with a series inductor (L2) directly connecting the first stage's drain (n2) to the second stage's gate (n5), effectively removing the capacitive coupling that caused the resonant peak. This change broadens the stability band by eliminating the LC resonance and reduces the feedback that destabilizes the circuit at off-band frequencies. Additionally, I removed the current mirror biasing network and replaced it with a simpler resistor divider (R1 and NM3) to bias the second stage, reducing the complexity and feedback paths that could contribute to instability. The DC blocking capacitor C3 is now placed directly between the second stage's drain and the output, ensuring a clean signal path without introducing additional resonances.
