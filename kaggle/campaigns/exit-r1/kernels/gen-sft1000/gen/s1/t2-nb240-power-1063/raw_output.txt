

The binding constraint `mu_min_wide_0.01-50GHz` is failing due to poor stability across the wide frequency range, likely caused by the LC interstage and drain loads introducing resonant peaks and phase shifts that reduce the stability margin. The two-stage structure with LC tuning, while effective for narrowband gain and noise figure, does not inherently suppress unwanted oscillations or feedback paths across the entire band of interest, especially when the interstage LC network couples back into the input or output ports. This is exacerbated by the lack of direct feedback suppression or additional damping elements in the current topology.

To address this, I propose a structural change that introduces a direct feedback suppression path from the output to the second stage's source, using a capacitor and inductor in parallel. This forms a low-impedance path at high frequencies, reducing unwanted feedback and stabilizing the system over the wide band. Additionally, the first stage's source is connected to an inductor, which helps isolate it from the interstage LC network, reducing parasitic coupling that could destabilize the system.

```netlist
C C1 VIN1 n0
L L1 n0 n1
NMOS NM1 n2 n1 n3 VSS
L L2 n3 VSS
NMOS NM2 n4 VDD n2 VSS
L L3 VDD n4
C C2 n4 VOUT1
R R1 VDD n5
NMOS NM3 n5 n5 VSS VSS
R R2 n5 n1
C ZC1 VOUT1 VSS
L ZL1 VOUT1 n2
```
