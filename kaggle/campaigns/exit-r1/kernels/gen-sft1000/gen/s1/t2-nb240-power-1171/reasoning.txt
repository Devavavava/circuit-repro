The binding constraint is the stability factor `mu_min_wide_0.01-50GHz`, which is negative, indicating instability outside the narrowband center frequency. This is caused by the LC interstage network (L2 and C2) creating a resonant feedback path that introduces a second pole in the frequency response, reducing stability margins across the wideband range. The two-stage structure with tuned loads and LC interstage coupling is inherently prone to instability at off-center frequencies due to the resonant coupling between stages, which the sizing tool could not fully compensate for.

To address this, I restructured the circuit to eliminate the resonant LC interstage coupling, which is the root cause of the instability. Instead of using an LC network between the stages, I replaced it with a direct connection between the first and second stage, using a capacitor (C2) to block DC while allowing AC signals to pass. This change reduces the resonant feedback and improves stability across the wideband range. Additionally, I reorganized the second stage to use a common-gate configuration (NM2) to improve output impedance and reduce Miller effect, while keeping the first stage as a common-source configuration (NM1) for high gain. The biasing network was preserved but simplified to maintain low noise and stable operation.
