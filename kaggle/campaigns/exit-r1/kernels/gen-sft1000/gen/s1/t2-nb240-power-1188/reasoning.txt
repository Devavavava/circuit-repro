The binding constraint is the noise figure (nf_db), which is slightly above the specification limit. This suggests that the noise contributions from the transistors and passive components are not sufficiently suppressed. The current-reuse topology in the anchor netlist may be introducing noise due to the shared DC path and the diode-connected NMOS/PMOS transistors used for biasing. Additionally, the presence of multiple resistive elements (R1, R2, R4, R5) and the lack of direct coupling from the input to the output may be contributing to noise degradation.

To address this, I propose a structural change that simplifies the biasing network and improves the signal path. The new topology eliminates the diode-connected transistors (NM3, PM2) and resistive biasing elements (R1, R2, R4, R5) and replaces them with a direct DC path from the input to the output through an inductor (L3). This reduces noise contributions from the bias network and improves the signal integrity by providing a clearer path for the RF signal. The NMOS and PMOS transistors are reconfigured to form a push-pull amplifier with a direct connection from the input to the output, enhancing the signal gain and reducing noise.
