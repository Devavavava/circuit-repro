The binding constraint is the gain (s21_db), which is insufficient by ~0.25 dB. The two-stage LNA's gain is limited by the interstage LC network (L2 and C2), which acts as a bandpass filter and introduces losses at the operating frequency. This network, while providing good interstage impedance matching, also attenuates the signal due to the series inductor (L2) and the capacitive loading (C2), reducing the overall gain. Additionally, the second stage's tuned load (L3) may not be optimally coupled to the output, further limiting the gain.

To address this, the topology is modified by reconfiguring the interstage network and the output coupling. The LC interstage network is replaced with a direct inductive coupling (L2) from the first stage's source to the second stage's gate, eliminating the capacitive load that caused signal loss. The second stage's drain is connected directly to the output through a capacitor (C2), reducing the impedance mismatch and improving signal transfer. The current mirror biasing is retained but simplified to reduce noise and improve stability. These changes enhance the signal path and reduce losses, thereby improving the gain.
