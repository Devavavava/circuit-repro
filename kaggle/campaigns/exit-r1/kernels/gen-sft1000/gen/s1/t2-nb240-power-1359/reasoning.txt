The binding constraint is the s21_db, which is too low by ~0.79 dB, indicating insufficient gain. The failed anchor netlist features a push-pull current-reuse topology with a shared DC path and a tuned load (L2+C2). This structure likely causes gain degradation due to poor impedance matching and signal coupling between the NMOS and PMOS stages, especially at the target frequency of 2.4 GHz. The shared DC path and the presence of multiple passive components (R2, R5, etc.) may introduce parasitic effects that reduce the signal amplitude at the output.

To address this, I propose a structural change to a common-gate NMOS LNA with a series inductor and a shunt capacitor for impedance transformation. This topology improves gain by providing better input/output impedance matching and reducing signal loss due to parasitic coupling. The removal of the PMOS stage and the current-reuse configuration simplifies the signal path, reducing noise and increasing gain. The series inductor (L2) and shunt capacitor (C2) help in tuning the output impedance, ensuring a higher voltage gain.
