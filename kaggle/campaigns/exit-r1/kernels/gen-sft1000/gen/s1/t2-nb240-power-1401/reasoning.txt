The binding constraint is the stability metric `mu_min_wide_0.01-50GHz`, which measures the stability of the amplifier over a wide frequency range. The failure indicates that the amplifier is marginally stable at some frequencies within this band, likely due to the feedback topology and the load structure. The resistive shunt feedback and the shunt-peaked inductor load in the anchor netlist create a feedback path that introduces phase shifts and may lead to instability at higher frequencies, especially when the amplifier is sized for maximum gain and minimum noise.

To address this, I changed the topology by introducing a common-gate (CG) stage in parallel with the common-source (CS) stage. This is achieved by adding a PMOS transistor (PM1) in a common-gate configuration, which provides a low-impedance path to ground for high-frequency signals, thereby reducing phase shifts and improving stability. The PMOS CG stage also helps in decoupling the feedback network from the output, preventing unwanted feedback loops that can destabilize the amplifier.

Additionally, I added a second inductor (L2) and capacitor (C2) in parallel with the original load inductor (L1) to provide a more controlled impedance at high frequencies, further enhancing stability. The resistive divider (R1, R2) and the self-biasing network (NM3, NM4, R5, R6) were retained but restructured to ensure proper DC biasing without interfering with the stability of the amplifier.

These changes collectively improve the phase margin and reduce the risk of oscillation, particularly at the higher end of the frequency band, thereby addressing the `mu_min_wide_0.01-50GHz` constraint.
