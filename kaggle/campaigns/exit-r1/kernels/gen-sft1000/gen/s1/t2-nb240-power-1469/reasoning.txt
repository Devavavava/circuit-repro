The original topology features a two-stage common-source amplifier with LC tuning at both stages and a current mirror biasing network. While this structure is effective for narrowband gain and noise performance, it lacks sufficient feedback and phase compensation to maintain stability across the full 0.01–50 GHz range.

The root issue lies in the absence of a feedback path from the output to the input or biasing network, which is essential for wideband stability. The current mirror biasing network (NM3, R1, R2, R3) provides local biasing but does not contribute to global stability. The LC interstage tuning (L2, C2) and drain loads (L1, L3) are optimized for narrowband performance but do not inherently suppress oscillations or enhance phase margin across a wide frequency range.

The capacitor ZC1 provides a high-frequency path from VDD to the bias node n7, helping to stabilize the biasing network at higher frequencies. This addition enhances the phase margin and suppresses oscillations across the wideband range, directly improving the `mu_min_wide_0.01-50GHz` metric.
