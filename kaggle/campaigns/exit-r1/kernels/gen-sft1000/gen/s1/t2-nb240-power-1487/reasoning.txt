The binding constraint `mu_min_wide_0.01-50GHz` is failing due to poor stability across a wide frequency range. The original topology, while optimized for narrowband performance at 2.4 GHz, lacks sufficient feedback or impedance control mechanisms to maintain stability outside the narrowband region. The LC interstage and tuned loads provide excellent gain and noise figure at the center frequency, but they do not inherently suppress oscillations or unwanted feedback paths that may emerge at off-center frequencies. This is exacerbated by the absence of a direct feedback path from the output to the input or a mechanism to dampen resonant modes across the wide band.

To address this, I introduce a feedback path from the output to the second stage via an additional NMOS transistor (ZN1) and a resistor (Rrep1) connected to VDD. This creates a controlled feedback mechanism that suppresses unwanted oscillations and improves stability across the entire 0.01–50 GHz range. The new NMOS transistor ZN1 is connected from VDD to n5 (the gate of NM2), with its source tied to VOUT1, effectively creating a feedback loop from the output to the second stage. The resistor Rrep1 connects VDD to n7, providing an additional impedance path that helps stabilize the biasing and reduce parasitic feedback.

These changes increase the stability margin by introducing a controlled feedback path and additional impedance nodes, which dampen resonant effects and suppress oscillations in the wideband regime. This directly improves the `mu_min_wide_0.01-50GHz` metric, addressing the root cause of the failure.
