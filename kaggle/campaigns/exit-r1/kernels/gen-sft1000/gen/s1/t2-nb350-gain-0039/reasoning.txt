The binding constraint is the noise figure (nf_db), which is too high by ~0.6 dB. This is likely due to the resistive shunt feedback and the shunt-peaked load, which introduce noise and degrade the noise performance of the LNA. The resistive feedback (R1) adds noise to the input signal, and the shunt-peaked load (L1+R2) increases the noise contribution from the output node, especially at the operating frequency of 3.5 GHz.

To address this, I redesigned the topology to use a common-gate (CG) stage in parallel with a common-source (CS) stage, which reduces the noise contribution from the feedback network and improves the noise figure. The CG stage provides a low-noise path with a high input impedance, while the CS stage provides gain. The inductor (L1) is now used for impedance transformation and frequency peaking, and the output is coupled through a capacitor (C2) to isolate the load from the active stage. The addition of a second NMOS transistor (NM2) in a current mirror configuration with a resistor (R3) and a capacitor (C1) helps in stabilizing the bias and reducing noise contributions from the current source.
