The failure is due to the insufficient stability margin (mu_min_wide_0.01-50GHz) caused by the poor phase and gain stability of the two-stage amplifier over a wide frequency range. The original topology, with its LC interstage and tuned loads, introduces resonances and phase shifts that lead to instability outside the narrowband design frequency (f0 = 3.5 GHz), despite meeting the narrowband specifications. The current mirror biasing and the LC interstage coupling between the two stages exacerbate this by creating feedback paths that reduce the stability margin.

To address this, I restructured the topology to improve stability and reduce the feedback coupling between stages. I replaced the second stage's configuration from a common-source with an inductive source degeneration (L2) and a common-gate configuration (NM2), which inherently improves stability by reducing the feedback from the output to the input. Additionally, I removed the LC interstage coupling, which was a source of instability, and replaced it with direct coupling through the second stage's source inductor (L2). This change reduces the phase shift and feedback at off-design frequencies, improving the stability margin. The biasing network was also restructured to isolate the second stage's biasing from the first stage, further reducing coupling and improving stability.
