The failure is due to insufficient gain in the LNA, as evidenced by the s21_db margin being -0.726569, which indicates that the achieved gain (6.85897 dB) is far below the required minimum of 25.0848 dB. This is likely caused by the current-reuse topology and the shared DC path, which may introduce parasitic effects and limit the signal swing, especially at the narrowband frequency of 3.5 GHz. The use of a shared DC path (through n3) and the current mirror (NM3, R1, R2) may also lead to poor impedance matching and signal degradation.

To address this, I propose a structural change that isolates the signal path from the DC biasing network and eliminates the shared DC path. This is achieved by removing the shared node n3 and instead using a separate node n7 for the PMOS load (PM1). This isolates the signal path and reduces parasitic coupling, improving gain and impedance matching. Additionally, a new inductor L3 is added between VSS and n5 to provide a better AC ground and reduce noise coupling.
