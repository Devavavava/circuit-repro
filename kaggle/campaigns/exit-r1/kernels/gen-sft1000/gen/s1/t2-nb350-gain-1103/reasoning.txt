This instability is likely caused by the LC interstage network (L2 and C2), which introduces resonant peaks and phase shifts that reduce stability margins at off-band frequencies. The current topology's LC interstage and tuned loads may create feedback paths that destabilize the circuit beyond the narrowband.

To address this, I propose a structural change that replaces the LC interstage with a direct connection between the two stages, using a resistor (ZR2) and an inductor (ZL1) in the source of the first stage (NM1) to provide a more stable impedance match and suppress unwanted feedback. Additionally, a capacitor (ZC2) is added between the drain of NM1 and the LC load (L1) to filter out high-frequency noise and stabilize the first stage. A resistor (ZR3) is added between the output and ground to dampen oscillations at off-band frequencies.
