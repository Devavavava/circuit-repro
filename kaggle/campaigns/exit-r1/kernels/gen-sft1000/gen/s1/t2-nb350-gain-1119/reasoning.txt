The failure is due to the poor stability margin (mu_min_wide_0.01-50GHz) caused by insufficient isolation and feedback suppression in the interstage network. The original topology uses a series inductor (L2) and a DC-block capacitor (C2) between stages, which may introduce unwanted resonances and feedback paths across a wide frequency range. This leads to a drop in the stability factor (mu) outside the narrowband center frequency (f0), causing the binding constraint to fail.

To address this, I replaced the interstage LC network with a shunt inductor (L2) and a capacitor (C2) connected to the source of the second stage (NM2), effectively creating a source-degeneration inductor. This change improves the isolation between stages and suppresses feedback by increasing the impedance seen at the source of NM2, thereby enhancing the stability margin across the wideband range. Additionally, I removed the unnecessary resistor (R1) and diode-connected transistor (NM3) bias network, simplifying the topology and reducing parasitic interactions that could degrade stability.
