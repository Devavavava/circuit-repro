The binding constraint, `mu_min_wide_0.01-50GHz`, measures the minimum stability factor over a wide frequency range. A negative margin indicates instability outside the target frequency band, which is critical for a narrowband LNA. The failure suggests that the current topology lacks sufficient feedback or damping at off-band frequencies, leading to potential oscillation or poor stability.

The original anchor netlist uses a two-stage tuned common-source LNA with LC interstage coupling. While this structure is effective for narrowband gain and noise figure, it lacks a mechanism to suppress instability at frequencies beyond the narrowband center. The absence of a feedback or damping element at the output or interstage could be the root cause of the instability.

To address this, I added a feedback resistor `ZR1` from the output node `VOUT1` to ground, and a resistor `ZR2` from the second stage's internal node `n5` to the bias node `n7`. These resistors provide additional damping at off-band frequencies, improving stability without significantly degrading gain or noise figure. The resistor `ZR3` between `VDD` and the interstage node `n4` further stabilizes the interstage LC network by reducing parasitic resonances.
