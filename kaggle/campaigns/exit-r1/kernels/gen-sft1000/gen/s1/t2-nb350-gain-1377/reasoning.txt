The binding constraint is the stability factor `mu_min_wide_0.01-50GHz`, which measures the stability of the LNA across a wide frequency range. The negative margin indicates that the circuit is unstable at some frequencies outside the design band, likely due to the lack of feedback or damping mechanisms in the current topology. The two-stage structure with LC loads and no feedback introduces resonances that can lead to instability, especially at frequencies near the interstage resonance or harmonics of the operating frequency.

To address this, I added a feedback path from the output to the second stage's source, using an NMOS transistor (ZN1) and a capacitor (ZC1) to ground. This feedback introduces a damping effect, improving the stability of the circuit by reducing the gain at higher frequencies and suppressing unwanted resonances. Additionally, I added a capacitor (ZC2) from the input node to the first stage's drain to help stabilize the input impedance and reduce coupling between stages, further enhancing stability.

These changes add structural feedback and damping without altering the core two-stage LC-tuned topology, directly addressing the stability limitations while maintaining the narrowband gain and noise figure performance.
