The failure is primarily due to noise figure (nf_max_db) being too high, exceeding the specification by ~0.07 dB. This suggests that the noise contribution from the transistors or the biasing network is not sufficiently suppressed. The current-reuse topology in the anchor netlist may be introducing additional noise due to the diode-connected NMOS (NM3) and PMOS (PM2) used for biasing, which can act as noise sources, especially at high frequencies. Additionally, the shared DC path and the lack of a dedicated low-noise biasing scheme may be contributing to the noise floor.

To address this, I restructured the biasing network to isolate the noise-sensitive components from the signal path. The new topology replaces the diode-connected NM3 with a NMOS transistor (NM3) connected to a separate bias node (n5), which is driven by a resistor (R1) from VDD and a capacitor (C2) to VSS. This isolates the bias network from the signal path, reducing noise coupling. The PMOS biasing network is also restructured, with PM2 connected to a dedicated bias node (n6) and a resistor (R4) to VSS. This change reduces the noise contribution from the bias network and improves the signal-to-noise ratio.

The addition of a resistor (Rrep1) between the internal node (n5) and the signal node (n3) provides a controlled path for bias current, ensuring stable operation while minimizing noise coupling. The capacitor (C ZC1) between n5 and n6 helps filter high-frequency noise from the bias network, further improving the noise figure.
