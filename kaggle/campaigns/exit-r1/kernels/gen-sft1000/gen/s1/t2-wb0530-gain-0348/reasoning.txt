The failure is primarily due to the insufficient stability margin (`mu_min_wide_0.01-50GHz`) caused by poor feedback and impedance matching in the current-reuse topology. The anchor netlist uses a shared DC path and lacks direct feedback from the output to the input stage, leading to a narrow stability band and a marginal phase margin at the target frequency. The absence of a direct feedback path and the use of a shared DC path increase the risk of instability, especially over a wideband.

To address this, I added a direct feedback path from the output to the input stage using an NMOS transistor (ZN1) and a resistor (Rrep1) connected from an internal node (n7) to the drain of NM3. This creates a local feedback loop that improves phase margin and enhances stability. Additionally, I introduced a resistor (Rrep1) to provide a controlled impedance path for feedback, ensuring that the feedback signal is properly attenuated and phase-shifted to prevent oscillation. These changes enhance the stability margin and widen the operating bandwidth, directly addressing the binding constraint.
