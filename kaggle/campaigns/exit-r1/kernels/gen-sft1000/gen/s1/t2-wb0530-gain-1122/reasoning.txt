The current-reuse topology in the anchor netlist uses a diode-connected NMOS (NM3) and a PMOS (PM2) for biasing, which may introduce noise due to their non-ideal behavior and lack of noise suppression. The noise floor is likely elevated due to the biasing network's structure and the lack of noise-shaping elements near the input.

To address this, I replaced the diode-connected NM3 with a current source (NM3) driven by a current mirror (NM2), which provides a more stable and lower-noise bias current. This change reduces the noise contribution from the biasing network by decoupling the bias current from the input signal path and providing a cleaner reference. Additionally, I added a resistor (Rrep1) between the bias node (n5) and the output of the current mirror (n6), which helps in stabilizing the bias current and further reducing noise coupling into the signal path. These structural changes improve the noise performance by isolating the bias network from the input and ensuring a more predictable and lower-noise current source.
