The failure is primarily due to noise figure (NF) being too high, exceeding the maximum allowed value of 2.1159 dB. The anchor netlist uses a current-reuse push-pull topology with a shared DC path and a tuned load (L2+C2), which may introduce noise due to the shared node and the complexity of the feedback paths. The diode-connected NMOS (NM3) and PMOS (PM2) in the bias network may also contribute to noise through their nonlinear behavior and current mismatch. The lack of a dedicated noise-suppressing node or a more direct signal path likely exacerbates the noise floor, especially at the high-frequency band (1.22 GHz).

To address this, I replaced the shared DC path with a more direct and isolated signal flow by introducing a dedicated NMOS (NM2) and PMOS (PM2) stage with separate load networks (L2 and L3), ensuring that the noise contributions from the biasing network are decoupled from the signal path. The new NMOS (NM2) and PMOS (PM2) stages provide a cleaner current path and reduce the noise coupling from the biasing network. Additionally, the removal of the diode-connected NM3 and PM2 in the bias network simplifies the feedback path and reduces noise sources. The new load networks (L2 and L3) are tuned to the operating frequency, improving impedance matching and reducing noise contributions from the load.
