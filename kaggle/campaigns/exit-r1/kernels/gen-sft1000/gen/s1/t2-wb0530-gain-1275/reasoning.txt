The failure is primarily due to noise figure (nf_max_db) being too high, exceeding the specification by ~0.17 dB. This is likely caused by the noise contribution from the input transistor NM1 and the current-reuse path, which introduces additional noise due to the diode-connected NM3 and the PMOS current mirror PM2. The noise from these components is amplified and not sufficiently suppressed by the current-reuse structure, especially at the operating frequency of ~1.22 GHz. Additionally, the shared DC path and the lack of a dedicated noise-suppressing element at the input node may be contributing to the elevated noise floor.

To address this, I added a noise-suppressing capacitor ZC1 between the internal node n5 (the gate of NM3) and the node x1, which is connected to the drain of NM1. This capacitor provides a low-impedance path for high-frequency noise at the input stage, effectively shunting it to ground and reducing the noise contribution from the current-reuse path. This change isolates the noise-sensitive nodes and improves the noise performance without significantly affecting the gain or stability.
