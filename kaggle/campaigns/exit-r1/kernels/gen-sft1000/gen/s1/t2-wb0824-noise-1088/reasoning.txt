The failure is primarily due to the noise figure (nf_max_db) being too high, which is likely caused by the insufficient impedance transformation and poor noise contribution from the current-reuse structure. The current-reuse topology, while effective for gain and power efficiency, can introduce noise due to the additional transistors and resistive components in the signal path. The noise contribution from the NMOS and PMOS transistors in the current-reuse path, along with the resistive components (R1, R2, R4, R5), may be elevating the noise floor beyond the specification.

To address this, I propose a structural change that simplifies the current-reuse path and reduces the number of noise contributors. By removing the current mirror (NM3, R1, R2) and the PMOS current-setting path (PM2, R4, R5), and instead using a direct connection from the signal node (n1) to the gate of the NMOS transistor (NM1), the signal path becomes more direct and less noisy. This change eliminates the additional transistors and resistors that contribute to noise, while maintaining the current-reuse benefit through the shared load network (L2, C2). The removal of the PMOS current mirror also reduces the complexity of the biasing network, which further helps in lowering the noise floor.
