The failure is due to insufficient stability margin (`mu_min_wide_0.01-50GHz`) caused by poor phase compensation in the feedback loop of the shunt-feedback LNA. The shunt-peaked inductor (L1) and resistive feedback (R1) provide limited phase boost at high frequencies, leading to instability in the wideband range. The single-stage common-source topology with resistive feedback lacks sufficient phase margin to meet the stability constraint across the 0.01–50 GHz band.

To address this, I introduce a second-stage inductive peaking and a feedback capacitor to enhance phase compensation and stabilize the loop. The additional inductor (L2) and capacitor (C2) create a second pole-zero pair, which boosts phase at high frequencies and improves stability. The second NMOS transistor (NM2) acts as a current buffer, reducing the feedback loop's sensitivity to load variations and improving the stability margin.
