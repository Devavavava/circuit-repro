The failure is primarily due to the noise figure (NF) being too high, exceeding the maximum allowed value of 2.0175 dB. The noise performance is dominated by the input transistor (NM1) and the biasing network, particularly the diode-connected NMOS (NM3) and the resistive divider (R1-R2). The current-reuse structure and the shared DC path may be causing excessive noise contributions from the biasing network and the output stage, especially at the target frequency. Additionally, the lack of a dedicated noise-shaping element at the input and the absence of a direct DC path from the output to ground may contribute to the elevated noise floor.

To address this, I introduced a direct DC path from the output (VOUT1) to ground (VSS) via a resistor (ZR1), which helps stabilize the output and reduce noise coupling from the output stage back to the input. This change improves the noise performance by decoupling the output node from the sensitive input node and providing a low-impedance path for noise currents. The addition of ZR1 also helps in maintaining a stable DC operating point, which is crucial for low-noise operation.
