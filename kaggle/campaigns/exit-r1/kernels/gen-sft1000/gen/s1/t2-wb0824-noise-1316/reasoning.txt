The failure is primarily due to noise performance limitations in the original topology. The noise figure (nf_max_db) is too high, exceeding the specification by approximately 0.3 dB. This is likely caused by the noise contribution from the current-reuse structure and the shared DC path, which introduces additional noise sources and degrades the signal-to-noise ratio. Additionally, the current mirror and biasing network contribute noise that is amplified in the gain stage.

To address this, I propose a structural change that eliminates the current-reuse configuration and replaces it with a simpler common-source topology with an inductive load. This reduces the number of noise-generating components and minimizes the impact of biasing networks on the noise figure. The removal of the current mirror and the use of a single-stage amplifier with an inductive load improve the noise performance by reducing the number of active components contributing noise and simplifying the signal path.
