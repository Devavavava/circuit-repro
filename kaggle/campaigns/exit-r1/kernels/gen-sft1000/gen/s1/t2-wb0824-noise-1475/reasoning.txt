The binding constraint is the minimum phase margin (mu_min_wide_0.01-50GHz), which is failing due to insufficient phase margin across the wideband frequency range. This is likely caused by the single-stage common-source (CS) topology with resistive shunt feedback, which introduces phase lag at higher frequencies and limits the stability margin. The shunt-peaked inductor (L1) and resistor (R2) load may not provide enough phase boost over the entire band, especially at the lower end of the frequency range.

To address this, I replaced the single-stage resistive shunt-feedback CS LNA with a two-stage topology that includes a common-gate (CG) stage for improved phase response. The CG stage (PM1 and NM3) introduces a phase lead at higher frequencies, enhancing the stability margin. Additionally, the addition of a second inductor (L2) and a feedback capacitor (C2) helps shape the frequency response to maintain a sufficient phase margin across the band. The inclusion of a second NMOS transistor (NM2) and a bias resistor (R1) provides better control over the DC operating point and improves linearity. These structural changes collectively increase the phase margin across the wideband range, addressing the failure in the binding constraint.
