The failure is due to the insufficient stability margin (mu_min_wide_0.01-50GHz) caused by the feedback topology and load configuration in the anchor netlist. The resistive shunt feedback and shunt-peaked inductor load create a narrow stability band, which fails to meet the wideband stability requirement. The feedback resistor R1 and the inductor L1 in the load contribute to phase shifts that reduce the stability margin across the wideband frequency range.

To address this, I introduce a common-gate (CG) stage in parallel with the main common-source (CS) amplifier. This CG stage provides additional phase boost at high frequencies, thereby increasing the stability margin. The CG stage is implemented using a PMOS transistor (PM1) with a source degeneration inductor (L2) and a feedback capacitor (C2) to the output. This configuration adds a low-impedance path to ground at high frequencies, reducing the phase shift and improving stability. Additionally, the CG stage helps in maintaining a low noise figure by providing a higher transconductance contribution from the PMOS transistor. The overall topology becomes more robust against phase shifts across the wideband frequency range, ensuring the stability margin meets the specification.
