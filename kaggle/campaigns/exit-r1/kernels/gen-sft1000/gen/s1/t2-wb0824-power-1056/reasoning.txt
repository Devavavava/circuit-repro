The failure is primarily due to the noise figure (nf_max_db) being too high, exceeding the specification by ~0.085 dB. This is likely caused by the noise contributions from the biasing network and the current-reuse structure, particularly the NMOS and PMOS diodes used for biasing. These diodes add significant noise, especially at the high-frequency operating point (f0 ≈ 1.39 GHz), which degrades the noise performance of the LNA. Additionally, the current-reuse structure, while beneficial for power efficiency, may not be optimally configured for noise minimization in this wideband context.

To address this, I propose a structural change that replaces the diode-connected NMOS and PMOS biasing network with a simpler and more noise-efficient configuration. Specifically, the NMOS diode (NM3) is replaced with a direct connection to the bias node (n5), and the PMOS diode (PM2) is removed, with its biasing handled through a direct connection to VDD. This change reduces the noise contribution from the bias network and simplifies the current-reuse structure, improving the noise figure while maintaining the desired performance in other metrics.
