The failure is due to the insufficient stability margin (mu_min_wide_0.01-50GHz) caused by the single-stage common-source (CS) topology with resistive shunt feedback. This configuration leads to a narrow stability band, especially at lower frequencies, because the feedback loop's phase and gain characteristics are not sufficiently controlled across the wideband. The shunt-peaked inductor and resistor load (L1+R2) help with bandwidth but do not address the stability issues effectively over the full 0.01–50 GHz range.

To improve stability, I introduce a second-stage common-gate (CG) amplifier with an inductor (L2) and a self-biased PMOS transistor (PM1) to provide a low-impedance path to ground and increase phase margin. This CG stage adds a second pole-zero pair that helps stabilize the feedback loop and widen the stability band. Additionally, a PMOS current source (PM2) with a resistor (R4) and a self-biased NMOS (NM3) ensures a stable bias point and reduces the impact of process variations on the feedback loop. The additional capacitors (C3 and C1) help decouple and isolate the feedback network, further improving phase and gain characteristics over the wideband.
