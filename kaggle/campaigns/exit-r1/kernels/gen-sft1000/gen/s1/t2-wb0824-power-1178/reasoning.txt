The failure is due to the noise figure (nf_max_db) being too high, which is primarily caused by the noise contribution from the input transistor NM1 and the biasing network. The current-reuse topology, while efficient in power, introduces additional noise paths through the biasing structure (NM3, R1, R2, etc.), which degrade the noise performance. The noise from the biasing network is coupled into the signal path, especially at the high-frequency operating point (f0 ≈ 1.38 GHz), leading to an increase in the overall noise figure.

To address this, the biasing network must be decoupled from the signal path to reduce noise coupling. In the revised netlist, the NM3 transistor is connected to a new internal node x1, which is tied to the drain of PM2 instead of VSS. This change isolates the biasing network from the signal path, reducing noise contributions from the biasing structure. Additionally, a new PMOS transistor ZP1 is introduced to provide a low-noise path for the bias current, further decoupling the signal and bias paths. The new resistor ZR1 connects the internal node x1 to the drain of NM1, providing a stable bias point without introducing noise into the signal path.
