

The failure is primarily due to noise figure (nf_db) being too high, exceeding the specification by ~0.17 dB. This suggests that the noise contribution from the input transistor NM1 is not sufficiently suppressed, possibly due to inadequate impedance matching or poor noise performance from the current-reuse structure. The current-reuse topology, while beneficial for power efficiency, may introduce additional noise or degrade the noise performance if not carefully designed. In this case, the NMOS gate biasing and the shared DC path may be contributing to higher noise, particularly at the input node.

To address this, I replaced the original topology with a fully differential LNA structure. This change introduces a more symmetric and balanced signal path, which helps in suppressing noise and improving impedance matching at the input. The addition of a second NMOS transistor (NM2) and a new node (n6) allows for better noise cancellation and improved signal linearity. The differential configuration also helps in rejecting common-mode noise, which is beneficial for achieving a lower noise figure. The revised topology includes a more direct and isolated DC path for the input signal, reducing the impact of noise from the bias network on the input transistor. The change in the load configuration (from a shared inductor-capacitor network to a more isolated inductor L3) also contributes to better noise performance and stability.
