The failure is primarily due to insufficient phase margin and stability in the feedback loop, which limits the stability factor (mu_min_wide_0.01-50GHz). The original topology uses a resistive shunt feedback and a shunt-peaked inductive load, which, while effective for broadband matching and gain, may not provide sufficient phase compensation across the entire 0.01–50 GHz range. This leads to a negative stability margin at the band edges, causing instability and failure of the mu_min constraint.

To address this, I introduce a second NMOS transistor (NM2) in a common-source configuration with a separate inductive load (L2), which adds an additional feedback path and enhances phase compensation. This change introduces a second stage of amplification and feedback, improving the phase margin and stabilizing the circuit over a wider frequency range. The addition of a resistor (R4) from VDD to the drain of NM1 further helps in balancing the feedback and reducing unwanted oscillations. The new topology ensures a more robust stability margin while maintaining the desired gain and noise figure.
