The binding constraint, `mu_min_wide_0.01-50GHz`, measures the stability of the LNA over a wide frequency range. A negative margin indicates instability, likely due to poor phase or gain margin at frequencies outside the design band. The original circuit's interstage LC network and drain loads may be causing resonances or feedback paths that reduce stability at off-band frequencies. This is exacerbated by the current-mirror biasing network, which introduces additional feedback and may destabilize the system at higher frequencies.

To address this, I redesigned the topology to reduce feedback paths and stabilize the system. The new netlist eliminates the interstage LC network and reconfigures the drain loads to be more passive and less resonant. The second stage is now a common-gate configuration, which inherently improves stability by reducing feedback from output to input. Additionally, the removal of the current-mirror bias network and its associated resistors simplifies the feedback structure and reduces the risk of instability at off-band frequencies. The revised layout ensures that the gain and phase margins are maintained across the entire frequency range, thus improving the `mu_min_wide_0.01-50GHz` metric.
