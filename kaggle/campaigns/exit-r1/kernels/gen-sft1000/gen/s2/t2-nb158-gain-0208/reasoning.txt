The failure is due to insufficient stability margin (mu_min_wide_0.01-50GHz) caused by the feedback and load configuration in the original circuit. The resistive shunt feedback and shunt-peaked inductor load create a narrow stability band, leading to instability at frequencies outside the narrowband center frequency (f0). The feedback resistor (R1) introduces a phase shift that, combined with the inductor (L1) and load resistor (R2), reduces the phase margin across the wide frequency range, causing the mu_min to fall below the required threshold.

To address this, I replaced the resistive shunt feedback with a current-reuse topology using a second NMOS transistor (NM2) and a current mirror (PM2, R1, NM3, PM1, R4, R5) to provide a more stable and predictable feedback path. This topology improves the phase margin by reducing the feedback loop's sensitivity to frequency variations. Additionally, I added a second inductor (L2) and capacitor (C2) to the output to further stabilize the feedback and improve the impedance matching across the band. The use of a PMOS transistor (PM2) in the current mirror ensures a more stable bias current, reducing the impact of process variations on the stability metric.
