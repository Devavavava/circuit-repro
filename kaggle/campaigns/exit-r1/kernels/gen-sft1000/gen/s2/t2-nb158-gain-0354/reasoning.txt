The binding constraint is the mu_min_wide_0.01-50GHz, which measures the stability margin of the LNA across a wide frequency range. The negative margin indicates that the circuit is unstable at certain frequencies outside the narrowband center frequency (f0 = 1.575 GHz), likely due to poor phase and gain behavior in the feedback paths, particularly from the second stage. The two-stage structure with LC loads and interstage coupling introduces resonant feedback that may not be sufficiently damped, leading to instability at off-band frequencies.

To address this, I restructured the circuit to improve stability by reducing feedback paths that contribute to instability and enhancing damping. The original topology had a direct LC load on the second stage's drain, which could resonate with parasitic capacitances and create a feedback path that destabilizes the circuit at off-band frequencies. I replaced this with a source-degeneration inductor (L2) in the second stage, which helps dampen unwanted resonances and improves stability by increasing the phase margin. Additionally, I reconnected the LC interstage network to the source of the second stage rather than its gate, reducing direct feedback and improving isolation. The biasing network was also restructured to ensure more controlled and stable gate biasing without introducing additional feedback paths.
