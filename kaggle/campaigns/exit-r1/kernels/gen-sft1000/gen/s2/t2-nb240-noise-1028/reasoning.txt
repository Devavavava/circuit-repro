The failure is driven by the `mu_min_wide_0.01-50GHz` constraint, which measures the stability margin of the LNA across a wide frequency band. The negative margin indicates that the circuit is unstable outside the narrowband center frequency (f0), likely due to the LC interstage network (L2 and C2) and the lack of a direct feedback path to suppress oscillations at off-band frequencies. The LC interstage introduces resonances that can lead to phase shifts and instability when the frequency deviates from f0.

To address this, I added a feedback path from the output (VOUT1) to the gate of the first stage (NM1) through a series inductor (ZL1) and a capacitor (ZC1), and from the output to the source of the second stage (NM2) through a resistor (ZR1). This creates a low-frequency feedback path that stabilizes the circuit across the wideband range, reducing the risk of oscillation and improving the stability margin. The inductor ZL1 and capacitor ZC1 provide a high-impedance path at f0, ensuring minimal impact on the narrowband performance, while the resistor ZR1 introduces a controlled feedback mechanism that enhances stability without degrading gain or noise performance.
