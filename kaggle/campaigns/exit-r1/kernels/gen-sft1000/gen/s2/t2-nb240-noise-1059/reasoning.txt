The binding constraint is the stability metric `mu_min_wide_0.01-50GHz`, which measures the stability of the LNA over a wide frequency range. The failure indicates that the circuit is unstable outside the narrowband operating frequency (f0=2400 MHz), likely due to the interstage LC network and the feedback paths introduced by the biasing network. The current structure has a direct feedback path through the bias network (R1, NM3, R2, R3), which can cause instability at off-band frequencies. Additionally, the interstage LC network (L2, C2) may resonate with parasitic capacitances and inductances, creating unintended feedback paths that reduce the stability margin.

To address this, I restructured the biasing network to eliminate direct feedback paths from the bias network to the amplifier stages. I replaced the current mirror bias network with a simpler configuration where the bias is applied directly to the second stage via a resistor (R2) from the bias node (n7) to the source of the second stage (n5). This reduces the number of feedback paths and minimizes the impact of the bias network on the stability of the system. I also removed the inductor L2 and capacitor C2 from the interstage network and replaced them with a direct connection from the drain of the first stage (n2) to the gate of the second stage (n6), which simplifies the feedback structure and reduces the potential for resonance-induced instability.
