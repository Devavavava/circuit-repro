The binding constraint is the s21_db, which is too low by ~0.74 dB. This indicates a poor gain at the operating frequency. The failure is likely due to the current-reuse topology's limited ability to amplify signals at 2.4 GHz, exacerbated by the shared DC path and the tuned load's impedance mismatch at the target frequency. The push-pull configuration may also be causing signal cancellation or phase shifts that degrade gain.

To address this, I propose a structural change to the LNA topology by removing the current-reuse configuration and instead using a single-stage common-source amplifier with an inductive source degeneration and a capacitive load. This configuration provides better gain and stability at high frequencies. The PMOS and NMOS transistors are reconfigured to be in a common-source configuration, with the NMOS transistor's source connected to an inductor, improving the gain and reducing noise. The capacitive load helps in achieving a better impedance match at the operating frequency.
