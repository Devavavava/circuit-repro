The failure is primarily due to the insufficient gain of the LNA, as evidenced by the binding constraint on s21_db, which is 5.95898 dB (well below the required 21.4913 dB). The current-reuse topology in the anchor netlist, while effective for noise and power, is not optimized for gain in this configuration. The push-pull structure and the tuned load (L2+C2) may be causing signal loss or insufficient amplification at the desired frequency, especially due to the shared DC path and the complex biasing setup.

To address this, I replaced the push-pull structure with a single-stage common-gate (CG) configuration, which is known for its high gain and good input matching in low-noise applications. The NMOS NM2 is connected in a CG configuration with its source tied to the inductor L1, which helps in boosting the gain. Additionally, the load inductor L3 and capacitor C2 provide a high-impedance load at the operating frequency, enhancing the gain and improving the output impedance. The removal of the PMOS and the current-reuse structure simplifies the biasing and eliminates potential signal degradation from the shared DC path. This structural change directly improves the gain by leveraging the CG topology's advantages, which should significantly increase the s21_db to meet the specification.
