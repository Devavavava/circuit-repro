The binding constraint is the gain (s21_db), which is too low by ~0.72 dB. The current-reuse push-pull configuration in the anchor netlist introduces significant inter-stage coupling and impedance mismatches, particularly between the NMOS and PMOS stages, which degrade the gain and stability. The shared DC path and the use of a current mirror for biasing add parasitic effects and reduce the signal transfer efficiency at the operating frequency. Additionally, the tuned load (L2+C2) is not optimally integrated with the signal path, leading to poor impedance matching and reduced gain.

To address this, I propose a structural change to a common-gate (CG) LNA topology with a cascode configuration. This topology improves gain by reducing the Miller effect and enhancing the output impedance. The CG stage provides a low input impedance, which helps in matching the input signal, while the cascode stage increases the output impedance and suppresses unwanted feedback. This configuration also avoids the shared DC path and current mirror biasing, reducing parasitic effects and improving signal integrity.
