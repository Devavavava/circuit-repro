The failure is due to the poor stability margin (mu_min_wide_0.01-50GHz), which is negative, indicating instability over a wide frequency range. The original topology has a two-stage common-source amplifier with LC resonant loads and a current mirror biasing scheme. However, the feedback path from the second stage to the first stage, through the interstage LC network and the current mirror, introduces a phase shift that reduces the phase margin, especially at frequencies outside the narrowband center frequency (f0=2.4 GHz). This causes the stability factor (mu) to drop below the required minimum, leading to oscillation or excessive gain peaking.

To address this, I redesigned the topology to reduce the feedback path's impact on stability. The new structure replaces the second stage's common-source configuration with a common-gate configuration (NM2), which inherently has a lower output impedance and reduces the Miller effect, improving phase margin. The interstage LC network is repositioned to provide a more controlled impedance transformation, reducing unwanted feedback. Additionally, the current mirror is reconfigured to bias the second stage directly from the interstage node, reducing the number of feedback loops and stabilizing the system.
