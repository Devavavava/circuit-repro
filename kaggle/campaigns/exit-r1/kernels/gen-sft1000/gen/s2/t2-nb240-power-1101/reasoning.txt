The binding constraint is the stability metric `mu_min_wide_0.01-50GHz`, which is failing due to poor stability across the wide frequency range. The original topology uses a current mirror bias network with resistive feedback to both stages, which introduces a feedback path that can resonate with the inductors in the LC tank circuits and interstage network. This resonance degrades the stability margin at off-band frequencies, particularly in the wideband range specified by the constraint. The LC interstage network (L2 and C2) and the tuned drain loads (L1 and L3) interact with the current mirror feedback, creating parasitic resonances that lower the stability margin.

To address this, the bias network is restructured to remove the resistive feedback from the second stage (NM2), which reduces the feedback-induced resonance. Instead, the bias is delivered directly to the first stage (NM1) through a separate current mirror (NM3 and R1), and the second stage is biased through a direct connection to the first stage's bias node (n2). This change minimizes the number of feedback paths and isolates the second stage from the LC resonances, improving the stability margin across the wideband range.
