The failure is primarily due to the inadequate gain of the amplifier, as evidenced by the s21_db metric being 11.0595 dB, which is 5.2378 dB below the required 16.2973 dB. This indicates that the transconductance and output impedance of the amplifier are not sufficient to achieve the desired gain at the operating frequency of 2.4 GHz. The current-reuse topology, while effective in reducing power consumption, may not provide enough gain in this configuration, especially due to the shared DC path and the tuned load (L2+C2), which may not be optimally coupled with the amplifier's output stage.

To address this, I propose a structural change that replaces the current-reuse push-pull configuration with a single-stage common-gate NMOS amplifier with an inductive source degeneration. This topology enhances the gain by increasing the transconductance and improving the output impedance. The inductive source degeneration (L3) helps to increase the effective transconductance of the NMOS transistor (NM2) by reducing the source resistance at high frequencies, thereby boosting the gain. Additionally, the common-gate configuration inherently provides a low input impedance and high output impedance, which is beneficial for achieving a higher gain and better matching at the input.

The removal of the PMOS push-pull stage and the tuned load (L2+C2) simplifies the circuit and reduces parasitic effects that may be degrading the gain. The addition of the inductive source degeneration (L3) and the reconfiguration of the NMOS stage (NM2) to a common-gate topology directly address the gain limitation by improving the transconductance and output impedance.
