The binding constraint is the minimum stability factor (mu_min_wide_0.01-50GHz), which is failing due to poor stability across the wide frequency range. The original topology, while effective at the center frequency (f0=3.5 GHz), lacks sufficient feedback and damping mechanisms to suppress oscillations and maintain stability at off-center frequencies. This is likely due to the absence of a direct feedback path from the output to the input or a stabilizing element in the signal path, which is critical for wideband stability.

To address this, I introduce a feedback path from the output to the first stage's source node using a resistor (Rrep1) and a capacitor (ZC2) to ground. This provides a low-impedance path for feedback, which helps dampen oscillations and improve stability across the frequency range. Additionally, a new NMOS transistor (ZN1) is added to provide a controlled current path from the output to the bias network, further stabilizing the circuit by reducing the effective gain at higher frequencies.
