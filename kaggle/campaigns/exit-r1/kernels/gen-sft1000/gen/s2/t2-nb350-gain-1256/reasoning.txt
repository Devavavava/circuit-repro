The failure is due to the poor stability and phase margin in the wideband range, as evidenced by the negative margin in the `mu_min_wide_0.01-50GHz` constraint. The original topology has a two-stage structure with LC interstage coupling, which introduces resonant peaks and phase shifts that can lead to instability outside the narrowband center frequency (f0). The current mirror biasing and the interstage LC network contribute to this instability, especially in the wideband range.

To address this, I propose a structural change that replaces the two-stage LC-tuned amplifier with a single-stage common-gate (CG) topology with an inductor at the source, coupled with a second stage using a common-source (CS) configuration. This change improves the phase margin and stability by reducing the number of resonant nodes and providing a more predictable impedance transformation across the band. The common-gate stage offers better noise performance and impedance matching, while the source inductor helps stabilize the feedback path. The single-stage CG configuration also reduces the number of LC components, minimizing the risk of resonant peaks that destabilize the system in the wideband range.
