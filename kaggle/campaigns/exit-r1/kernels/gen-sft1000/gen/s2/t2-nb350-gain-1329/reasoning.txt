The failure is due to the poor stability and bandwidth characteristics of the two-stage LNA, as indicated by the binding constraint `mu_min_wide_0.01-50GHz` being negative. The current topology, while achieving good gain and noise figure, suffers from insufficient phase margin and stability across the wide frequency range, likely due to the LC interstage network and the lack of a direct feedback path to control the phase response. The LC interstage introduces a resonant peak that may cause phase shifts, leading to instability at the edges of the operating band.

To address this, I modified the topology by adding a direct feedback path from the output of the second stage to the input of the first stage via an additional NMOS transistor (ZN1) and a capacitor (ZC1). This creates a controlled feedback loop that helps stabilize the amplifier and improve the phase margin across the entire frequency range. Additionally, I replaced the LC interstage network with a simpler inductor (L2) connected to the gate of the second stage, reducing the complexity and potential for phase shifts. The capacitor ZC1 between VDD and n4 helps filter out high-frequency noise and stabilize the biasing of the second stage.
