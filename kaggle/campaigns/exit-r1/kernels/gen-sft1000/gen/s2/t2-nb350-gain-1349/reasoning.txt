The failure is due to the insufficient stability margin (`mu_min_wide_0.01-50GHz`) caused by the LC interstage resonant network (L2 and C2) and the second-stage output LC load (L3 and C3). These components introduce resonant peaks and phase shifts that reduce the stability margin across the wideband range, especially at frequencies near the resonant points. The current topology lacks sufficient damping or phase compensation to maintain a stable operation over the entire band.

To address this, I restructured the LNA by moving the LC interstage network to the source of the second stage (NM2), effectively turning it into a source-degeneration inductor. This change helps to increase the phase margin by reducing the feedback gain at higher frequencies. Additionally, I replaced the second-stage output LC load with a direct connection to VDD, simplifying the output stage and reducing the risk of resonant instability. The bias network was reconfigured to use a single current mirror (NM3) feeding both stages through resistors (R2 and R3), maintaining low noise and stable biasing. These changes improve the stability margin without sacrificing gain or noise performance.
