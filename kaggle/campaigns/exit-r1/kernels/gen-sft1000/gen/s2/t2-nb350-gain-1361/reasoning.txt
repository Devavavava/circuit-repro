The binding constraint, `mu_min_wide_0.01-50GHz`, measures the stability of the LNA over a wide frequency range, and its failure indicates that the circuit is unstable outside the narrowband center frequency. This instability is likely due to the LC interstage resonance, which introduces a resonant peak in the feedback path, leading to phase shifts that reduce the phase margin at off-band frequencies. The LC tank between stages creates a strong coupling between the output of the first stage and the input of the second stage, especially at frequencies far from the center frequency, which can lead to positive feedback and instability.

To address this, I restructured the circuit to eliminate the LC interstage resonance by replacing it with a direct coupling between the stages, using a single inductor in the source of the first stage and a second inductor in the source of the second stage. This change reduces the resonant feedback path and improves stability over the wideband. Additionally, I removed the current mirror bias network and replaced it with a simpler resistor-based biasing scheme, which reduces the number of nodes and potential sources of instability.

The revised netlist avoids the LC interstage tank and uses inductors in the source paths of the two stages to provide impedance transformation and noise reduction, while maintaining a stable feedback path.
