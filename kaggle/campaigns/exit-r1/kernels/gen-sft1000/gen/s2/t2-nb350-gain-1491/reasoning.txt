The binding constraint `mu_min_wide_0.01-50GHz` is failing due to insufficient stability across the wide frequency range, particularly at frequencies outside the narrowband center frequency (f0 = 3.5 GHz). This is likely caused by the LC interstage network (L2 and C2), which introduces resonances and phase shifts that destabilize the amplifier at off-band frequencies. The two-stage structure with LC loads and interstage LC coupling creates a feedback path that reduces the stability margin (`mu`) at higher frequencies, leading to a negative margin in the wideband stability metric.

To address this, I restructured the topology to eliminate the LC interstage coupling, which is the primary source of instability. Instead of using an LC network between the two stages, I replaced it with a direct connection from the first stage to the second stage, while maintaining a tuned load on the second stage. This reduces the number of inductors from 3 to 2, simplifying the feedback path and improving stability across the wideband range. Additionally, I moved the second stage's gate to a node directly connected to the first stage's drain, reducing the complexity of the interstage coupling and minimizing the introduction of resonant feedback paths. The bias network was preserved to maintain the same gate biasing strategy, ensuring consistent performance while improving stability.
