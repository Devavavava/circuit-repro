The noise figure (nf_max_db) is the binding constraint, and it is failing by ~0.32 dB. The issue arises from the noise contribution of the NM3 diode and the biasing network (R1, R2, R4, R5, PM2, NM3). These components add extraneous noise to the signal path, particularly due to the NM3 diode and the resistive biasing network, which are not directly involved in the signal amplification but are part of the DC path. This increases the overall noise floor, limiting the achievable noise figure. Additionally, the current-reuse topology, while beneficial for gain and power efficiency, introduces noise coupling through the biasing network if not carefully managed.

To address this, I replace the NM3 diode with a PMOS transistor (PM3) connected to the same internal node (n5) and the bias network (n6), while also connecting its source to n2 (the output of the main amplifier stage). This change reduces the noise contribution from the biasing network by isolating it from the signal path and using a PMOS transistor instead of a diode for biasing, which has lower noise characteristics. The PM3 transistor effectively acts as a noise-cancelling component, improving the noise figure by reducing the extraneous noise added by the biasing network. This structural change ensures that the biasing network is decoupled from the signal path, minimizing noise injection and improving the noise performance of the LNA.
