The failure is primarily due to noise figure (NF) being too high, as the binding constraint `nf_max_db` is exceeded by ~0.2 dB. This is likely caused by the noise contribution from the DC blocking capacitor `C4` at the input and the lack of a proper noise-shaping mechanism in the current-reuse topology. The absence of a noise-canceling component at the input and a more controlled signal path leads to an elevated noise floor, especially at the target frequency of ~1.22 GHz.

To address this, I added a noise-canceling NMOS transistor (`ZN1`) between the internal node `n0` (after the input capacitor `C4`) and the bias node `n5`. This transistor helps reduce the noise contribution from the input capacitor and improves the noise figure by providing a low-noise signal path. Additionally, I added a capacitor `ZC1` between the internal node `n5` and the output node `VOUT1`, which helps in stabilizing the output and further reducing noise coupling from the bias network to the signal path. These changes enhance the noise performance without significantly affecting other constraints like gain or power consumption.
