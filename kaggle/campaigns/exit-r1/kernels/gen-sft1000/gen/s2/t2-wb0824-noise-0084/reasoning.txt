The failure is due to insufficient stability and poor noise performance in the wideband region, particularly around the operating frequency of 1.385 GHz. The anchor circuit uses a push-pull current-reuse topology with a shared DC path, which introduces parasitic feedback paths and limits the stability margin. The current-reuse structure, while beneficial for power efficiency, creates a feedback path through the shared DC node (n3), leading to a lower stability factor (mu_min) and a poor noise figure in the wideband range.

To address this, I restructured the circuit by removing the shared DC path and isolating the push-pull stages. The NMOS and PMOS transistors are now connected to separate DC nodes (VSS and VDD), eliminating the parasitic feedback that was causing instability. Additionally, the load network was simplified and repositioned to improve impedance matching and reduce noise coupling. The removal of the shared DC node and the use of a more direct output coupling (C2) enhance the stability and noise performance over the wideband range.
