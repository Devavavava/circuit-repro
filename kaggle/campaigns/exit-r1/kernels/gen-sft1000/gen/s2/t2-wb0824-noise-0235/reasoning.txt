The failure is primarily due to the noise figure (NF) being too high, which is limited by the noise contribution from the input transistor NM1 and the biasing network. The current-reuse topology in the anchor netlist introduces additional noise paths through the biasing structure (NM3, R1, R2, PM2, R4, R5), which degrade the noise performance. The noise figure is also affected by the load configuration (L2 and C2), which may not be optimally tuned for noise suppression across the wideband frequency range. The shared DC path and the use of diode-connected transistors in the bias network introduce parasitic effects and noise coupling, exacerbating the issue.

To address this, the topology is restructured to eliminate the noise-inducing bias network and reduce the number of noise contributors. The NM3 and R1-R2 biasing structure are removed, and the gate of NM1 is directly connected to the input through a capacitor, reducing noise coupling. The PMOS biasing structure (PM2, R4, R5) is replaced with a simpler configuration that minimizes noise contribution. Additionally, the load network is reorganized to ensure better noise suppression and stability across the wideband frequency range. These changes reduce the overall noise contribution from the biasing network and improve the signal-to-noise ratio at the input.
