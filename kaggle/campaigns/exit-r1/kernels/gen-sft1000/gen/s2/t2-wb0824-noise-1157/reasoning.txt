The failure is primarily due to the noise figure (nf_max_db) being too high, exceeding the specification by approximately 0.26 dB. This is likely caused by the noise contributions from the biasing network and the current-reuse structure in the anchor netlist. Specifically, the NMOS diode NM3 and the R1-R2 feedback loop introduce noise that is amplified in the signal path, especially at the high-frequency operating point (1.385 GHz). Additionally, the current-reuse topology, while effective in boosting gain, may not be optimally noise-shaped in this configuration.

To address this, I propose a structural change by replacing the NM3 diode with a PMOS diode (ZP1) connected to a new internal node x1, which is tied to the output of the NMOS transistor NM1. This change introduces a more stable and lower-noise current reference, as PMOS diodes generally exhibit lower flicker noise compared to NMOS diodes. The new PMOS diode ZP1 is connected to the input of NM1, providing a more direct and cleaner current reference path. Additionally, a resistor ZR1 is added between the input of NM1 and the output of PM2, helping to decouple the signal path from the biasing network, thus reducing noise coupling into the signal chain.

These changes improve the noise figure by reducing the noise contribution from the biasing network and improving the signal-to-noise ratio in the amplifier stage.
