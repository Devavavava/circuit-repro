The binding constraint is the noise figure (nf_max_db), which is slightly over the limit by ~0.007 dB. This suggests that the noise contribution from the transistor NM1 or the biasing network is too high for the given frequency and topology. The current-reuse structure, while effective for gain and power efficiency, may introduce noise due to the additional biasing components and the shared DC path. The noise from the biasing network (NM3, R1, R2, etc.) and the input impedance mismatch may be contributing to the noise floor.

To address this, I modified the biasing structure by replacing the NMOS diode (NM3) with a PMOS diode (NM3 -> PM3) and reconnected the nodes to isolate the biasing network from the signal path more effectively. This change reduces the noise contribution from the biasing network by decoupling it from the signal path and using a PMOS transistor for biasing, which can offer lower noise in this configuration. Additionally, I restructured the load network to use an inductor (L2) connected to a new node (n5), which helps in isolating the biasing network and improving the noise performance.
