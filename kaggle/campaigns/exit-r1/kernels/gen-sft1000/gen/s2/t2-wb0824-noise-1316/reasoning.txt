The failure is primarily due to noise figure (nf_max_db) exceeding the specification. The noise performance is limited by the input transconductance stage and the feedback paths, particularly the NMOS NM3 and the resistor R2 in the original netlist. These components introduce noise and degrade the signal-to-noise ratio, especially at the target frequency of ~1.38 GHz. The current-reuse topology, while beneficial for gain and power efficiency, does not sufficiently isolate the input from noise-generating elements in the feedback path.

To address this, I replaced the NMOS NM3 and resistor R2 with a capacitor ZC1 and a resistor ZR2, connecting n5 to n1 and VDD, respectively. This change introduces a noise-canceling feedback path, reducing the noise contribution from the input transconductance stage. Additionally, the new capacitor ZC1 decouples the noise from the feedback loop, improving the signal-to-noise ratio at the input. The change in resistor R5 to connect to VSS instead of n3 also helps in reducing noise coupling from the load to the gain stage.

These structural changes improve the noise figure by isolating the input from noise-generating components and introducing a cleaner feedback path, directly addressing the binding constraint.
