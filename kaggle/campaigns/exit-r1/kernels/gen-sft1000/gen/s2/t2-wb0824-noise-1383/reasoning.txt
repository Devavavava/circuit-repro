The failure is due to the insufficient stability margin (mu_min_wide_0.01-50GHz) over the wideband range, which is caused by the feedback loop and the shunt-peaked load not adequately suppressing oscillations at higher frequencies. The resistive shunt feedback and the inductor-based load, while effective for broadband matching and gain, do not sufficiently dampen the phase shifts that accumulate across the wideband operation, leading to instability.

To address this, the topology must be modified to provide more robust phase compensation and stability over the entire frequency range. Adding a second NMOS transistor in a common-gate configuration (NM2) with a series inductor (L2) and capacitor (C2) creates a low-impedance path for high-frequency signals, improving phase margin and reducing feedback-induced oscillations. This change enhances the stability by introducing a controlled phase shift at high frequencies, while maintaining the broadband characteristics of the original design.
