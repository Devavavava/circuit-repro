The failure is due to the insufficient stability and poor phase margin in the current-reuse LNA topology, which leads to a degradation in the stability factor (mu_min_wide_0.01-50GHz) over the wideband frequency range. The anchor netlist uses a push-pull configuration with a shared DC path and a tuned load (L2+C2), which introduces parasitic feedback and resonant effects that destabilize the amplifier at higher frequencies. The current-reuse structure, while beneficial for gain and noise, lacks sufficient isolation and phase compensation across the wideband, causing the stability factor to dip below the required threshold.

To address this, I redesigned the topology to eliminate the shared DC path and reduce feedback coupling between the input and output. The new structure uses a common-gate configuration for the NMOS amplifier (NM1) with a direct connection to the output through a resonant inductor (L3), which improves isolation and suppresses parasitic feedback. The PMOS stage (PM1) is reconfigured to provide a stable current source to the NMOS amplifier without introducing a shared DC path. The addition of a second resonant inductor (L3) and a decoupling capacitor (C2) between the NMOS amplifier and the output enhances phase margin and suppresses unwanted resonances. The biasing network is simplified to avoid introducing additional feedback paths or parasitic capacitances that could destabilize the circuit.
