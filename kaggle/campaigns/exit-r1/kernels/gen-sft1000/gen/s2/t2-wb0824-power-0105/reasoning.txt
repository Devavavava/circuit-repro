The failure is due to insufficient gain and poor stability in the wideband operation, as evidenced by the s21_db margin being negative (i.e., the achieved gain is below the specification). The anchor netlist uses a resistive shunt-feedback configuration with a shunt-peaked inductive load. While this topology offers good input matching and moderate gain, it struggles to maintain sufficient gain across the entire wideband frequency range due to the limited amplification of the single-stage common-source (CS) amplifier and the parasitic effects of the inductive load at high frequencies.

The proposed solution introduces a two-stage topology with a cascode configuration and an additional inductive peaking element to enhance gain and stability. The first stage uses a PMOS transistor (PM1) in a common-gate configuration, which provides a low input impedance and helps reduce the Miller effect, improving bandwidth. The second stage is a common-source NMOS transistor (NM1) with a cascode PMOS (PM2) to increase output impedance and gain. An additional inductive peaking element (L2) is added to the output to compensate for high-frequency losses and improve gain flatness. The resistive feedback network (R1, R2) is restructured to provide better DC bias stability and control over the gain-bandwidth product.
