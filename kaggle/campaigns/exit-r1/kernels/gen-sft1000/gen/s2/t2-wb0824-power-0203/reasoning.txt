The failure is primarily due to noise figure (nf_max_db) exceeding the specification, which suggests that the noise contribution from the input transistor or the biasing network is too high. The anchor netlist uses a diode-connected NMOS (NM3) for biasing, which introduces additional noise due to its nonlinear behavior and the lack of a dedicated noise-shaping mechanism. This is exacerbated by the absence of a proper noise-suppression element in the signal path, leading to a higher-than-allowed noise floor.

To address this, I introduce a noise-canceling capacitor (ZC1) between the internal bias node (n5) and the drain of NM1 (n1). This capacitor provides a low-impedance path for high-frequency noise from the bias network to ground, effectively decoupling it from the signal path and reducing the noise contribution to the input transistor. This change directly targets the noise figure, reducing the noise floor without compromising other performance metrics like gain or stability.
