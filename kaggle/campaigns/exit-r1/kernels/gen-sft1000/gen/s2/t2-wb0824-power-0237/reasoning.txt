The failure is primarily due to noise figure (nf_max_db) being too high, exceeding the specification by ~0.14 dB. This is likely caused by the noise contributions from the biasing network and the current mirror structure in the anchor netlist. The NMOS diode-connected transistor NM3 and the resistor R1 in the bias network introduce significant noise at the input node, especially when the current mirror is not perfectly matched or when the biasing network is not optimized for noise. Additionally, the lack of a direct noise-shielded path from the input to the active amplifying stage may be exacerbating the noise pickup.

To address this, I restructured the biasing network to isolate the noise-sensitive input node from the biasing components. Specifically, I replaced the NM3 diode with a PMOS transistor connected to a separate bias node, and reconfigured the current mirror to use a different topology that minimizes noise injection. This change ensures that the biasing network does not directly couple noise into the input signal path. Additionally, I added a new capacitor (ZC1) to decouple the biasing node from the signal path, further reducing noise coupling.
