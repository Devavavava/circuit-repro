The binding constraint `mu_min_wide_0.01-50GHz` is failing due to instability in the amplifier's phase margin across the wideband frequency range. This is likely caused by the single-stage resistive shunt-feedback topology, which has limited phase compensation and is prone to oscillation at higher frequencies, especially when the feedback network interacts poorly with the load inductance. The shunt-peaked load (L1+R2) and the resistive feedback (R1) may not provide sufficient phase margin across the entire band, leading to a negative stability margin (`mu_min`) at certain frequencies.

To address this, I propose a structural change to the topology by introducing a second-stage common-gate (CG) amplifier with a shunt inductor and a self-biased NMOS transistor, which provides additional phase compensation and improves stability. This is achieved by adding a PMOS transistor (PM1) and a NMOS transistor (NM2) in a CG configuration with a shunt inductor (L2), along with a self-biased NMOS transistor (NM3) to provide a stable current source. The additional inductor (L2) and the CG stage help to isolate the feedback path and provide a more controlled phase response over the wideband range, improving the stability margin.
