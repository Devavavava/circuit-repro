The failure is due to the insufficient stability margin (mu_min_wide_0.01-50GHz), which is negative, indicating instability over the specified frequency range. This is likely caused by the single-stage common-source (CS) topology with resistive shunt feedback and a shunt-peaked inductive load, which may not provide enough phase margin across the wideband operation. The feedback network and the inductive load may not adequately suppress oscillations or provide sufficient damping at higher frequencies, leading to instability.

To address this, I propose a structural change to the topology by introducing a second-stage common-gate (CG) amplifier with an inductive source degeneration and a resistive feedback network. This configuration enhances the phase margin and provides better stability over a wide frequency range. The addition of a PMOS transistor in the second stage with an inductive source degeneration helps in achieving a flatter phase response and improved gain-bandwidth product. The resistive feedback network from the second stage to the first stage also helps in stabilizing the overall system.
