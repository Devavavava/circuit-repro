The failure is primarily due to noise figure (nf_max_db) being too high, despite the circuit meeting most other constraints. This suggests that the noise contribution from the biasing network or the transistors themselves is not being adequately suppressed. In the anchor netlist, the NMOS transistor NM3 is connected as a diode (n5-n5), which may introduce noise due to its nonlinear behavior and lack of proper impedance control. Additionally, the current mirror biasing structure may not be effectively isolating noise from the signal path.

To address this, I replaced NM3 with a PMOS transistor (ZP1), connected as a current source (n5-n6), which provides a more stable and lower-noise biasing reference. This change improves noise performance by reducing the noise contribution from the bias network. The PMOS current source offers better impedance matching and a more predictable noise behavior compared to the diode-connected NMOS in the original design. This structural change directly targets the noise figure constraint, reducing its value by improving the signal-to-noise ratio at the input stage.
