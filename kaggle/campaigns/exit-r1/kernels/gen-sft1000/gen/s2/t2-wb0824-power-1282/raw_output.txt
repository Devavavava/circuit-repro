

The binding constraint `mu_min_wide_0.01-50GHz` failed due to insufficient phase margin across the wideband frequency range, likely caused by the lack of a stabilizing feedback mechanism in the anchor netlist. The original topology relied on a resistive shunt feedback and a shunt-peaked inductive load, which may not provide sufficient phase compensation at the lower and upper edges of the band, leading to instability and a negative margin in the stability factor `mu_min`.

To address this, I introduced a second-order feedback loop using a PMOS transistor (PM2) and a NMOS transistor (NM3) in a current mirror configuration, along with an inductor (ZL1) and a PMOS transistor (ZP1) to provide additional phase compensation and stabilize the amplifier across the wideband. This added feedback loop helps to increase the phase margin and improve stability, especially at the band edges. The addition of the current mirror and inductor in the feedback path enhances the amplifier's ability to maintain a stable gain and phase response over the full frequency range, directly addressing the instability issue that caused the failure.
