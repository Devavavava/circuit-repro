The failure is due to the noise figure (NF) being too high, as the binding constraint `nf_max_db` is exceeded by ~0.18 dB. This suggests that the noise contribution from the input transistor NM1 or the biasing network is too significant, likely due to the lack of a noise-shaping mechanism or insufficient isolation from noise sources in the bias network. The current-reuse topology, while efficient, may be amplifying noise from the bias path, particularly from NM3 and the resistive biasing network (R1, R2, R4, R5), which are directly connected to the signal path. The absence of a noise-canceling or noise-isolating element in the bias path is likely the root cause.

To address this, I introduce a noise-canceling capacitor (ZC1) between the internal node n2 (the output of NM1) and the bias node n5. This capacitor provides a low-impedance path for high-frequency noise from the bias network to ground, effectively decoupling the signal path from the noise sources in the biasing network. This change reduces the noise contribution from the bias network to the signal path, thereby lowering the overall noise figure.
