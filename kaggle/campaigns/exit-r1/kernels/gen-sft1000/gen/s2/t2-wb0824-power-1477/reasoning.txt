The failure is due to the insufficient stability margin (mu_min_wide_0.01-50GHz) in the original resistive shunt-feedback CS LNA topology. The feedback resistor (R1) and the shunt-peaked inductor (L1) create a feedback path that is not sufficiently controlled over the wideband range, leading to a drop in the stability factor (mu) at certain frequencies within the band. This instability is exacerbated by the single-stage CS topology, which lacks sufficient phase margin and feedback control to maintain stability across the entire 0.01–50 GHz range.

To address this, I propose a structural change that introduces an additional feedback path and a second stage to enhance phase margin and stability. The new topology includes a second NMOS transistor (NM2) and a PMOS transistor (PM1) to form a second stage with a local feedback path, which helps maintain phase margin and improve stability over the wideband. The additional inductor (L2) and capacitors (C1, C2, C3, C4) help shape the frequency response and provide better impedance matching and feedback control. The introduction of a PMOS current source (PM1) and a resistive divider (R4, R5) ensures a stable bias point without introducing DC sources or bias networks.
