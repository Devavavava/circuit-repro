The binding constraint is the minimum stability factor (mu_min) over the 0.01–50 GHz band, which is failing due to insufficient phase margin and poor feedback control in the original topology. The resistive shunt feedback in the anchor netlist creates a narrowband stability window, and the shunt-peaked inductor (L1) does not sufficiently suppress oscillatory modes at the edges of the band. This results in a marginally unstable region near the band edges, causing the mu_min_wide constraint to fail.

To address this, I replace the resistive shunt feedback with a capacitive feedback network and introduce a second NMOS transistor to provide active feedback and improve phase compensation. This change enhances the stability factor across the band by providing a more robust feedback mechanism and better phase control. The addition of a second NMOS transistor (NM2) and a feedback capacitor (ZC1) between VDD and n2 helps stabilize the amplifier over a wider frequency range. The new topology ensures a more uniform and stable gain response, avoiding the instability that caused the failure.
