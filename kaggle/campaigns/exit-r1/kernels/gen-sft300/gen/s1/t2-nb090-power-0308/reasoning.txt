The failure is primarily due to the noise figure (nf_db) being just slightly above the specification limit, indicating that noise contributions from the input transistor or biasing network are limiting the performance. This is likely caused by the noise generated at the gate of NM1, which is influenced by the biasing network (R1, R2, R4, R5) and the diode-connected NM3. The current-reuse topology, while effective for gain and power efficiency, may introduce noise coupling or insufficient noise isolation at the input stage.

To address this, I propose a structural change that isolates the noise-sensitive input transistor (NM1) from the biasing network by introducing a separate bias path. This is achieved by replacing the diode-connected NM3 with a PMOS transistor (PM2) that is biased directly from VDD, and connecting it to a new node (n6) that is decoupled from the main signal path. This change reduces noise coupling from the bias network to the input signal path, thereby lowering the noise figure. The new configuration also ensures that the gate of NM1 is isolated from the noise sources in the biasing network, improving the overall noise performance of the LNA.
