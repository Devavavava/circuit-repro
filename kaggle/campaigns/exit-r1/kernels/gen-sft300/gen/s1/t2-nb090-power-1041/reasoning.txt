The failure is primarily due to poor input matching, as evidenced by the s11_max_db constraint being violated by ~0.67 dB. This suggests that the input impedance of the LNA is not sufficiently matched to the source impedance at the target frequency (900 MHz), leading to a reflection coefficient that is too high. The original topology uses a simple capacitor (C1) to couple the input to the gate of NM1, without any additional impedance transformation or tuning at the input. This likely results in a mismatch at the input port, especially given the narrowband nature of the design and the high-frequency operation.

To address this, I added an inductor (ZL1) in series with the input coupling capacitor (C1), creating an LC tank at the input. This introduces a resonant circuit that can be tuned to match the input impedance at the operating frequency. This change enhances the input matching by allowing the input impedance to be adjusted more precisely, reducing reflections and thus improving the s11_max_db metric.
