The failure is primarily due to poor stability and poor phase margin at the operating frequency, as evidenced by the negative margin on the `mu_min_wide_0.01-50GHz` constraint, which indicates insufficient stability across the band. This is likely caused by the lack of a feedback path or a stabilizing element in the second stage, leading to poor phase compensation and potential oscillation at higher frequencies. The absence of a direct feedback path from the output to the bias network or from the second stage to the first stage exacerbates this issue.

To address this, I introduce a feedback path from the second stage (node n6) to the bias network (node n7) using an additional NMOS transistor (ZN1), which connects node n6 (output of the second stage) to VDD and VSS, with its gate tied to n7. This addition introduces a local feedback mechanism that helps stabilize the second stage, improving the phase margin and thus the stability metric (`mu_min_wide_0.01-50GHz`). The feedback helps dampen oscillations and ensures a more robust operation across the frequency band.
