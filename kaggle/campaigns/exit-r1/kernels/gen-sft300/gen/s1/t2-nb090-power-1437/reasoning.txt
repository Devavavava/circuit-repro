The failure is due to insufficient stability margin (`mu_min_wide_0.01-50GHz`) caused by the lack of a dedicated stability-enhancing element in the feedback path. The original topology relies on resistive shunt feedback and a shunt-peaked inductor load, which may not be sufficient to ensure unconditional stability across the entire 0.01–50 GHz band. The feedback resistor (R1) alone does not provide enough phase compensation to prevent oscillations or instability at higher frequencies, especially in the presence of parasitic capacitances and inductor quality factors.

To address this, I added a capacitor (ZC1) in parallel with the inductor (L1) and a resistor (ZR1) in series with the output capacitor (C2). The capacitor ZC1 introduces a zero in the feedback path, improving phase margin and stability. The resistor ZR1 adds a controlled impedance in the output path, helping to dampen resonant peaks and ensure a more uniform stability margin across the band. These changes provide additional phase compensation and reduce the likelihood of instability, directly addressing the binding constraint.
