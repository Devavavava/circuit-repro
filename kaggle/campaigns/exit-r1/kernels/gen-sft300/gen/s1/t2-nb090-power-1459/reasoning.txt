The binding constraint is the stability factor `mu_min_wide_0.01-50GHz`, which measures the stability of the LNA across a wide frequency range. The negative margin indicates that the circuit is unstable at some frequencies outside the narrowband center frequency (f0=900 MHz). This instability is likely due to the lack of a feedback or stabilizing element in the second stage, which is critical for ensuring unconditional stability across the entire band.

The current topology uses a two-stage CS configuration with LC tank loads, but no direct feedback or stabilizing component in the second stage. The absence of a feedback path or a stabilizing capacitor/inductor in the second stage allows for potential oscillation or instability at off-band frequencies, especially when the second stage's output is directly connected to the LC tank load without any impedance control.

To address this, I added a feedback path from the output of the second stage (n6) to the input of the first stage (n1) via a capacitor (ZC1) and a resistor (ZR1) to VDD. This introduces a controlled feedback mechanism that stabilizes the second stage and reduces the likelihood of oscillation at off-band frequencies. The addition of ZC1 and ZR1 provides a low-impedance path for feedback at high frequencies, ensuring that the stability factor meets the required minimum across the entire 0.01–50 GHz range.
