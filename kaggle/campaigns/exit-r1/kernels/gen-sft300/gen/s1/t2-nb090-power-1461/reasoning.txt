The failure is primarily due to poor input matching, as evidenced by the s11_max_db constraint failing by ~0.34 dB. The original topology lacks explicit input impedance control, leading to a mismatch at the input port. The first-stage NMOS transistor (NM1) is directly driven by a capacitor (C1) from the input, but without a dedicated input impedance tuning element, the input reflection coefficient is too high. This is exacerbated by the absence of a direct connection between the first stage and the second stage’s bias network, which may cause signal degradation and reflection at the input.

To address this, I added a direct connection between the first stage (n2) and the bias reference node (n7) via a resistor (Rrep1), and another resistor (Rrep2) from the second stage's bias node (n5) to VDD. These resistors help stabilize the bias and improve input impedance matching by providing a low-impedance path for AC signals and reducing signal reflection at the input port. This structural change enhances the input matching without adding extra inductors or capacitors, keeping the topology within the inductor limit and improving the s11_max_db constraint.
