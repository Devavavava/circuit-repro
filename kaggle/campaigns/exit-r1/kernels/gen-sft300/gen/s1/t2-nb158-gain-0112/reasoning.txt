The failure is primarily due to the insufficient gain (s21_db) in the current-reuse push-pull CS LNA topology. The anchor netlist employs a current-reuse configuration with a shared DC path, but the gain is limited by the inherent noise and impedance mismatch in the push-pull structure, especially at the operating frequency of 1.575 GHz. The gain is further constrained by the tuning of the load (L2+C2), which may not be optimally set to maximize the transimpedance gain at the desired frequency. Additionally, the shared DC path introduces unnecessary noise and impedance mismatches that degrade the overall gain and noise figure.

To address this, the topology is restructured to use a single-stage common-gate (CG) configuration with a cascode structure, which is known for its high gain and good noise performance at RF frequencies. This configuration avoids the push-pull structure's inherent limitations and provides better impedance matching at the input and output. The use of a separate DC biasing network (NM3 and R2) ensures that the signal path is not compromised by DC components, while the addition of a second inductor (L2) and capacitor (C1) helps in tuning the output impedance and improving the gain.
