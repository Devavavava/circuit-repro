The binding constraint is the insufficient gain (s21_db), which is limited by the second-stage transistor NM2. The second stage is underdriven due to the lack of a direct biasing path to the gate of NM2, relying instead on a resistive divider (R2 and R3) from the current mirror reference (R1 -> NM3). This configuration leads to poor gain and stability, especially at the target frequency, due to the high impedance and insufficient control over the operating point of NM2. Additionally, the absence of a direct biasing path to NM2's gate introduces unnecessary phase shifts and impedance mismatches, reducing the overall gain and causing the s21_db to fall short of the required 33.994 dB.

To address this, I added a direct biasing path from VDD to the gate of NM2 via a resistor (ZR2) and a capacitor (ZC2) to ensure a stable and controlled operating point. This improves the gain by ensuring NM2 is properly biased and reduces phase shifts that could otherwise degrade the gain and stability. The capacitor ZC2 helps to filter out high-frequency noise, while ZR2 provides a low-impedance path for biasing. This structural change ensures that the second stage is more robust and contributes effectively to the overall gain of the LNA.
