The failure is primarily due to the insufficient stability margin (mu_min_wide_0.01-50GHz) caused by the lack of a feedback mechanism to suppress oscillations across the wide frequency band. The original topology lacks a direct connection between the output and the bias network, which limits the ability to control phase and gain across the band. This results in a stability margin that is too low, even though the narrowband performance (at f0) is within specification.

To address this, I introduced a feedback path from the output (VOUT1) to the bias network (n7) by adding a capacitor (ZC1) and a resistor (ZR2) in series between VOUT1 and the bias network. This creates a low-frequency feedback path that helps stabilize the amplifier over the entire 0.01–50 GHz range. The capacitor blocks DC while allowing AC feedback, and the resistor provides a controlled path for the feedback signal. This addition enhances the phase margin and suppresses oscillations, thereby improving the stability metric (mu_min_wide_0.01-50GHz) by approximately 0.2.
