The binding constraint is the insufficient forward gain (s21_db), which is failing by ~0.32 dB. The two-stage LNA is unable to achieve the required minimum gain of 28.53 dB at the target frequency. This is likely due to the second stage's transconductance being too low, or its output impedance being too high, leading to a weaker signal transfer from the first to the second stage. The LC interstage network may also be causing unwanted impedance mismatches or signal attenuation at the operating frequency.

To improve the forward gain, I added a second inductor (L4) in parallel with the first stage’s drain inductor (L1), connecting from VDD to the second stage’s input node (n4). This additional inductor helps to improve the impedance matching and increase the effective transconductance of the second stage by reducing the voltage drop across the interstage network. This change enhances the signal transfer from the first to the second stage, thereby increasing the overall gain.
