The failure is primarily due to the insufficient stability margin (`mu_min_wide_0.01-50GHz`), which is negative by ~0.734, indicating instability at some frequency within the 0.01–50 GHz range. This instability likely arises from the second-stage NMOS (NM2) being too weakly driven or having poor feedback suppression due to the LC interstage network and lack of additional damping. The LC interstage (L2 and C2) may be allowing for resonant peaking or phase shifts that reduce the stability margin.

To address this, I introduce a shunt capacitor (ZC1) between VDD and the internal node n2, which is the drain of NM1. This capacitor provides a low-impedance path to ground at high frequencies, thereby reducing the gain of the first stage at those frequencies and improving the stability margin. This change adds a small amount of damping without significantly affecting the gain or noise figure, as the capacitor is only active at frequencies beyond the LNA's operating band.
