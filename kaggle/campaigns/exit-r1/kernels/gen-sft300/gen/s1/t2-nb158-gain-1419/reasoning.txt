The failure is primarily due to poor input matching, as evidenced by the s11_max_db constraint being violated by ~0.51 dB. The input impedance is not sufficiently matched to the source, likely because the first stage's input network lacks a direct path for real impedance adjustment. The existing configuration relies on a single DC-blocked capacitor (C1) to couple the input signal to the gate of NM1, but this does not provide enough flexibility to tune the input impedance at the target frequency (1.575 GHz). The LC interstage network and the second stage's tuning also contribute to a mismatched load at the input, reducing the overall reflection coefficient.

To address this, I added a direct resistor (ZR1) from VDD to the drain of NM1 and another resistor (ZR2) from VDD to the gate of NM2. These resistors provide additional impedance tuning paths at the target frequency, helping to improve the input matching by allowing the real part of the input impedance to be adjusted more effectively. This structural change enables better control over the input impedance without introducing additional inductors or capacitors, maintaining the low-noise and high-gain characteristics of the two-stage amplifier.
