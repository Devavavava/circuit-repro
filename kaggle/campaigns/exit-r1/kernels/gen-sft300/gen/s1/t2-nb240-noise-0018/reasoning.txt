The binding constraint is the insufficient forward gain (s21_db), which is 10.4194 dB instead of the required 12.2534 dB. This is likely due to the current-reuse topology and the shared DC path limiting the signal swing and gain. The shared DC path between the NMOS and PMOS stages may cause signal degradation and reduce the overall gain. Additionally, the load network (L2+C2) might not be optimally tuned for maximum gain at the target frequency.

To address this, I replaced the current-reuse push-pull configuration with a single-stage NMOS common-source amplifier with an inductive source degeneration and a resistive load. This topology allows for higher gain by avoiding the signal-splitting effects of the push-pull configuration and provides better control over the gain-bandwidth product. The inductive source degeneration (L1 and C1) helps in stabilizing the gain and improving linearity, while the resistive load (R1) ensures a higher voltage swing at the output.
