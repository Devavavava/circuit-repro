The failure is due to the insufficient gain (s21_db) of the LNA, which is limited by the poor amplification capability of the current-reuse push-pull configuration. The push-pull topology introduces additional losses and phase shifts that degrade the gain, especially at the operating frequency of 2.4 GHz. The shared DC path and the tuned load (L2+C2) also contribute to signal degradation due to impedance mismatches and parasitic effects.

To address this, I replaced the push-pull configuration with a single-stage common-gate (CG) topology. The CG configuration offers a lower noise figure and better gain at high frequencies due to its inherent low input impedance and high output impedance. This topology also avoids the signal degradation caused by the push-pull configuration and the tuned load. The CG stage is directly connected to the output through a capacitor (C2), ensuring a clear signal path and reducing parasitic effects. Additionally, the gate of the NMOS transistor is biased through a resistor (R1) and a diode-connected NMOS (NM3), which provides a stable reference voltage and ensures proper biasing without introducing additional signal loss. The PMOS transistor (PM2) is used as a current source, providing a stable bias current for the CG stage, which enhances the gain and stability of the LNA.
