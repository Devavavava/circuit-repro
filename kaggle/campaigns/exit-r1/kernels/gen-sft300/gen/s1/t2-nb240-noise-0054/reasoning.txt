The failure is due to the insufficient gain (s21_db) of the LNA, which is limited by the low transconductance of the NMOS and PMOS amplifying stages. The current-reuse topology, while effective in reducing noise, may not provide sufficient voltage gain at the operating frequency due to the limited gain-bandwidth product of the NMOS/PMOS devices and the load impedance mismatch. Additionally, the shared DC path and the use of a diode-connected NMOS for biasing may introduce unnecessary losses and reduce the overall gain.

To address this, I propose a structural change that replaces the current-reuse push-pull configuration with a cascode topology, which can enhance the transconductance and improve the voltage gain. This is achieved by introducing a PMOS cascode device (PM2) and a resistor (R3) to provide a stable current path and improve the output impedance. Additionally, I added a capacitor (ZC1) to decouple the bias network from the signal path, and a resistor (ZR1) to provide a stable DC reference for the NMOS biasing. These changes help increase the gain and ensure better impedance matching at the operating frequency.
