The failure is primarily due to the insufficient stability margin (`mu_min_wide_0.01-50GHz`) at the operating frequency, which is a critical constraint for the LNA's robustness. The current topology, while meeting most of the performance metrics like noise figure (NF), gain (S21), and input/output matching (S11), lacks sufficient phase and gain stability across the wide frequency range. The interstage LC network and the current-mirror biasing contribute to the instability, especially at off-band frequencies, where the feedback paths may become too weak or phase-shifted to maintain unconditional stability.

To address this, the structural change introduces a feedback path from the output (VOUT1) to the input side of the first stage (n2) via a resistor (ZR2) and a capacitor (ZC1), which helps in stabilizing the system by providing a controlled feedback mechanism. Additionally, a capacitor (ZC2) is placed between the second stage's drain load (VDD) and the interstage LC network (n4), which helps in reducing parasitic coupling and improving the phase margin. These changes enhance the stability margin without significantly impacting the other performance metrics.
