The binding constraint is the minimum stability factor (mu_min) over a wide frequency range (0.01–50 GHz). The original design fails this constraint due to poor stability across the band, likely caused by the lack of a dedicated stability-enhancing component in the feedback path or the absence of a mechanism to suppress oscillations at off-band frequencies. The LC interstage network and the current mirror biasing may not provide sufficient phase margin or damping at higher frequencies, leading to a stability margin that is too low at some points in the band.

To address this, I added a small capacitor (ZC1) between the current mirror reference node (n7) and the interstage LC network (n4). This capacitor provides a low-impedance path for high-frequency signals, helping to dampen resonances and improve stability by introducing a phase shift that counteracts potential instability in the feedback loop. The capacitor is strategically placed to affect the stability without significantly degrading the noise figure or gain.
