The failure is primarily driven by the insufficient stability margin (mu_min_wide_0.01-50GHz) due to the narrowband nature of the shunt-peaked load and the lack of a dedicated stability-enhancing component. The original circuit relies on a resistive shunt feedback and a shunt-peaked inductor (L1) to shape the frequency response, but this topology does not inherently provide sufficient phase margin across a wide bandwidth, especially at the edges of the operating band. The low noise figure and poor input match also suggest inadequate impedance transformation and signal coupling at the desired frequency.

To address this, I introduce a common-gate (CG) stage in parallel with the main common-source (CS) amplifier. This CG stage provides a low-impedance path for signal feedback and helps to improve the phase margin by introducing a zero in the feedback loop, which stabilizes the amplifier over a wider frequency range. Additionally, the CG stage enhances the input match by providing a lower impedance at the input node, which complements the resistive feedback (R1) and reduces the noise contribution from the input network. The addition of a capacitor (C2) across the CG stage and the output provides a frequency-dependent feedback path that further enhances stability and bandwidth.
