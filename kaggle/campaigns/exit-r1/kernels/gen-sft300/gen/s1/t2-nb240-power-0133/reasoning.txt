The failure is primarily due to the insufficient gain (s21_db) of the LNA, which is limited by the current-reuse push-pull topology and the load configuration. The current-reuse structure, while effective in reducing noise, introduces additional loss and phase shifts that degrade the gain at the target frequency. The tuned load (L2+C2) is not optimally interacting with the amplifier's output impedance, leading to a mismatch that reduces the overall gain. Additionally, the diode-connected NM3 and PM2 transistors used for biasing do not provide sufficient stability or control over the current flow, contributing to the poor gain performance.

To address this, I replaced the current-reuse push-pull structure with a common-gate (CG) stage for the PMOS, which offers better impedance matching and higher gain at the operating frequency. The CG configuration allows for a lower output impedance, improving the interaction with the tuned load and boosting the gain. I also reconfigured the load network to ensure a more effective resonance at 2.4 GHz, which enhances the transducer gain. Finally, I simplified the biasing structure by removing the diode-connected NM3 and PM2 transistors, replacing them with a direct connection to VDD and VSS, which reduces parasitic effects and improves stability.
