The failure is primarily due to the insufficient gain (s21_db) of the LNA, which is limited by the poor transconductance and impedance mismatch in the current-reuse topology. The anchor netlist uses a push-pull configuration with a shared DC path and a current-reuse structure, which introduces parasitic effects and impedance mismatches at the output node, degrading the gain and stability. Additionally, the shared DC path and the use of a PMOS current mirror for biasing may be causing a reduction in the effective transconductance of the amplifier, especially at the target frequency of 2.4 GHz.

To address this, the topology is restructured to eliminate the shared DC path and reduce parasitic coupling. A common-gate configuration is introduced for the output stage, which improves the output impedance matching and enhances gain. This is achieved by replacing the PMOS current mirror with a resistor-based biasing structure and reconfiguring the output network to use a common-gate configuration with an additional inductor and capacitor for impedance tuning. These changes reduce the impact of parasitics and improve the gain-bandwidth product, leading to a significant improvement in s21_db.
