The current topology, while optimized for gain and noise figure at the center frequency (f0 = 2.4 GHz), lacks sufficient feedback or impedance control mechanisms to suppress oscillations or instability outside the narrowband tuning range. This is likely exacerbated by the absence of a direct feedback path from the output to the input or a damping element in the interstage network.

To address this, I introduced a resistor (ZR2) from VDD to the interstage node (n4), which adds a controlled impedance path and helps dampen any resonant peaks or parasitic oscillations in the interstage LC network. This change enhances the stability margin by introducing a small but effective feedback mechanism without significantly degrading the gain or noise performance. The resistor also helps to reduce the Q-factor of the interstage LC tank, thereby broadening the bandwidth of the stability margin and improving the mu_min_wide constraint.
