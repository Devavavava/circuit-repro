The binding constraint is the insufficient forward gain (s21_db), which is limited by the poor impedance matching and the low transconductance of the NMOS input transistor (NM1) due to the high gate resistance in the current-reuse configuration. The current-reuse topology introduces a high impedance path through the diode-connected NM3 and resistive divider (R1-R2), which degrades the signal integrity and reduces the achievable gain at the operating frequency. Additionally, the shared DC path and the tuned load (L2+C2) may not be optimally placed to support the desired gain-bandwidth product.

To address this, I replace the current-reuse configuration with a cascode topology that enhances the transconductance and improves impedance matching. The NM3 diode-connected transistor is replaced with a cascode NMOS (ZN1) that is biased by a separate current source (ZN2) to ensure a stable and high transconductance. This change reduces the gate resistance and improves the signal transmission through the amplifier. Additionally, a separate DC bias path (ZN2 and Rrep1) is introduced to decouple the biasing from the signal path, ensuring better stability and performance. The addition of a resistive feedback (Rrep1) helps in stabilizing the gain and improving the linearity of the amplifier.
