The failure is primarily due to insufficient gain (s21_db) in the LNA, which is limited by the current-reuse topology and the shared DC path. The push-pull configuration with NMOS and PMOS transistors introduces additional losses and phase shifts, reducing the overall gain at the operating frequency. The current-reuse structure also increases the noise figure and degrades the stability margin, especially at high frequencies. The shared DC path between the input and output further complicates the impedance matching and signal integrity, leading to a suboptimal gain and poor stability.

To address this, I propose a structural change to the LNA topology by replacing the push-pull configuration with a single NMOS transistor in a common-source configuration, with a resonant inductor-capacitor (LC) load. This simplifies the signal path, reduces losses, and improves gain and stability. The LC load provides a high-impedance path at the operating frequency, enhancing the transconductance and gain. Additionally, the DC path is restructured to isolate the input and output, ensuring better impedance matching and signal integrity. The bias network is moved to a separate node, allowing for more precise control of the operating point without interfering with the signal path.
