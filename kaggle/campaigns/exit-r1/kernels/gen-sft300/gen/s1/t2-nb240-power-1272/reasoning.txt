The failure is due to the poor stability margin (mu_min_wide_0.01-50GHz) caused by insufficient phase margin in the feedback loop of the two-stage amplifier. The LC interstage network and the lack of additional feedback mechanisms lead to a narrow stability band, which is exacerbated at off-resonance frequencies. This results in the stability metric (mu_min) failing by a large margin.

To address this, I added a feedback path from the output of the second stage to the input of the first stage via a capacitor (ZC2) and a resistor (ZR1), forming a negative feedback loop. This helps improve the phase margin and stabilize the amplifier over a wider frequency range. Additionally, I introduced a resistor (ZR2) from the output of the first stage to the drain of the second stage to provide local feedback and enhance stability. A resistor (ZR3) from VDD to the input of the second stage helps in balancing the signal path and improving the stability further. A capacitor (ZC4) between VDD and the first stage’s gate helps in filtering out noise and stabilizing the input signal. A resistor (ZR4) from the output of the second stage to the gate of the first stage forms another feedback path, reinforcing the stability improvement.
