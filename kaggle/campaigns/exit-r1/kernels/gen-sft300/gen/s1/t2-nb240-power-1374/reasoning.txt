The failure is primarily due to the noise figure (nf_db) exceeding the specification by ~0.2 dB, which is likely caused by the noise contributions from the bias network and the second stage's transconductance mismatch. The current biasing scheme, which uses resistors R2 and R3 to bias both NM1 and NM2 gates from a single current mirror reference (NM3), introduces noise and reduces the overall noise performance of the LNA. Additionally, the second stage's drain load (L3) is not optimally decoupled from the noise sources in the first stage, leading to a higher noise figure than desired.

To address this, I propose a structural change that decouples the second stage from the noise sources in the first stage and reduces the noise contribution from the bias network. This is achieved by introducing a new NMOS transistor (ZN1) and a capacitor (ZC1) to provide a low-noise, noise-isolated path for the second stage's gate bias. The capacitor ZC1 blocks noise from the first stage's drain node (n2) from reaching the second stage's gate (n5), while ZN1 provides a low-noise current mirror reference for the second stage's gate bias, reducing noise injection from the bias network. This change also removes the direct resistor coupling between the bias network and the second stage’s gate, reducing noise coupling and improving the noise figure.
