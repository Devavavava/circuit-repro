The failure is primarily due to the inadequate stability margin (mu_min_wide_0.01-50GHz), which is critically below the required minimum. This is likely caused by the shared DC path between the NMOS and PMOS stages, leading to a poor phase margin and potential oscillations across the wide frequency band. Additionally, the current-reuse topology, while beneficial for gain, may be exacerbating the instability by creating a feedback loop that reduces the phase margin at higher frequencies.

To address this, I restructured the circuit to isolate the DC paths of the NMOS and PMOS stages and introduce a separate biasing network for the PMOS stage. This is achieved by adding a resistor (ZR1) between VDD and the gate of NM3, and a PMOS transistor (ZP1) that provides a dedicated biasing path for the PMOS stage. This change decouples the DC paths, improving the stability margin by reducing feedback-induced phase shifts. The addition of a capacitor (ZC1) between VSS and the source of NM3 further stabilizes the NMOS stage by filtering out high-frequency noise and providing a low-impedance path to ground for AC signals.
