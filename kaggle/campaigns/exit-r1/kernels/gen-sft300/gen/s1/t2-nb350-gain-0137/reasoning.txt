The existing structure lacks a direct feedback path from the output to the input or a stabilizing component that can dampen oscillations, especially at frequencies outside the narrowband center frequency (3.5 GHz). The absence of such a mechanism results in a low stability margin, which is critical for robust operation in the presence of process variations and parasitics.

To address this, I introduced a feedback path using a capacitor and resistor network (Crep1, Rrep1, Rrep2) that connects the output (VOUT1) to the gate of the NMOS transistor (NM3) and to ground. This feedback helps stabilize the amplifier by introducing a controlled phase shift and damping oscillations. Additionally, I added a resistor (ZR1) between the output (VOUT1) and a new internal node (n2), which helps manage the output impedance and further improve stability. The new capacitor (ZC1) between VDD and the internal node (n2) provides a high-frequency bypass, reducing noise and improving the power supply rejection ratio. These changes collectively enhance the stability margin without compromising gain or noise figure.
