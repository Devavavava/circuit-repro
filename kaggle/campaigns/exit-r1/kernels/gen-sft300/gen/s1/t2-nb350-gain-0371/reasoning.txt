The failure is primarily due to insufficient gain (s21_db), which is limited by the current-reuse topology and the load configuration. The anchor netlist uses a current-reuse push-pull configuration with a tuned load (L2+C2), which may not be optimal for achieving the required gain at 3.5 GHz. The gain is further constrained by the input and output coupling, as well as the impedance mismatch between the transistors and the load.

To address this, I propose a structural change that replaces the push-pull configuration with a single NMOS transistor in a common-gate configuration, with an inductive source degeneration. This topology improves gain by reducing the Miller effect and increasing the output impedance. The inductive source degeneration (L2) helps to increase the transconductance of the transistor at the operating frequency, thereby boosting the gain. Additionally, the use of a separate inductor (L1) for input coupling and a resistor (R3) for biasing provides better control over the signal path and DC operating point.
