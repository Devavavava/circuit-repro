The binding constraint is the stability margin (mu_min_wide_0.01-50GHz), which is failing due to poor stability over a wide frequency range. The original topology, while achieving good gain and noise figure, suffers from insufficient phase margin and stability across the band, likely due to the current-reuse push-pull configuration and the tuned load (L2+C2), which can introduce resonances and phase shifts that destabilize the amplifier at off-band frequencies. The lack of a dedicated stabilizing element or a more robust feedback mechanism is likely the root cause.

To address this, I added a new NMOS transistor (ZN1) and a capacitor (ZC1) in a shunt feedback configuration from the output (n2) to VSS, and a capacitor (ZC2) from VDD to the input node (n1), and a resistor (ZR1) from the output (n2) to VDD. This introduces a local negative feedback path that enhances stability over a wide frequency range. The shunt feedback from n2 to VSS via ZN1 and ZC1 helps to reduce gain at higher frequencies and improve phase margin, while ZC2 and ZR1 help to suppress oscillations and stabilize the amplifier across the band. These additions are structural and do not interfere with the original biasing or sizing network, allowing the downstream optimizer to continue working with the same constraints and objectives.
