The binding constraint `mu_min_wide_0.01-50GHz` is failing due to poor stability across a wide frequency range. The original topology uses a two-stage common-source configuration with LC-tuned loads and an interstage LC coupling, which is effective for narrowband gain and noise figure but lacks sufficient feedback or damping at frequencies outside the narrowband center frequency (f0 = 3.5 GHz). This leads to a low stability factor (μ) at off-band frequencies, especially in the 0.01–50 GHz range, as evidenced by the negative margin of -0.029624.

The issue is exacerbated by the lack of a direct feedback path from the output to the input or a damping mechanism at the second stage. The current topology relies on the LC interstage coupling for signal transfer, but this does not inherently provide stability across a wide band. The absence of a shunt or series resistor/capacitor to dampen oscillations at off-band frequencies contributes to the poor μ_min_wide_0.01-50GHz performance.

To address this, the proposed structural change introduces a feedback mechanism by adding a resistor (ZR2) from VDD to n4 (the node connected to the interstage LC network), and a capacitor (ZC2) from VDD to n7 (the bias node). These additions provide a low-frequency path for feedback, which helps stabilize the circuit over a wider frequency range. The resistor ZR2 introduces a small amount of negative feedback, reducing gain at off-band frequencies and improving μ. The capacitor ZC2 helps bypass high-frequency noise and provide a stable bias point, further enhancing stability.
