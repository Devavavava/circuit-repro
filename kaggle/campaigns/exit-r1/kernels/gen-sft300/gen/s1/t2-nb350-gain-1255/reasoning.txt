The failure is primarily due to insufficient gain (s21_db) in the current-reuse push-pull CS LNA topology. The gain is limited by the load impedance seen at the output node n2, which is dominated by the resonant LC tank (L2 and C2). At the target frequency of 3.5 GHz, the impedance of this tank is not sufficiently high to provide the required voltage gain, especially when combined with the current-reuse configuration. The output impedance of the NMOS transistor NM1 is also not optimized for maximum transconductance and voltage gain at this frequency. Additionally, the current-reuse structure introduces a non-ideal feedback path that degrades the gain at the operating frequency.

To address this, I modified the topology by replacing the resonant LC tank with a resistive load (Rrep1) and adding a second NMOS transistor (NM3) in a common-gate configuration to improve the output impedance and transconductance. This change allows for better voltage gain by increasing the effective transconductance of the amplifier and reducing the impact of the non-ideal current-reuse feedback. The addition of NM3 also helps in stabilizing the output impedance and improving the gain at the target frequency.
