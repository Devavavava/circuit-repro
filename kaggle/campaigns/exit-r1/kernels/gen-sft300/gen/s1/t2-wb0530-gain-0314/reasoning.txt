The failure is due to the insufficient stability margin (`mu_min_wide_0.01-50GHz`) across the wideband frequency range. The anchor circuit uses a resistive shunt-feedback CS LNA with a shunt-peaked inductive load, which is effective for broadband gain and input matching but lacks sufficient phase margin at higher frequencies, leading to instability. The `mu_min` is marginally above the requirement, but the wideband stability metric (`mu_min_wide_0.01-50GHz`) is failing, indicating that the circuit is unstable at certain frequencies within the 0.01–50 GHz range.

To address this, I replace the resistive shunt feedback with a capacitive feedback network and restructure the load to include a second inductor and a PMOS transistor, forming a current-reuse topology. This change increases the phase margin by introducing additional poles and zeros that stabilize the feedback loop over the wideband range. The PMOS transistor also helps to reduce the output impedance, improving the stability and allowing for better control over the feedback phase.
