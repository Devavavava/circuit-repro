The feedback resistor (R1) and the inductor (L1) in the original design create a feedback loop that is not robust enough to suppress oscillations at low frequencies, leading to a negative stability margin.

To address this, I replaced the resistive shunt feedback with a PMOS transistor (PM1) in a common-gate configuration, which provides a more controlled and stable feedback path. The PMOS transistor is biased by a resistor (R3) connected to the drain of the NMOS transistor (NM1), ensuring a stable operating point. A second inductor (L2) is added in parallel with the output capacitor (C2) to enhance the stability by providing additional phase margin at low frequencies. A new resistor (R4) is added to ground to provide a stable reference for the PMOS transistor's source. The input capacitor (C1) is also adjusted to ensure proper DC blocking and AC coupling. These changes improve the stability margin by providing a more predictable and robust feedback mechanism and enhancing the phase margin at low frequencies.
