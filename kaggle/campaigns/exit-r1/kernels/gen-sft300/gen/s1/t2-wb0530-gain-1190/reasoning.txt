The noise figure (NF) is the binding constraint, and it is failing due to the noise contribution from the NMOS transistor NM3 and the resistor R1 in the bias network. In the anchor netlist, NM3 is connected as a diode-connected NMOS with its source tied to VSS and its gate connected to n5, which is also connected to R1 (VDD → n5). This configuration introduces a significant noise contribution from the bias network, particularly from the resistor R1 and the diode-connected NM3, which increases the overall noise figure of the LNA.

The current-reuse topology in the anchor netlist is also contributing to the noise figure because the shared DC path and the lack of noise isolation between the biasing network and the signal path are causing unwanted noise coupling. This is exacerbated by the fact that R1 is directly connected to the gate of NM3, which is part of the signal path, leading to a degradation in noise performance.

To address this, I introduce a noise-isolated biasing network that decouples the noise contribution from the biasing circuitry to the signal path. This is achieved by adding a new capacitor (ZC1) and a new inductor (ZL1) to create a noise-isolated path for the bias current. Additionally, I introduce a new internal node (n4) and use it to connect the biasing network to the signal path through ZC1 and ZL1, which helps in reducing the noise coupling from the bias network to the signal path.

By reconfiguring the biasing network with these additional components, the noise contribution from the biasing circuit is significantly reduced, which improves the noise figure of the LNA and brings it within the required specification.
