The original topology relies on a resistive shunt feedback and a shunt-peaked inductive load, which may not provide sufficient phase compensation at higher frequencies, leading to instability. This is exacerbated by the lack of active feedback mechanisms to stabilize the amplifier over the entire band.

To address this, I propose replacing the resistive shunt feedback with an active common-gate (CG) stage that provides additional phase compensation and improves stability. This is achieved by adding a PMOS transistor (PM1) in a CG configuration, which introduces a low-impedance path to ground at high frequencies, thereby improving phase margin. Additionally, a second NMOS transistor (NM2) is added in a source follower configuration to provide a low-impedance path from the feedback node to the output, enhancing the stability factor. A capacitor (C3) is introduced between the feedback node and the input to provide additional phase compensation at high frequencies. These changes collectively improve the stability factor across the wideband frequency range while maintaining the desired gain and noise figure.
