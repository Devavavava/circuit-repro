The noise figure (NF) is the binding constraint, failing by ~0.32 dB. The anchor netlist uses a current-reuse push-pull configuration with a diode-connected NMOS (NM3) for biasing, which introduces noise due to the non-ideal behavior of the NMOS in the diode configuration. Additionally, the PMOS current-setting (PM2) and resistor-based biasing (R1, R2, R4, R5) add noise and degrade the noise performance. The lack of a proper noise-canceling mechanism in the input stage and the presence of multiple noise contributors in the bias network are the primary causes of the elevated NF.

To address this, I propose a structural change by replacing the diode-connected NMOS (NM3) with a PMOS transistor (PM3) and connecting it to the same internal node (n5) as the original NM3. This change introduces a PMOS-based biasing mechanism that reduces noise contributions from the bias network. The PMOS transistor in this configuration has lower noise figure compared to the NMOS in diode configuration, especially at high frequencies. This change also helps in stabilizing the bias current and reducing the noise floor, thereby improving the overall noise performance of the LNA.
