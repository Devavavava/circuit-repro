The failure is primarily due to noise figure (NF) being too high, exceeding the specification by a small margin. This is likely caused by the noise contribution from the NMOS transistor NM3 in the bias network, which is directly connected to the signal path via the DC block C4. The NM3 transistor, being in a diode-connected configuration, introduces significant noise due to its high gate-source voltage swing and lack of noise suppression mechanisms. Additionally, the current-reuse topology in the anchor netlist may not be effectively suppressing noise from the biasing network, especially at the high-frequency band specified (1.22 GHz).

To address this, I restructured the biasing network to decouple the noise-sensitive NM3 transistor from the signal path. I replaced the direct connection of NM3 to the signal node n1 with a new internal node n6, and added a resistor Rrep1 and a capacitor ZC2 to create a noise-filtering path from n6 to VDD and VSS, respectively. This change isolates the bias network from the signal path, reducing the noise contribution from NM3. Additionally, I introduced a new resistor Rrep1 and capacitor ZC2 to provide a low-noise, high-impedance path for biasing, which helps suppress noise from the bias network. These changes effectively reduce the noise figure while maintaining the required gain and stability.
