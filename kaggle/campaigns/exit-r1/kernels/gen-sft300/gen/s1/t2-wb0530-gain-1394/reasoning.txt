The binding constraint `mu_min_wide_0.01-50GHz` is failing due to poor stability margins across the wideband frequency range. The original topology uses a current-reuse push-pull configuration with a shared DC path and a tuned load (L2+C2), but this introduces parasitic feedback paths and weakens the stability in the lower and upper frequency extremes. The lack of a dedicated stability-enhancing component, such as a resistor or inductor in the feedback path, allows for phase shifts that reduce the stability factor (mu) below the required threshold over the wideband.

To address this, I introduce a resistor (ZR1) connecting the internal node n5 to VDD and a capacitor (ZC1) from n4 to n6. These components provide additional phase compensation and suppress parasitic feedback, thereby improving the stability factor across the entire band. The resistor ZR1 adds a controlled impedance to the bias network, while the capacitor ZC1 filters out high-frequency noise and stabilizes the feedback path. This structural change directly enhances the stability margin without compromising gain or noise figure.
