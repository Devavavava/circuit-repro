The binding constraint is the minimum stability factor (`mu_min_wide_0.01-50GHz`), which is failing due to instability in the wideband frequency range. This is likely caused by the lack of a feedback path from the output to the input or from the output to the biasing network, leading to insufficient phase margin and poor stability at higher frequencies. The original topology relies on a resistive shunt feedback configuration with a shunt-peaked inductive load, which may not provide sufficient phase compensation across the entire band.

To address this, I introduced a feedback path from the output to the biasing network by adding a PMOS transistor (PM1) and a resistor (R3) connecting the bias node (n3) to the output (VOUT1). This creates a new feedback loop that enhances stability by providing additional phase margin and damping at high frequencies. The PMOS transistor also helps in stabilizing the bias point, reducing the risk of oscillation in the wideband range.
