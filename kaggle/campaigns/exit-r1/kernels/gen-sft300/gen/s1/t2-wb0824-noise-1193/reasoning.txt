The failure is due to insufficient stability margin (mu_min_wide_0.01-50GHz) across the wideband frequency range. The original circuit uses a resistive shunt feedback topology with a shunt-peaked inductive load. While this configuration provides good noise and gain performance, it lacks sufficient phase margin over the entire band, leading to instability at certain frequencies. This is exacerbated by the feedback resistor (R1), which sets the DC bias but does not contribute to stability across the band.

To address this, I replaced the resistive shunt feedback with a current-reuse topology that includes an additional NMOS transistor (NM2) and a PMOS transistor (PM1). This configuration allows for better control of the phase response across the band, improving stability. The addition of a capacitor (C2) between the output and the inductive load helps to further stabilize the circuit by providing a low-impedance path for high-frequency signals, reducing phase shifts that could lead to instability. The use of a PMOS transistor in the current-reuse configuration also helps in improving the stability margin by providing a more predictable and controlled feedback path.
