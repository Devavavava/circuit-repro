The failure is primarily due to the noise figure (nf_max_db) being too high, exceeding the specification by approximately 0.2 dB. This is likely caused by the noise contribution from the biasing network and the current-reuse structure, which introduces additional noise sources and degrades the overall noise performance. The current-reuse topology, while beneficial for gain, may not be optimally configured for noise in this wideband application. Additionally, the shared DC path and the placement of the tuned load may not be ideal for noise suppression across the entire frequency range.

To address this, I introduce a structural change that isolates the noise-sensitive components from the biasing network. This is achieved by adding a new NMOS transistor (NM2) in a diode-connected configuration, which acts as a noise-canceling element. NM2 is connected between n4 and VDD, with its source tied to n5 (the gate reference node). This change provides a low-noise path for the signal while decoupling the biasing network from the signal path, reducing the noise contribution from the biasing components. This structural modification helps lower the noise figure without significantly affecting the gain or the input/output matching.
