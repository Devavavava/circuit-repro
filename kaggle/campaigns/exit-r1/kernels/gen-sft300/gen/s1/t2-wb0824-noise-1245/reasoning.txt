The binding constraint is the minimum stability factor (mu_min) over the wideband frequency range (0.01–50 GHz), which is failing due to instability caused by the feedback and inductive peaking in the original topology. The resistive shunt feedback and shunt-peaked load may introduce phase shifts that reduce stability margins at certain frequencies, especially in a wideband configuration. The original structure lacks explicit stabilization elements to counteract these phase shifts, leading to a negative margin in the mu_min_wide constraint.

To address this, I introduce a new topology that adds a second inductor (L2) and a PMOS transistor (PM1) to provide active stabilization and improve phase compensation. The PMOS transistor is used in a common-source configuration with a resistive load (R3) and a second inductor (L2) to provide additional phase margin and suppress oscillations. This configuration helps maintain a stable operation across the wideband frequency range by introducing a controlled phase shift and reducing the feedback-induced instability. The new topology also includes a second capacitor (C2) to provide additional isolation and a resistor (R3) to help stabilize the output node.
