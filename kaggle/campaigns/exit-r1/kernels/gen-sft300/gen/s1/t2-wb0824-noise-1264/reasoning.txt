The failure is primarily driven by the noise figure (nf_max_db) exceeding the specification, indicating that the noise contribution from the input transistor NM1 is too high. This is likely due to the lack of a noise-shaping mechanism or insufficient noise isolation between the input and the rest of the circuit. The current-reuse topology, while effective for gain and power efficiency, may not inherently suppress noise in this wideband configuration, especially at the given frequency (1.38 GHz).

The structural issue is that the input transistor NM1 is directly connected to the output through the shared node n2, which is also connected to the resonant load L2 and C2. This creates a noise coupling path from the input to the output, increasing the noise figure. Additionally, the absence of a noise-isolation element at the input stage allows thermal noise from the transistor and the resonant load to propagate to the output.

To address this, I introduce a noise-isolation capacitor (ZC1) between VDD and the internal node n5, which is the gate reference point of NM3. This capacitor acts as a high-frequency noise shunt, reducing the noise contribution from the power supply and the transistor NM3 to the input signal path. It also helps decouple the gate reference from high-frequency noise, improving the noise figure by isolating the input transistor from noise sources in the bias network and the resonant load.
