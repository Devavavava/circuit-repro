The binding constraint is the minimum stability factor (`mu_min_wide_0.01-50GHz`), which is failing due to insufficient phase margin across the wideband frequency range. This is likely caused by the feedback network and the shunt-peaked load configuration not adequately suppressing oscillatory behavior at higher frequencies. The resistive shunt feedback and the inductive peaking may not be sufficient to maintain stability over the entire band, especially in the absence of a dedicated stability-enhancing element.

To address this, I introduce a PMOS transistor (PM1) in a common-gate configuration, connected between the feedback node (n3) and the output node (n2), with its source tied to VDD and gate tied to the drain of the NMOS transistor (NM1). This addition provides an additional path for signal feedback that enhances phase margin and stabilizes the amplifier over the wideband. The PMOS transistor acts as a low-impedance path at high frequencies, helping to dampen oscillations and improve stability without compromising gain or noise performance.
