The failure is primarily due to the insufficient stability margin (mu_min_wide_0.01-50GHz), which is critical for ensuring unconditional stability across the wideband frequency range. The original topology, while effective in achieving good noise figure (NF), gain (S21), and power consumption (IDD), suffers from poor phase and magnitude stability due to the lack of a dedicated stability-enhancing element in the feedback path. The current-reuse configuration and the shared DC path between the NMOS and PMOS stages may introduce parasitic feedback paths or insufficient phase margin at certain frequencies, especially at the lower end of the wideband range. This results in a negative stability margin, causing the amplifier to oscillate or exhibit poor performance at the edge of the operating band.

To address this, a structural change is introduced: a new capacitor (ZC1) is added between the internal node n2 and the output node VOUT1. This capacitor provides a low-impedance path for high-frequency signals, effectively increasing the phase margin and enhancing stability by reducing the feedback loop gain at higher frequencies. Additionally, it helps in shaping the frequency response to meet the ripple constraint on S21. The capacitor ZC1 is placed in a position that does not interfere with the DC biasing or the main signal path, ensuring minimal impact on the noise figure and gain.
