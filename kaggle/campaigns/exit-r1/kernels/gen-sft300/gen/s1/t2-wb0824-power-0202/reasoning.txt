The noise figure (NF) is the binding constraint, failing by ~0.2 dB. This is likely due to the noise contribution from the NMOS transistor NM3 in the bias network, which is connected as a diode (n5-n5) and tied to VSS via R2. This configuration introduces a noise source that is not effectively suppressed, especially at the high-frequency operating point (f0 = 1.385 GHz). The current-reuse topology is otherwise effective for gain and stability, but the noise contribution from NM3 is amplified by the feedback and gain stages, pushing the NF above the specification limit.

To address this, I replace the diode-connected NM3 with a PMOS transistor (ZP1) in a common-source configuration, driven by a separate bias network (R4, R5, R2, and R1). This change decouples the noise contribution from the bias network and reduces the effective noise at the input stage. The PMOS transistor ZP1 is connected to a new node n6, which is biased through R4 and R5, ensuring a stable and low-noise current path. This structural change suppresses the noise contribution from the bias network and improves the overall noise figure.
