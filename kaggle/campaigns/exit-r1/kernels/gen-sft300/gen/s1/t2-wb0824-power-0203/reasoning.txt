The failure is primarily driven by the noise figure (NF) exceeding the specification, as indicated by the negative margin of -0.137912 for the nf_max_db constraint. This suggests that the noise contribution from the input transistor NM1 and the biasing network is too high for the target bandwidth. In the anchor netlist, NM3 is connected as a diode-connected NMOS, which introduces noise from the gate and drain, and the biasing network uses resistors R1, R2, R4, and R5, which are known to add thermal noise. The current-reuse topology, while efficient for gain, may not be optimally suppressing noise in this wideband scenario.

To address this, I replaced the diode-connected NM3 with a resistor Rrep1 connected between n5 and n6, and added a capacitor ZC1 between n5 and n6 to short noise from the biasing network to ground. This change removes the noise-generating NM3 transistor and replaces it with a passive component, reducing the noise contribution from the biasing path. The capacitor ZC1 also helps to decouple the biasing network from the signal path, further improving noise performance. Additionally, I added a resistor Rrep2 between n2 and VDD to provide a more stable and lower-noise bias point for the input transistor NM1.
