The binding constraint `mu_min_wide_0.01-50GHz` is failing due to insufficient stability margin across the wideband frequency range. This is likely caused by the feedback topology and the shunt-peaked load configuration, which may not be sufficient to maintain stability over the entire 1–50 GHz range. The single-stage resistive shunt feedback may not adequately suppress oscillations or provide sufficient phase margin at higher frequencies, especially when combined with the inductive load peaking.

To address this, I propose a structural change to the feedback topology. Instead of using a single NMOS transistor with resistive feedback, I introduce a second-stage NMOS transistor (NM2) and a PMOS transistor (PM1) in a cascode configuration. This provides additional phase margin and improved stability over a wideband. Additionally, I add a capacitor (ZC1) between VDD and the gate of NM1 to provide a low-frequency path for feedback, ensuring stable DC operation. The capacitor (ZC2) between VSS and n3 helps in stabilizing the shunt-peaked inductive load. The resistor (ZR2) from VDD to n3 provides an additional path for current, enhancing stability and reducing the risk of oscillation.
