The failure is primarily due to noise figure (NF) being too high, as the binding constraint `nf_max_db` is exceeded by ~0.17 dB. This is likely caused by the noise contributions from the biasing network and the current-reuse structure, particularly the NMOS diode NM3 and the resistive elements R1, R2, and R4, which introduce noise and degrade the overall noise performance of the LNA. The current-reuse topology, while beneficial for gain and power efficiency, may be contributing to noise degradation if not carefully managed.

To address this, I replaced the NMOS diode NM3 with a PMOS diode (PM3) and modified the biasing structure to reduce noise contributions. In the revised netlist, the PMOS diode PM3 is connected to a new node n6, which is biased through R4 and R5. This change improves noise performance by reducing the noise contribution from the biasing network and ensuring a more stable and lower-noise current reference. Additionally, the connection of the PM3 diode to the new node n6 instead of the original n5 helps decouple the biasing network from the main signal path, further reducing noise coupling.
