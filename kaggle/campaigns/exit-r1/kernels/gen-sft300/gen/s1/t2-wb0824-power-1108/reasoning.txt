The failure is primarily due to the poor input matching, as evidenced by the s11_max_db constraint being violated by ~0.11 dB. This indicates that the input impedance of the LNA is not sufficiently matched to the source impedance at the operating frequency, leading to a reflection coefficient that exceeds the specification. The root cause is likely the inadequate impedance transformation at the input port, which is exacerbated by the configuration of the input network and the lack of active impedance control.

To address this, I introduce a new NMOS transistor, NM3, in a diode-connected configuration, and connect it to a new internal node, n0, which is also connected to the gate of NM1. This change effectively introduces a feedback mechanism that helps in stabilizing and tuning the input impedance. By tying the gate of NM1 to n0, which is now influenced by the diode-connected NM3, the input impedance can be more precisely controlled, improving the match at the input port. This structural change adds a degree of active impedance control that was absent in the original design, thereby addressing the s11_max_db constraint.
