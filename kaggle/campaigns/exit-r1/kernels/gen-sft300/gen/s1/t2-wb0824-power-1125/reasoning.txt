The anchor netlist employs a current-reuse topology with a push-pull configuration, which is inherently prone to instability at the band edges due to the feedback and phase shifts introduced by the inductive load (L2) and the resonant tank (L2+C2). The lack of a dedicated stability-enhancing component, such as a resistor or capacitor in the feedback path, exacerbates this issue, especially at lower frequencies where the phase margin is most vulnerable.

To address this, I introduce a small capacitor (ZC1) between the internal node n6 and the output node n2. This capacitor provides a low-impedance path at high frequencies, which helps to stabilize the feedback loop and improve the phase margin across the wideband range. The capacitor is placed strategically in the feedback path of the PMOS stage, ensuring that it does not interfere with the main signal path or the biasing network. This structural change enhances the stability margin without significantly affecting the gain or noise figure, as the capacitor operates primarily at the band edges where stability is most critical.
