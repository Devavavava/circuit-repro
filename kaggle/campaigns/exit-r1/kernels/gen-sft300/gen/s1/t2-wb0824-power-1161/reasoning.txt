The binding constraint `mu_min_wide_0.01-50GHz` is failing due to insufficient stability margin across the wideband frequency range. The original circuit uses a resistive shunt-feedback configuration with a shunt-peaked inductive load, which, while effective for broadband gain and input matching, may not provide sufficient phase margin at higher frequencies, especially when the amplifier is sized for maximum performance. This leads to a reduced stability factor (`mu`) at the edges of the band, causing the amplifier to oscillate or become unstable in certain frequency ranges.

To address this, I propose a structural change to the LNA topology by replacing the resistive shunt feedback with a current-reuse configuration that incorporates an additional NMOS transistor in the feedback path. This change introduces a current mirror structure that helps improve the phase margin and stability over the wideband range. The new topology adds a PMOS transistor (`PM1`) for current mirroring and an additional NMOS transistor (`NM2`) to provide a low-noise, stable feedback path. This change enhances the amplifier's stability while maintaining its broadband performance and input match.
