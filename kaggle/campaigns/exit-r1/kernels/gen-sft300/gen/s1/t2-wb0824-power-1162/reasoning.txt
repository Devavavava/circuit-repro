The binding constraint is the minimum stability factor (mu_min_wide_0.01-50GHz), which is failing due to poor stability across the wideband frequency range. The anchor netlist uses a resistive shunt-feedback configuration with a shunt-peaked inductive load, which, while effective for broadband gain and input matching, does not inherently ensure sufficient phase margin or stability across the entire band. This is especially true at the lower and upper edges of the band, where the feedback phase may not adequately suppress oscillations, leading to a reduced stability factor.

To address this, I propose a structural change to the topology by replacing the resistive shunt feedback with an active feedback loop using a PMOS transistor and an additional inductor. This change introduces a second-stage feedback path that actively stabilizes the amplifier by providing a controlled phase shift and additional damping at critical frequencies. The PMOS transistor (PM1) in the feedback loop provides a low-impedance path for AC signals, improving the phase margin. The addition of the inductor (L2) in series with the PMOS transistor helps shape the frequency response to enhance stability across the wideband range. The resistor (R4) and capacitor (C3) in the feedback loop further fine-tune the phase and gain characteristics, ensuring sufficient stability margin without compromising the input match or gain performance.
