The failure is due to the insufficient stability margin (mu_min_wide_0.01-50GHz) in the wideband frequency range, which is critical for ensuring unconditional stability across the entire operating band. The original topology relies on a resistive shunt feedback and a shunt-peaked inductive load, which, while effective for gain and bandwidth, may not provide sufficient phase margin across the wideband, especially at higher frequencies. This is exacerbated by the single-stage common-source (CS) configuration, which can introduce phase lag that reduces the stability margin.

To address this, I propose a structural change to the topology by introducing a second NMOS transistor (NM2) in a cascode configuration, along with a capacitor (ZC1) between the drain of NM2 and an internal node (n4). This addition creates a second stage that introduces additional phase shift and helps in boosting the stability margin. The capacitor ZC1 also helps in further peaking the output impedance at higher frequencies, improving the phase margin and ensuring unconditional stability across the wideband. The addition of the NM2 transistor and ZC1 capacitor provides a more robust feedback path and enhances the phase response of the amplifier, directly addressing the stability issue.
