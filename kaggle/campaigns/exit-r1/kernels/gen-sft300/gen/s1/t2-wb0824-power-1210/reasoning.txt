The failure is due to insufficient stability margin (mu_min_wide_0.01-50GHz) over the wideband range, which is critical for ensuring unconditional stability across the entire frequency band of operation. The original netlist employs a resistive shunt feedback configuration with a shunt-peaked inductive load, which, while effective for wideband gain and input matching, may not provide sufficient phase margin across the entire 0.01–50 GHz range. This is likely due to the feedback network and load configuration not adequately suppressing oscillatory modes at higher frequencies, leading to a negative stability margin.

To address this, I propose a structural change that introduces a second-stage active feedback loop to enhance stability. This is achieved by adding a second NMOS transistor (NM2) in a common-gate configuration, which provides a low-impedance path for feedback and helps control the phase shift across the band. Additionally, a capacitor (ZC1) is added between the output and the bias node of the second stage to provide additional phase compensation. A resistor (ZR1) is also added from the input to ground to improve the input impedance and help stabilize the feedback loop.
