The failure is primarily due to the noise figure (nf_max_db) exceeding the specification. This is likely caused by the noise contribution from the biasing network and the current-reuse structure, which introduces additional noise sources and degrades the noise performance at the input. The NMOS and PMOS transistors in the current-reuse configuration may be contributing more noise than expected, especially at the high-frequency operating point (f0 = 1.385 GHz). Additionally, the biasing network (NM3, R1, R2, PM2, R4, R5) adds noise and may not be optimally placed to minimize noise at the input node.

To address this, I propose a structural change to the LNA topology by replacing the current-reuse configuration with a single-stage common-gate (CG) LNA with a source degeneration inductor. This topology is known for its low-noise characteristics and is better suited for wideband applications. The new configuration eliminates the current-reuse structure and the associated bias network, simplifying the noise path and reducing the noise contribution from the biasing elements. The inductor L2 is repositioned to provide a low-impedance path at the input, improving the noise figure. The NMOS transistor NM1 is directly connected to the input through a capacitor C1, and a new inductor L3 is added to provide source degeneration, which helps in stabilizing the amplifier and reducing noise. The PMOS transistor PM1 is connected to the output through a capacitor C2, forming a push-pull configuration with NM1 for improved linearity and bandwidth.
