The binding constraint is the noise figure (nf_max_db), which is slightly over the specification by ~0.08 dB. The noise is likely dominated by the input transistor NM1 and the biasing network, particularly the diode-connected NMOS NM3 and the resistive divider (R1, R2, R4, R5). These components introduce noise and reduce the noise figure due to their non-ideal behavior and the current-reuse topology. The current-reuse structure, while beneficial for gain, may not be optimally configured for noise in this wideband application, especially when the input transistor is not perfectly matched to the source impedance.

To address this, I replaced the diode-connected NM3 with a PMOS transistor (ZP1) connected to VDD and a resistor (Rrep1) to VSS, forming a current mirror that better matches the input transistor’s noise characteristics. Additionally, I reconfigured the biasing network to use a NMOS transistor (ZN1) as a current source, driven by a resistor (Rrep2) to VDD and a capacitor (ZC1) to the output node n2, which helps decouple noise from the signal path. These changes improve the noise matching and reduce the noise contribution from the bias network, directly addressing the nf_max_db constraint.
