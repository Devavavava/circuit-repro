The binding constraint is the stability factor `mu_min_wide_0.01-50GHz`, which measures the stability of the LNA across the entire 1–50 GHz band. The failure indicates that the circuit is marginally unstable in this band, likely due to the feedback topology and the inductive load configuration. The resistive shunt feedback and the shunt-peaked inductor load may not provide sufficient phase margin across the wideband, especially at lower frequencies where the inductor behaves more like a short, reducing the feedback effectiveness and increasing the risk of oscillation.

To address this, I modified the topology by replacing the resistive shunt feedback with a NMOS transistor-based feedback path. This change introduces a dynamic feedback mechanism that can actively adjust the phase and gain across the frequency band, improving stability. The addition of a PMOS transistor (PM1) and a capacitor (C4) between VDD and the feedback node enhances the phase margin by adding a zero at a lower frequency, which counteracts the phase loss from the inductive load. The capacitor C2 is now connected to a new internal node (n4), which helps in shaping the feedback loop's frequency response. The NMOS transistor NM2 is added to provide a more controlled current path for the feedback, improving the stability factor across the band.
