The binding constraint is the minimum stability factor (mu_min) over a wide frequency range (0.01–50 GHz), which is failing due to instability at frequencies outside the narrowband design focus. The original circuit uses a current-reuse topology with a shared DC path and a tuned load (L2+C2), which may not provide sufficient feedback or damping at off-band frequencies. This leads to a reduced stability margin, especially at higher frequencies where the LC tank may resonate or become reactive, causing phase shifts that reduce the stability factor.

To address this, the circuit topology is restructured to include a second NMOS transistor (NM2) in a feedback configuration, connecting the output (VOUT1) to the shared DC node (n2) and the gate of NM2 to the DC node (n5). This creates a local negative feedback path that helps stabilize the amplifier at off-band frequencies. Additionally, the NM2 transistor is connected to VDD and VSS, ensuring it operates as a feedback element without introducing additional noise or power consumption. This change enhances the stability factor by reducing the likelihood of oscillation and improving phase margin across the wide frequency range.
