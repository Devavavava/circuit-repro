The failure is due to insufficient gain (s21_db) in the resistive shunt-feedback CS LNA. The shunt-peaked load (L1+R2) and the feedback resistor (R1) are limiting the gain at the target frequency, likely due to the feedback network's impedance mismatch and the load's frequency-dependent behavior. The resistive feedback and shunt peaking are not providing sufficient amplification at 900 MHz, leading to a marginal s21_db that falls short of the specification.

To address this, I replaced the resistive shunt-feedback configuration with a common-gate (CG) stage in parallel with a common-source (CS) stage, forming a cascode-like structure. This topology enhances gain by reducing the Miller effect and improving the output impedance. The addition of a second NMOS transistor (NMOS NM2) in a common-gate configuration helps isolate the output, while the inductor (L2) and capacitor (C2) in the feedback path improve the frequency response. The inclusion of a PMOS transistor (PMOS PM1) adds a second stage for gain boosting and improved linearity. The capacitor (C1) and resistor (R1) now provide a more effective DC blocking and biasing mechanism, while the resistors (R2, R3, R4) and inductors (L1, L2) are arranged to optimize impedance matching and stability.
