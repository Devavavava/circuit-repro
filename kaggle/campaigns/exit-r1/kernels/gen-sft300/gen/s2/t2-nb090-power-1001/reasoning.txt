The failure is primarily due to the noise figure (nf_db) being too high, exceeding the specification by approximately 0.23 dB. This is likely caused by the noise contributions from the input transistor NM1 and the biasing network, particularly the NMOS diode NM3 and the resistor R2, which may not be optimally configured to minimize noise at the operating frequency. The current-reuse topology, while effective for gain and power efficiency, may not be sufficient to suppress noise in this configuration, especially without additional noise-shaping elements.

To address this, I introduce a noise-shaping inductor (L3) in series with the input capacitor C4 and a new internal node n7. This inductor helps to filter out high-frequency noise components at the input, thereby reducing the noise contribution from the bias network and improving the noise figure. The inductor L3 is connected between VDD and n7, and n7 is connected to n2, which is the drain of NM1. This modification adds a low-noise path at the input and helps to isolate the noise-sensitive input transistor from the biasing network, effectively shaping the noise spectrum to favor lower noise at the desired frequency band.
