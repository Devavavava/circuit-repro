The failure is primarily driven by the poor input matching, as evidenced by the s11_max_db constraint being violated by approximately -0.7 dB. This indicates that the input reflection coefficient is too high at the target frequency, which limits the LNA's ability to efficiently couple with the signal source. The root cause lies in the lack of explicit input impedance tuning in the anchor netlist. The first-stage NMOS transistor (NM1) is directly connected to the input through a DC-blocking capacitor (C1), but no impedance-matching component is present at the input to ensure a low reflection coefficient at the center frequency (f0 = 900 MHz).

The anchor netlist relies on the LC interstage network (C2 and L2) for some level of impedance transformation, but it is not sufficient to achieve the required input matching. This is particularly problematic in a narrowband LNA where precise impedance matching at f0 is critical. The absence of an explicit input impedance-matching component, such as a shunt capacitor or an inductor in parallel with the input, leads to a mismatch that degrades the s11 performance.

To address this, I introduce a new shunt capacitor (ZC1) connecting the drain node of NM1 (n2) to the internal node n4, which is part of the LC interstage network. This addition provides a controlled impedance path at f0, effectively tuning the input impedance of the first stage to better match the source impedance. The capacitor ZC1 acts as a frequency-selective component that enhances the impedance transformation at the operating frequency, improving the input reflection coefficient and thereby resolving the s11_max_db constraint violation.
