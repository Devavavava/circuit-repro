The binding constraint is the insufficient gain (s21_db), which is 12.27 dB instead of the required 14.88 dB. This indicates that the circuit's gain is too low at the operating frequency, likely due to poor impedance matching or inadequate signal amplification in the current-reuse topology. The current-reuse structure, while effective for noise reduction, may not be optimally configured for high-gain in this narrowband application, especially when sized under the given constraints.

To address this, I restructured the LNA by replacing the current-reuse push-pull topology with a single-stage common-gate (CG) NMOS amplifier with an inductive source degeneration. This change improves gain by leveraging the inductor's ability to provide a high impedance at the operating frequency, thereby increasing the transconductance of the NMOS transistor. The addition of a PMOS cascode stage (PM2) and a resonant load (L2 and C2) further enhances gain and stability. The biasing network is simplified with a resistive divider (R1 and R2) and a diode-connected NMOS (NM3) to ensure stable operation without introducing unnecessary noise or power consumption.
