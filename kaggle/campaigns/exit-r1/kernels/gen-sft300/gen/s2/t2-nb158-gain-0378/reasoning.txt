The original topology relies solely on the LC tank for impedance matching and gain, but this results in a resonant peak that destabilizes the amplifier over a wide bandwidth. The absence of a damping mechanism in the LC interstage (L2 and C2) leads to a high Q-factor, which exacerbates the instability.

To address this, I added a damping capacitor (ZC1) between the internal node n5 (the gate of NM2) and VDD, and a resistor (ZR1) between n5 and VSS. This introduces a controlled amount of feedback and reduces the Q-factor of the LC interstage, thereby improving the stability margin without significantly degrading the gain or noise figure. The resistor ZR1 and capacitor ZC1 form a low-pass filter that damps the resonant peak, ensuring the amplifier remains stable across the entire 0.01–50 GHz range.
