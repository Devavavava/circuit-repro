The failure is primarily due to the insufficient stability margin (`mu_min_wide_0.01-50GHz`) at the operating frequency, which is critical for ensuring the amplifier remains unconditionally stable across the band. The current topology, while optimized for gain and noise figure, lacks a dedicated stabilizing element that would help suppress oscillations and improve phase margin, especially in the second stage. This is exacerbated by the absence of a feedback or damping mechanism to control the phase shift introduced by the LC resonant tanks and the second-stage transistor.

To address this, I added a small capacitor (`ZC1`) between the output of the second stage (n6) and an internal node (n4), which is connected to the LC interstage network. This capacitor introduces a controlled phase shift and damping effect, effectively stabilizing the amplifier by reducing the feedback gain at problematic frequencies. It also helps in shaping the output impedance, thereby improving the stability margin without significantly degrading the gain or noise performance.
