The binding constraint is the insufficient forward gain (s21_db), which is below the required 22.4403 dB. The two-stage amplifier is underperforming due to the interstage LC network, which introduces a resonance that limits the bandwidth and amplification at the center frequency. The LC interstage (L2 and C2) introduces a second-order resonance that causes a gain dip at f0, preventing the amplifier from meeting the gain target. This is exacerbated by the lack of direct coupling between stages, which reduces the effective transconductance contribution from the second stage.

To address this, the LC interstage network is replaced with a direct capacitive coupling (C2) and a resistor (ZR2) in parallel with the second inductor (L2), effectively turning the second stage into a common-source amplifier with a resistive load. This change removes the resonant peaking that was limiting the gain and allows for a more consistent and higher gain at the center frequency. The resistor ZR2 provides a controlled impedance path for the second stage, ensuring stable amplification without the detrimental effects of the LC resonance.
