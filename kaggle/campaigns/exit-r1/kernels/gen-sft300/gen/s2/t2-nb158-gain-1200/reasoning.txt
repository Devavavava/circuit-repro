The failure is primarily due to the noise figure (nf_db) exceeding the specification. This is likely caused by the noise contribution from the biasing network, particularly the diode-connected NMOS (NM3) and the resistor R1, which are part of the current reference path. These components add noise that degrades the overall noise performance of the LNA, especially at the operating frequency of 1.575 GHz. Additionally, the current-reuse topology may be introducing additional noise through the PMOS (PM2) and its associated resistors (R4, R5), further degrading the noise figure.

To address this, I have redesigned the biasing network to reduce its noise contribution. The diode-connected NMOS (NM3) is now connected to a different node (n4) to minimize its impact on the signal path. Additionally, the resistor R2 is connected to a new internal node (n6) to decouple the biasing network from the signal path. This change reduces the noise coupling from the biasing network into the signal chain. The PMOS (PM2) is now connected to the new internal node (n6), and the resistor R4 is connected to the new node (n7), which helps in stabilizing the bias current while minimizing noise injection.
