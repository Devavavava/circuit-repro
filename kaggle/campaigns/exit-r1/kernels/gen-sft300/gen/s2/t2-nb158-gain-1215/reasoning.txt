The failure is due to insufficient gain in the second stage, as evidenced by the s21_db margin being -0.5155, which indicates the achieved gain is below the required 33.6057 dB. The current topology has a second stage with a tuned drain load (L3) but lacks a direct coupling path from the second stage to the output, which may cause signal loss and reduce the overall gain. This is compounded by the absence of a direct connection from the second stage's drain to the output node, which limits the signal transfer efficiency.

To address this, I added a direct coupling path from the second stage (NM2) to the output via a capacitor (ZC1) and an inductor (ZL2), which helps in maintaining a low-loss path for the signal while also improving impedance matching. Additionally, I introduced a second NMOS transistor (ZN1) as a cascode device, connected between the first stage and the second stage, which helps in boosting the gain by increasing the output impedance of the first stage and reducing Miller effect. This structural change enhances the signal transfer from the first stage to the second stage and improves the overall gain of the LNA.
