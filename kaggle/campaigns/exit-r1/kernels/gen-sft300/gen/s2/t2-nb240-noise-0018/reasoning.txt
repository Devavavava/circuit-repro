The binding constraint failure is due to insufficient gain (s21_db) in the current-reuse push-pull configuration. The anchor netlist's topology lacks a direct low-impedance path for the signal to traverse from the input to the output, leading to a significant loss in gain. The shared DC path and the use of a current-reuse structure without a dedicated signal path reduce the amplifier's ability to amplify the signal effectively at 2.4 GHz. Additionally, the output is loaded by a resonant circuit (L2+C2), which may not provide a stable and low-impedance load at the operating frequency, further degrading the gain.

To address this, I redesigned the topology to provide a more direct signal path and ensure a stable, low-impedance load at the output. I removed the resonant load (L2+C2) and replaced it with a direct connection from the output node to the load capacitor (C2), ensuring a clear signal path from the input to the output. I also restructured the biasing network to be more robust and ensure stable operation. The new topology includes a direct connection from the input to the gate of the NMOS transistor, and the output is now directly connected to a load capacitor, improving signal integrity and gain.
