The failure is primarily due to insufficient gain (s21_db) in the current-reuse topology. The anchor netlist uses a push-pull configuration with a shared DC path, which introduces parasitic effects and limits the gain-bandwidth product. The current-reuse structure, while beneficial for noise, suffers from poor impedance matching and signal degradation at the output due to the shared load and the lack of a dedicated high-impedance path for the output signal. The tuned load (L2+C2) is not effectively decoupled from the signal path, leading to reduced gain and poor stability.

To address this, I redesigned the topology to use a fully differential configuration with a separate output stage, ensuring a high-impedance path for the output signal and improved impedance matching. The new structure introduces a second NMOS transistor (NM3) for active biasing and a second PMOS transistor (PM2) for current mirroring, which helps in stabilizing the output and improving gain. The addition of a second inductor (L2) and capacitor (C2) in the output stage provides a better tuned load, enhancing gain and bandwidth. The new configuration also eliminates the shared DC path, reducing parasitic effects and improving stability.
