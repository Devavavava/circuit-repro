The failure is due to insufficient stability margin (mu_min_wide_0.01-50GHz) across the operating band, which is critical for robustness in narrowband applications. The anchor netlist uses a resistive shunt-feedback configuration with a shunt-peaked inductive load, which, while effective for gain and noise, lacks sufficient phase margin at higher frequencies due to the single-pole nature of the feedback loop and the resonant behavior of the inductor-capacitor network. This results in a stability margin that falls below the required threshold, particularly at the band edges.

To address this, I introduce a second-stage common-gate (CG) amplifier with a series inductor and capacitor, which adds a zero to the feedback loop, improving phase margin and stabilizing the system over a wider frequency range. This topology change enhances the stability margin without significantly degrading gain or noise performance.
