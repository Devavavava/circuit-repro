The binding constraint is the insufficient forward gain (s21_db), which is limited by the poor impedance transformation and signal coupling in the current-reuse push-pull configuration. The anchor netlist uses a shared DC path with a current mirror and diode-connected transistors, which introduces parasitic effects and reduces the effective transconductance at the operating frequency. Additionally, the shared DC path and the use of a diode-connected NMOS (NM3) for biasing may introduce signal loss and degrade the gain at the center frequency (2.4 GHz).

To address this, the topology is restructured to use a single-stage common-gate (CG) configuration with an inductive source degeneration. This configuration offers better impedance matching and higher transconductance at the desired frequency. The CG stage is coupled to a resonant LC tank (L2 and C2), which enhances the gain at the center frequency by providing a low-impedance path for the signal. The removal of the diode-connected NMOS (NM3) and the shared DC path reduces parasitic effects and signal loss, allowing for a more efficient signal amplification. The inductive source degeneration (L1) and the resonant load (L2 and C2) work together to boost the gain and stabilize the operation at 2.4 GHz.
