The binding constraint is the insufficient gain (s21_db), which is limited by the poor transconductance of the NMOS amplifier (NM1) and the inadequate current-reuse configuration. The original topology relies on a diode-connected NMOS (NM3) and a PMOS current mirror (PM2) to set the bias current, which may not provide sufficient current to the amplifier core at the operating frequency. This leads to a weak signal amplification at 2.4 GHz, causing the gain to fall short of the target by about 0.74 dB.

To address this, I replaced the diode-connected NM3 with a PMOS transistor (ZP1) connected to the gate of NM3, forming a PMOS current source that provides a more stable and higher current to the NMOS amplifier (NM1). This change increases the transconductance of the amplifier core, thereby boosting the gain at the target frequency. The PMOS current source (ZP1) is connected to the same internal node (n5) as the original NM3, ensuring that it operates under the same bias conditions without introducing additional complexity. This structural change improves the s21_db by increasing the effective transconductance of the amplifier, directly addressing the gain limitation.
