The binding constraint is the insufficient forward gain (s21_db), which is limited by the poor impedance matching and the weak signal amplification in the current-reuse topology. The anchor netlist employs a push-pull configuration with a shared DC path, which introduces parasitic effects and degrades the gain at the operating frequency. The current-reuse structure, while beneficial for noise, may not be optimally configured for high-gain at 2.4 GHz due to the mismatch between the NMOS and PMOS stages and the lack of a dedicated tuning element for the output.

To address this, I propose a structural change that replaces the push-pull configuration with a cascode topology. This change introduces a second NMOS transistor (NM2) in series with NM1, forming a cascode stage. This configuration improves the output impedance and enhances the gain by reducing the Miller effect and improving the transconductance. Additionally, adding a second inductor (L2) and a capacitor (C2) in the output path provides better impedance tuning at the operating frequency, ensuring a more favorable phase and magnitude response for s21_db. The removal of the PMOS-based current mirror (PM2, R4, R5) and the simplification of the biasing network reduce parasitic effects and improve the overall stability and gain.
