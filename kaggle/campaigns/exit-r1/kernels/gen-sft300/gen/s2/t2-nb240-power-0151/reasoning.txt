The binding constraint is the insufficient forward gain (s21_db), which is limited by the current-reuse topology's inherent trade-off between gain and noise figure. The anchor netlist employs a push-pull configuration with a shared DC path, which introduces parasitic effects and impedance mismatches that degrade the gain at the target frequency. Additionally, the shared DC path and the use of a current mirror for biasing increase the noise contribution and reduce the signal fidelity.

To address this, I propose a structural change that eliminates the shared DC path and reconfigures the current-reuse mechanism to reduce parasitic coupling and improve impedance matching. This is achieved by removing the NMOS current mirror (NM3) and its associated resistors (R1, R2), and instead using a separate bias network for the NMOS transistor (NM1) through an additional NMOS transistor (NM2) and resistor (R2). This change ensures a more stable and isolated biasing scheme for NM1, reducing noise and improving gain. The PMOS transistor (PM1) is now directly biased by a resistor (R1) connected to VDD, simplifying the signal path and reducing impedance mismatches. This structural change improves the signal integrity and forward gain at the target frequency.
