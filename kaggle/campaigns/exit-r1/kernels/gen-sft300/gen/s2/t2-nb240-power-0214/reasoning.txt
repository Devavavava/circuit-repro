The binding constraint `mu_min_wide_0.01-50GHz` measures the stability of the LNA across a wide frequency range. The negative margin indicates that the circuit is unstable outside the narrowband center frequency (f0=2.4 GHz). This instability is likely due to the second stage's lack of a feedback path or damping mechanism, which causes the output impedance to vary unpredictably across frequencies, leading to potential oscillations or poor phase margin at off-band frequencies.

In the anchor netlist, the second stage (NM2) has no direct connection to the bias network or any feedback path that could stabilize its output impedance. The LC interstage (L2 and C2) provides resonance at f0 but does not inherently suppress oscillations or stabilize the output at off-band frequencies. This lack of damping is the primary cause of the instability.

To address this, I added a feedback path from the output of the second stage (n6) to the bias network (n7) via an inductor (ZL1) and a capacitor (ZC2). This creates a low-impedance path for high-frequency signals to return to the bias network, effectively damping the output impedance and stabilizing the circuit across a wider frequency range. Additionally, a resistor (ZR1) is placed between the output (VOUT1) and ground to provide a controlled load and further improve stability at off-band frequencies.
