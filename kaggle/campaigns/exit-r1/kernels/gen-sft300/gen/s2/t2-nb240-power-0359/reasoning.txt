The failure is due to insufficient gain (s21_db) in the resistive shunt-feedback CS LNA. The binding constraint is s21_db being too low by ~0.3 dB. The structure uses a shunt-peaked inductive load, which is effective for bandwidth extension but may not maximize gain at a specific frequency. The feedback resistor (R1) sets the DC bias but also introduces a trade-off in gain and stability. The lack of an active feedback path and the single-stage topology likely limits the achievable gain at the target frequency.

To address this, I propose a structural change to a two-stage topology with active feedback and an additional NMOS transistor to improve gain and stability. The new netlist includes an additional NMOS transistor (NM3) and a PMOS transistor (PM2) to form an active feedback path, which enhances gain and stabilizes the amplifier at the target frequency. The addition of a resistor (R4) and a capacitor (ZC1) helps in biasing and stabilizing the second stage.
