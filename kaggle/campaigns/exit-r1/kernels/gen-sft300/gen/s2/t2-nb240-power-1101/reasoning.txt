The binding constraint `mu_min_wide_0.01-50GHz` is failing due to poor stability across the wide frequency range. This is likely caused by the LC interstage resonant network (C2 and L2), which introduces a second resonance that interacts poorly with the feedback and gain of the two-stage amplifier. The LC interstage network, while effective for narrowband matching and gain boosting at the center frequency (f0), does not provide sufficient phase margin or damping over the 0.01–50 GHz band. This results in a stability factor (`mu`) that dips below the required minimum, especially at frequencies away from f0, leading to potential oscillation or instability.

To address this, I replace the LC interstage network with a resistive interstage coupling, using a resistor (ZR2) in place of the inductor L2 and capacitor C2. This change eliminates the resonant feedback path that causes instability outside the narrowband tuning frequency, while still allowing DC coupling between stages. The resistor ZR2 provides a controlled impedance that dampens unwanted resonances and ensures a more stable phase response across the wide frequency range. The rest of the topology remains unchanged, preserving the two-stage CS structure and the LC load tuning for the output stage.
