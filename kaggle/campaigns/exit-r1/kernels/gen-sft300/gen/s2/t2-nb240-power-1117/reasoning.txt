The failure is primarily due to insufficient gain (s21_db) in the current-reuse push-pull LNA topology. The anchor netlist uses a shared DC path with a current mirror and a tuned load, which may lead to poor signal coupling and insufficient voltage gain at the operating frequency. The gain is limited by the configuration of the NMOS and PMOS stages, which may not be optimally aligned for maximum transconductance and impedance matching at 2.4 GHz.

To address this, I introduce a new topology that uses a common-gate stage (NM2) for the NMOS path and a common-source stage for the PMOS path, with a separate DC path for the PMOS stage. This change improves the signal coupling and impedance matching by decoupling the DC paths and ensuring better transconductance contribution from both NMOS and PMOS devices. Additionally, the new topology includes a separate DC path for the PMOS stage (via R R4 and C C4), which helps in stabilizing the operating point and reducing noise contributions from the DC path.
