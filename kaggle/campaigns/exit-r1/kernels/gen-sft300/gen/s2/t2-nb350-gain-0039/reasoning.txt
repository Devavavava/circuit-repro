The failure is primarily due to the noise figure (nf_db) being too high, exceeding the specification by approximately 0.6 dB. This is likely caused by the noise contribution from the resistive shunt feedback network (R1) and the lack of a proper noise-cancelling mechanism in the amplifier topology. The resistive feedback introduces significant noise, especially at the input, which degrades the noise figure. Additionally, the single-stage common-source (CS) configuration with a shunt-peaked inductive load may not be optimal for noise performance at the specified frequency.

To address this, I propose a structural change to the topology by introducing a second NMOS transistor (NM2) in a cascode configuration and adding an inductive peaking element (L2) in the output stage. This modification helps reduce the noise contribution from the feedback resistor and improves the noise figure by providing a more efficient noise-cancelling mechanism. The additional inductor (L2) also helps in stabilizing the output and improving the gain-bandwidth product. The new configuration introduces a second stage that enhances the overall noise performance and ensures the amplifier meets the required specifications.
