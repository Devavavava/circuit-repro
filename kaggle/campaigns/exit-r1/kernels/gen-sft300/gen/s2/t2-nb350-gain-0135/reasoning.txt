The binding constraint is the insufficient forward gain (s21_db), which is too low by ~0.73 dB. This is likely due to the push-pull topology and the current-reuse structure introducing parasitic losses and signal degradation at the output. The tuned load (L2+C2) is not effectively boosting the gain due to the shared DC path and the lack of direct signal coupling from the input to the output. Additionally, the diode-connected NM3 and PM2 are not optimally biasing the amplifier, leading to suboptimal transconductance and gain.

To address this, I propose a structural change to a single-stage common-gate (CG) LNA with a resonant inductor at the input and a resistive load. This topology enhances gain by reducing the Miller effect and improving the signal coupling. The resonant inductor (L1) at the input boosts the gain at the target frequency, while the resistive load (R2) provides a stable output impedance. The NMOS transistor (NM1) is directly connected to the input, eliminating the push-pull structure and reducing signal degradation. This change should significantly improve the forward gain while maintaining low noise and stability.
