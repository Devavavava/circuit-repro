The binding constraint is the gain (s21_db), which is insufficient by ~0.73 dB. This is likely due to the current-reuse topology's inability to maintain sufficient transconductance at the operating frequency, possibly exacerbated by the shared DC path and the tuned load configuration. The shared DC path introduces parasitic effects and limits the dynamic range of the active devices, reducing gain. The tuned load (L2+C2) may also be causing impedance mismatches or resonant losses at the operating frequency, degrading the gain.

To address this, I propose a structural change that replaces the shared DC path with a separate AC path for the input and output, reducing parasitic coupling and improving signal integrity. This is achieved by connecting the input capacitor (C1) to a new internal node (n4), and using a separate inductor (L2) to couple the output node (n2) to the new internal node (n4), while maintaining the tuned load on a separate node (n6). This change isolates the AC signal path from the DC biasing network, reducing parasitic interactions and allowing the amplifier to achieve higher gain at the target frequency.
