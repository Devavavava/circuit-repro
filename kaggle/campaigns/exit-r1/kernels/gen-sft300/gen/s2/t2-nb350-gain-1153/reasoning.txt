The failure is primarily due to the poor stability margin (mu_min_wide_0.01-50GHz) across the wide frequency band, which is critical for ensuring the LNA remains unconditionally stable. The original topology lacks sufficient feedback or damping mechanisms at off-band frequencies, leading to a low stability factor (mu) at frequencies outside the narrowband center frequency (f0). The current mirror biasing and the LC-tuned loads provide strong performance at f0, but they do not sufficiently suppress oscillatory modes at other frequencies, especially in the lower and upper edges of the 0.01–50 GHz band.

To address this, I added a feedback path from the output to the bias network using a capacitor (ZC2) and a resistor (ZR2) to provide a low-frequency damping mechanism. This helps stabilize the amplifier at lower frequencies where the LC resonances are less effective. Additionally, I introduced a resistor (ZR1) between VDD and the input node (n1) to provide a small amount of noise suppression and improve the stability margin by adding a controlled impedance path at the input. These changes do not interfere with the narrowband gain and noise figure performance but enhance the stability across the entire frequency band.
