The binding constraint `mu_min_wide_0.01-50GHz` is failing due to poor stability across the wide frequency range. The original topology relies on LC tuning for both stages, which is effective at the center frequency (3.5 GHz) but insufficient to maintain stability at off-center frequencies. The lack of a dedicated stabilizing element in the interstage network (e.g., a capacitor or resistor) allows for unwanted feedback paths, reducing the stability margin (`mu`) at frequencies outside the narrowband tuning range.

To address this, I introduce a stabilizing capacitor `ZC1` between VDD and node n4, which is part of the LC interstage network. This capacitor provides an additional low-impedance path for high-frequency signals, suppressing feedback and improving the stability margin across the wide band. This structural change adds a passive element that does not interfere with the narrowband gain and noise figure but enhances the stability margin at off-center frequencies.
