The binding constraint `mu_min_wide_0.01-50GHz` is failing due to poor stability across the wide frequency band, likely caused by the LC interstage network and the lack of feedback or damping mechanisms in the current topology. The two-stage structure, while effective at the center frequency (3.5 GHz), does not inherently suppress oscillations or phase shifts at off-band frequencies, leading to a low stability margin (`mu_min`) in the 0.01–50 GHz range. This is exacerbated by the absence of a feedback path or active stabilization element in the original design.

To address this, I added a feedback path from the output (`VOUT1`) to the first-stage gate (`n1`) via a capacitor (`ZC1`) and a resistor (`ZR1`) to the second-stage source node (`n5`). This introduces a controlled feedback mechanism that stabilizes the amplifier over a broader frequency range. Additionally, a resistor (`Rrep1`) from the second-stage source to the first-stage source (`n2`) helps dampen oscillations and improve phase margin by reducing the effective gain of the second stage at higher frequencies. These changes enhance the stability margin without significantly degrading the gain or noise figure.
