The binding constraint is the stability factor `mu_min_wide_0.01-50GHz`, which measures the stability of the LNA across a wide frequency range. The failure indicates that the circuit is unstable at some frequency within this band, likely due to the feedback topology and load configuration in the anchor netlist. The resistive shunt feedback and shunt-peaked inductive load may be causing a resonant peak in the input or output impedance, leading to a dip in the stability factor at certain frequencies.

To address this, I propose a structural change that introduces a second NMOS transistor in a common-gate configuration, along with a capacitor and inductor to form a series-peaking network. This adds a second stage that helps stabilize the circuit by providing a broader frequency range of negative feedback and reducing the resonant effects of the original shunt-peaked load. The additional transistor and peaking network help to increase the stability factor across the wideband by improving the phase margin and reducing the likelihood of oscillation.
