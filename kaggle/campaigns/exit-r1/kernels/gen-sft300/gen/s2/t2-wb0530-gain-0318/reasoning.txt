The failure is due to the insufficient stability margin (mu_min_wide_0.01-50GHz) caused by the feedback and load configuration in the original circuit. The resistive shunt feedback and shunt-peaked inductor-load combination, while effective for gain and input matching, do not sufficiently suppress oscillations across the wideband frequency range, especially at the lower end of the band. This leads to a negative stability margin, violating the mu_min constraint.

To address this, I added a second NMOS transistor (NM2) in a common-gate configuration, connected between the output of the shunt-peaked inductor (n3) and the input node (n1), with its source tied to VDD. This provides additional phase compensation and enhances stability by introducing a low-impedance path to ground at higher frequencies, thereby increasing the stability margin across the entire band. The addition of NM2 modifies the feedback and load interaction, improving the phase response and ensuring a positive stability margin without degrading the gain or input match.
