The binding constraint is the minimum stability factor `mu_min_wide_0.01-50GHz`, which is failing due to insufficient stability across the wideband frequency range. The original topology uses a current-reuse push-pull configuration with a shared DC path, which likely introduces parasitic feedback paths and insufficient isolation between the input and output, especially at higher frequencies. This results in a low stability margin, as evidenced by the `mu_min` being just below the threshold (0.999966 vs. 1.0).

To address this, I introduced a dedicated NMOS transistor (NM3) connected between VDD and the internal node n5, which was previously unconnected to VDD. This change adds a direct DC path from VDD to n5, improving the stability of the biasing network and reducing the risk of parasitic oscillations. Additionally, I connected the source of PM2 (a previously unused node) to n5, ensuring that the PMOS current mirror has a stable reference point. These changes help decouple the biasing network from the signal path and enhance the stability factor across the wideband frequency range.
