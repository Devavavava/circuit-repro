The binding constraint is the noise figure (nf_max_db), which is exceeded by approximately 0.1956 dB. This suggests that the noise contribution from the input transistor NM1 is too high, likely due to its high noise-generating characteristics in the current-reuse topology. The noise figure is dominated by the noise from the first transconductance stage, and the current-reuse configuration may be introducing additional noise or reducing the effective noise matching at the input.

To address this, I propose a structural change that introduces a noise-canceling feedback mechanism by adding a PMOS transistor (ZP1) connected between the gate of NM1 and the output node (n4), which is part of the tuned load (L2+C2). This change creates a noise-canceling path from the output back to the input transistor's gate, effectively reducing the noise contribution from NM1 by providing a low-noise feedback signal. This feedback helps to lower the overall noise figure while maintaining the desired gain and stability characteristics.

The addition of ZP1 also provides a new DC path for the noise-canceling feedback, which is essential for maintaining the proper biasing and operation of the LNA without introducing additional noise sources. This change does not interfere with the existing current-reuse topology or the tuned load, and it is structurally distinct from the anchor netlist.
