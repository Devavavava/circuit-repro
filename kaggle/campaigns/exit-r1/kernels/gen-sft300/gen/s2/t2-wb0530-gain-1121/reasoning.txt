The failure is due to insufficient stability margin (mu_min_wide_0.01-50GHz), which is critical for ensuring unconditional stability across the wideband frequency range. The original topology uses a resistive shunt feedback configuration with a shunt-peaked inductive load, which, while effective for wideband gain and input matching, does not inherently provide sufficient phase margin at higher frequencies, especially in the presence of process variations and parasitics. The lack of a dedicated stability-enhancing element in the feedback path limits the phase margin, leading to a negative stability margin at the band edges.

To address this, I added a PMOS transistor (PM1) in a common-gate configuration, which introduces a second feedback path and enhances phase margin by providing an additional low-impedance path for high-frequency signals. This configuration also helps in stabilizing the amplifier by reducing the feedback loop's phase lag at higher frequencies. Additionally, I introduced a capacitor (ZC1) between the inductor (L1) and the output node (n2), which further helps in shaping the frequency response and improving stability by reducing peaking and enhancing phase margin. A resistor (ZR1) was also added between the output node (n2) and the supply (VDD) to provide a controlled load and improve the output impedance matching, which helps in maintaining stability over the wideband.
