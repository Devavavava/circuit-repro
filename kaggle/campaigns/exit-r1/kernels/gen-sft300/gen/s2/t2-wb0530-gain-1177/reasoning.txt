The failure is primarily due to the noise figure (nf_max_db) exceeding the specification, which is likely caused by the noise contribution from the NMOS transistor NM3 in the biasing network. This diode-connected NMOS is part of the current mirror reference, and its noise is amplified through the signal path due to the current reuse topology, especially at the high frequency of operation (1.22 GHz). The noise from NM3 is not effectively suppressed, leading to a higher-than-allowed noise figure.

To address this, I propose replacing the diode-connected NM3 with a PMOS transistor (ZP1) connected in a similar configuration but with its source tied to VDD and its drain connected to n5, which is the node connected to the gate of NM1. This change introduces a PMOS transistor in the biasing network, which has lower noise contribution at high frequencies compared to NMOS due to its lower mobility and different noise characteristics. This structural change effectively reduces the noise contribution from the biasing network, thereby lowering the overall noise figure of the LNA.
