The failure is primarily due to noise figure (NF) being too high, exceeding the maximum allowed value of 2.5187 dB by approximately 0.25 dB. This is likely caused by the noise contribution from the biasing network, particularly from NM3 and PM2, which are connected to the internal nodes and may introduce additional noise due to their configuration. Additionally, the current-reuse topology may not be optimally suppressing noise from the biasing path, especially at the operating frequency.

To address this, I propose a structural change that reduces the noise contribution from the bias network and improves the noise performance. This is achieved by reconfiguring the biasing network to use a separate NMOS transistor (NM3) in a diode-connected configuration with its drain and gate tied to the same node (n5), but instead of connecting it directly to the DC path, it is connected to an internal node (n4) that is shared with the tuned load (L2+C2). This change isolates the noise-sensitive biasing path from the main signal path and reduces the noise contribution from the biasing network. Additionally, the PMOS transistor (PM2) is now connected to the internal node (n4) rather than VSS, which helps in reducing noise coupling and improving the noise figure.
