The failure is primarily due to poor input matching, as evidenced by the s11_max_db constraint being violated by ~0.498 dB. The anchor netlist uses a simple inductor-capacitor (L1-C4) input DC-blocking network, which may not provide sufficient impedance transformation or bandwidth support for the wideband operation at 1.22 GHz. This results in a suboptimal reflection coefficient at the input port, especially at the band edge, leading to a lower-than-required return loss.

To address this, I introduce a series inductor (L3) between the internal node n5 and the gate of NM3, which helps improve impedance matching by providing additional reactance at the input frequency. This change enhances the input matching by allowing better control over the input impedance, particularly at the operating frequency and across the wideband range. The inductor L3 also helps in decoupling the bias network from the signal path, reducing unwanted interactions that could degrade the input matching.
