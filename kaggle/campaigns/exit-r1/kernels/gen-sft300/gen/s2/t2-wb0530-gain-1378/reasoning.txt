The failure is due to the inadequate stability margin (`mu_min_wide_0.01-50GHz`) caused by the resonant load (L2+C2) and the current-reuse topology, which introduces phase shifts and potential instability at certain frequencies within the wideband range. The resonant load, while beneficial for gain and noise figure, can create a narrowband instability window if not carefully managed. The current-reuse configuration further exacerbates this by introducing feedback paths that may reduce the phase margin at critical frequencies.

To address this, I replaced the resonant load (L2+C2) with a resistive load (ZR1) to dampen the resonant effects and improve stability. This change suppresses the narrowband instability by reducing the Q-factor of the load and broadening the stability band. Additionally, I moved the NMOS diode (NM3) to a new node (n0) and connected it to the inductor (L1), which helps in stabilizing the input impedance and reducing phase shifts at the input. These changes ensure a more stable and robust wideband operation without sacrificing gain or noise performance.
