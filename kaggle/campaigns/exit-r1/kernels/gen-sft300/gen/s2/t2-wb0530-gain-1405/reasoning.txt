The failure is primarily due to noise figure (nf_max_db) exceeding the specification, which is caused by the noise contribution from the biasing network and the lack of noise isolation in the current-reuse configuration. The anchor netlist uses a diode-connected NMOS (NM3) and PMOS (PM2) for biasing, which introduces noise and degrades the noise performance of the LNA. Additionally, the current-reuse topology, while beneficial for gain, can amplify noise if not properly managed.

To address this, I redesigned the biasing network to use a resistor-based approach instead of diode-connected transistors, which reduces noise injection. I also restructured the current-reuse path by connecting the NMOS (NM1) and PMOS (PM1) to a shared current sink (ZN1), which improves noise isolation and stability. The addition of a resistor (ZR1) between VDD and the bias reference point (n5) helps in stabilizing the biasing network and reducing noise. These changes effectively lower the noise figure while maintaining the required gain and stability.
