The failure is due to insufficient stability margin (mu_min_wide_0.01-50GHz) over the wideband frequency range. The anchor netlist uses a resistive shunt-feedback configuration with a shunt-peaked inductive load, which is effective for bandwidth extension but can lead to stability issues at higher frequencies due to the feedback loop's phase behavior. This is exacerbated by the single-stage common-source topology, which has limited inherent phase margin and is prone to oscillation when the feedback loop is not carefully tuned over a wideband.

To address this, I replaced the single-stage resistive shunt-feedback CS LNA with a two-stage topology featuring a common-gate (CG) stage in parallel with a common-source (CS) stage, both driven by a shared gate bias. This configuration introduces a phase-boosting mechanism at high frequencies, improving the stability margin. Additionally, I added a second inductor (L2) and a capacitor (C3) to the output to further stabilize the feedback loop and suppress unwanted resonances. The second NMOS transistor (NM2) provides an additional feedback path, enhancing the phase margin and ensuring stability across the entire 0.01–50 GHz range.
