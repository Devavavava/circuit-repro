The noise figure (NF) is the binding constraint, failing by ~0.099 dB. This suggests that the noise contribution from the input transistor or the biasing network is too high for the target specification. The original circuit uses a diode-connected NMOS (NM3) and PMOS (PM2) for biasing, which introduces noise due to their non-ideal behavior and the resistive elements (R1, R2, R4, R5) in the bias network. These resistors and the diode-connected transistors act as noise sources, degrading the noise figure. Additionally, the current-reuse topology may not be optimally configured for noise suppression at the target frequency.

To address this, I replaced the diode-connected NM3 with a resistive load (ZR1) connected to the bias node n5, and removed the diode-connected NM3 and PM2 from the bias network. This eliminates the noise contribution from the diode-connected transistors and reduces the number of resistive elements in the bias path. The resistive load ZR1 provides a stable bias point without introducing additional noise. This change reduces the noise floor by removing the noisy diode-connected transistors and simplifies the biasing network, leading to a lower noise figure.
