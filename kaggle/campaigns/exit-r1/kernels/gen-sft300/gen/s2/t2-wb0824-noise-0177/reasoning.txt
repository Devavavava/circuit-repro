The failure is primarily due to the insufficient stability margin (mu_min_wide_0.01-50GHz) in the original circuit, which is below the required minimum of 1.0. The resistive shunt feedback and the inductor-capacitor load configuration may not provide enough phase margin at higher frequencies, especially in the absence of active compensation.

To address this, I propose a structural change by replacing the resistive shunt feedback with a NMOS transistor (NM2) connected between the feedback node (n2) and the output node (VOUT1), and adding an additional capacitor (ZC1) between the output node (VOUT1) and the internal node (n3). This change introduces a new feedback path that provides active stabilization and improves phase margin across the wideband range. The NMOS transistor (NM2) acts as a low-impedance path for AC signals, helping to dampen oscillations, while the capacitor (ZC1) provides additional phase compensation at higher frequencies.
