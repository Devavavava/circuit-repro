The noise figure (NF) is the binding constraint, and it is failing due to the inadequate noise performance of the current-reuse topology. The anchor circuit uses a diode-connected NMOS (NM3) and PMOS (PM2) for biasing, which introduces noise and limits the achievable NF. Additionally, the shared DC path and the lack of proper noise isolation between the input and the active devices contribute to the elevated noise floor. The noise performance is further degraded by the use of resistive bias networks (R1, R2, R4, R5), which are not optimal for low-noise applications.

To address this, I restructured the biasing network to eliminate resistive noise contributors and improve noise isolation. I replaced the diode-connected NM3 with a NMOS transistor (NM4) that is biased through a separate DC path (R2 and C2), reducing the noise contribution from the biasing network. Additionally, I introduced a new NMOS transistor (NM3) with a different connection to isolate the biasing network from the signal path, and I replaced the PMOS diode (PM2) with a PMOS transistor (PM2) connected to a new node (n6) to ensure a cleaner gate bias. The inductor L2 was reconnected to VDD to improve noise isolation at the output stage. These changes reduce the noise contribution from the biasing network and improve the noise performance of the LNA.
