The binding constraint is the minimum stability factor (mu_min_wide_0.01-50GHz), which is failing due to insufficient stability margin across the wideband frequency range. The original topology, while optimized for noise and gain, lacks sufficient feedback or damping mechanisms to ensure stability at all frequencies, especially in the lower and upper edges of the band. This is likely due to the absence of a direct feedback path from the output to the input or a dedicated stability-enhancing element.

To address this, I introduced a feedback capacitor (ZC1) connecting the output node (n2) to the gate of the NMOS transistor (NM3), which acts as a feedback path from the output to the input stage. This change enhances the stability of the amplifier by introducing a controlled phase shift and damping oscillations. Additionally, I added a resistor (ZR3) between the output (n2) and a new internal node (x1), and another resistor (ZR4) between the internal node (x1) and the DC path (n5). These components help to regulate the feedback strength and provide a low-impedance path for any oscillatory components, further improving stability. The capacitor (ZC1) and resistors (ZR3 and ZR4) work together to ensure the stability factor remains above the required minimum across the entire band.
