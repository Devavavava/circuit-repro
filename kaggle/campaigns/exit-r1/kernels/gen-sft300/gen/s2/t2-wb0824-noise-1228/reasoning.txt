The failure is primarily due to noise figure (NF) being too high, exceeding the maximum allowed value of 2.0175 dB. The noise performance of the LNA is dominated by the noise contributions from the input transistor and the biasing network. In the anchor netlist, the noise is exacerbated by the use of a diode-connected NMOS (NM3) and a resistive biasing network (R1, R2, R4, R5), which introduces additional noise and degrades the NF. The current-reuse structure, while beneficial for gain and power efficiency, does not sufficiently suppress noise in the wideband operation at the specified frequency (1.385 GHz).

The structural change proposed introduces a new NMOS transistor (ZN1) and a capacitor (ZC1) to decouple the bias network from the signal path. This helps reduce the noise contribution from the biasing network by isolating it from the input and output nodes. The new NMOS (ZN1) acts as an additional noise-canceling element, while the capacitor (ZC1) provides a low-impedance path for high-frequency noise, thereby improving the noise figure.
