The failure is primarily due to the noise figure (NF) being too high, exceeding the specified maximum of 1.9331 dB. The noise contribution is likely dominated by the input transistor NM1 and the biasing network, particularly the diode-connected NM3 and the resistive divider (R1, R2, R4, R5). The current-reuse topology, while effective for gain and power efficiency, may introduce additional noise pathways or degrade the noise performance due to the non-ideal behavior of the biasing network and the shared DC path. The noise from the biasing resistors and the diode-connected NM3 may be coupled into the signal path, increasing the overall noise figure.

To address this, I modify the biasing network by adding a capacitor (ZC2) between VDD and the gate of PM2, and a resistor (ZR1) between the gate of PM2 and the drain of NM3. This decouples the gate of PM2 from the noise sources in the biasing network, reducing the noise contribution from the biasing resistors and diode-connected transistors. The capacitor ZC2 provides a low-impedance path for high-frequency noise, while the resistor ZR1 stabilizes the gate voltage of PM2 and reduces noise coupling. These changes improve the noise figure by isolating the signal path from the noisy biasing network.
