The failure is primarily due to the noise figure (nf_max_db) exceeding the specification. This is likely caused by the noise contribution from the biasing network and the diode-connected NMOS (NM3) and PMOS (PM2) devices, which are part of the current-reuse structure. The diode-connected transistors introduce additional noise, especially at the input stage, which degrades the noise figure. The current-reuse topology also adds complexity to the noise path, making it harder to meet the tight noise constraint.

To address this, the topology is restructured to eliminate the diode-connected transistors (NM3 and PM2) and simplify the biasing network. This reduces the noise contribution from these devices. Additionally, the output load is reconfigured to a simple inductor (L3) and capacitor (C2), which improves the noise performance by reducing the noise coupling from the bias network to the signal path. The input coupling capacitor (C3) is moved to the input node (n0) to ensure DC blocking and reduce noise injection from the bias network.
