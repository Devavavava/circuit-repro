The failure is primarily due to noise figure (nf_max_db) being exceeded by ~0.092 dB, which is critical for the specification. This suggests that the noise contribution from the input transistor NM1 or the biasing network is too high, likely due to the noise generated by the current mirror NM3 and the biasing resistors (R1, R2, R4, R5). The current-reuse topology, while beneficial for gain, may not be optimally suppressing noise in this configuration, especially at the given wideband frequency. The noise from the current mirror and biasing resistors is not sufficiently attenuated, leading to a higher-than-allowed noise figure.

To address this, I replaced the current mirror NM3 and its associated resistors (R1, R2, R4, R5) with a simpler biasing network that reduces noise contributions. Specifically, I removed the current mirror and replaced it with a direct connection from R2 to VDD, and added a capacitor (ZC1) to decouple the bias network from the signal path, reducing noise coupling. Additionally, I replaced the diode-connected NM3 with an NMOS transistor (ZN1) connected to the bias node n5, which helps in stabilizing the bias point and reducing noise. These changes simplify the biasing network and reduce noise contributions from the current mirror and resistors, thereby lowering the overall noise figure.
