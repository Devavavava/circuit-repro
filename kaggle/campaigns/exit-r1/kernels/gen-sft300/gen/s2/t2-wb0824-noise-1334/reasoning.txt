The failure is due to the insufficient stability margin (mu_min_wide_0.01-50GHz) across the wideband frequency range, which is a critical constraint for robust operation. The anchor circuit uses a resistive shunt-feedback configuration with a shunt-peaked inductive load. While this topology supports wideband operation, it lacks sufficient phase margin at higher frequencies, leading to instability. The feedback resistor (R1) and the inductor (L1) in the load are not sufficient to maintain the required stability margin over the entire band.

To address this, I propose a structural change that introduces a second inductor (L2) and an additional NMOS transistor (NM3) in a common-gate configuration. This change adds a second feedback path, improving phase compensation and enhancing the stability margin. The new NMOS transistor (NM3) provides an additional current path and helps balance the feedback loop, while the inductor (L2) introduces additional phase shift to stabilize the system over the wideband range. These additions enhance the stability margin without significantly degrading the noise figure or gain, ensuring compliance with the mu_min_wide constraint.
