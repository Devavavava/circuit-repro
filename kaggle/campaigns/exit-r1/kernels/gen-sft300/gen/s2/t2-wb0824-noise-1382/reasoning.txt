The failure is primarily due to the noise figure (nf_max_db) being too high, exceeding the specification by approximately 0.157 dB. This suggests that the noise contribution from the transistor NM1 is too significant, likely due to its low transconductance and high noise coefficient in the current configuration. The noise is exacerbated by the lack of a proper noise-shaping mechanism in the output stage, particularly because the output is directly connected to the tuned load (L2+C2) without any additional noise-suppression elements.

To address this, I introduced a noise-canceling NMOS transistor (ZN1) in a feedback configuration. This transistor is connected between the output node (n2) and the internal node (n4), with its source tied to VSS and its gate connected to the internal node (n5) that was previously used for biasing. This change creates a local negative feedback path that helps reduce the noise contribution from the main transistor NM1. The feedback path also helps stabilize the output and reduce the overall noise figure by providing an additional noise-canceling mechanism.

The addition of ZN1 effectively lowers the noise figure by reducing the noise contribution from the main transistor and improving the signal-to-noise ratio at the output. This change does not significantly affect the other constraints such as S11, S21, and power consumption, as the feedback is local and does not interfere with the gain or matching of the circuit.
