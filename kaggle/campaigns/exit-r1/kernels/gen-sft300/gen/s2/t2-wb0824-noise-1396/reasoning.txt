The failure is primarily due to noise figure (nf_max_db) exceeding the specification, despite meeting other constraints like input return loss (s11_max_db) and gain (s21_db). The noise figure is dominated by the input transistor (NM1) and the biasing network, which includes the diode-connected NM3 and the resistive divider (R1, R2). The current-reuse topology, while effective for gain and power efficiency, introduces noise contributions from the biasing network and the current mirror (NM3), which are not sufficiently mitigated in the original netlist. This is exacerbated by the fact that the noise contributions from the biasing network are not isolated from the signal path, especially at the high frequency (f0 = 1.385 GHz).

To address this, I restructured the biasing network to isolate the noise sources from the signal path. The diode-connected NM3 is now connected to an internal node (n6) instead of being directly tied to the signal path. This change reduces the noise coupling from the biasing network into the signal path. Additionally, I introduced a new inductor (L3) and a capacitor (C4) to provide a low-noise, high-impedance path for the bias current, reducing the noise contribution from the biasing network. The new inductor (L3) and capacitor (C4) also help in decoupling the bias network from the signal path, improving the noise figure.
