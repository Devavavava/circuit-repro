The failure is primarily due to the insufficient stability margin (mu_min_wide_0.01-50GHz) in the wideband frequency range. The original netlist uses a resistive shunt-feedback configuration with a shunt-peaked inductive load (L1+R2). While this topology is effective for achieving a broadband input match and a high gain, it lacks sufficient feedback and passive damping elements to suppress oscillations and ensure stability across the entire 0.01–50 GHz range. The stability factor (mu) is critical for ensuring unconditional stability, especially in wideband applications, and the original design fails to meet the minimum mu_min requirement due to the absence of additional damping mechanisms.

To address this, I added a capacitor (ZC1) between the drain of the NMOS transistor (n2) and the inductor (n3) and another capacitor (ZC2) between the output (VOUT1) and the internal node (n2). These capacitors provide additional frequency-dependent feedback and damping, effectively increasing the stability margin across the wideband. The capacitor ZC2 also helps in shaping the output impedance and reducing unwanted resonances, while ZC1 provides local feedback at the transistor level, suppressing oscillatory modes.
