The binding constraint is the insufficient forward gain (s21_db), which is limited by the poor impedance transformation and signal attenuation in the current topology. The resistive shunt feedback and shunt-peaked inductor load are causing a mismatch in the broadband frequency range, leading to a roll-off in gain at the target frequency. This is exacerbated by the single-stage common-source configuration, which lacks sufficient gain-bandwidth product to meet the specification at the given high-frequency operating point.

To address this, I propose replacing the resistive shunt feedback with a second NMOS transistor in a cascode configuration, which enhances the output impedance and improves gain. Additionally, I introduce a second inductor and capacitor to form a series-peaking network at the output, which compensates for the frequency-dependent losses in the load and improves the gain flatness. The addition of a PMOS transistor in a current mirror configuration helps stabilize the bias and increase the transconductance of the amplifier. These structural changes improve the impedance matching and boost the forward gain across the wideband frequency range, ensuring the s21_db meets the required specification.
