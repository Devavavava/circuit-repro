The failure is primarily due to the insufficient stability margin (`mu_min_wide_0.01-50GHz`) over the wideband frequency range. The original circuit uses a resistive shunt feedback configuration with a shunt-peaked inductive load, which, while effective for broadband gain and input matching, struggles to maintain sufficient phase margin across the entire 0.01–50 GHz range. The stability factor (`mu`) is marginally below the required threshold, likely due to the feedback network not adequately suppressing oscillatory modes at higher frequencies.

To address this, I introduce a second inductor (L2) and a second NMOS transistor (NM2) to create a current-reuse topology with an additional inductive peaking element. This change adds a second inductive path that enhances the phase margin by introducing a controlled phase shift at higher frequencies. The new inductor (L2) and NMOS (NM2) form a secondary feedback path that helps stabilize the circuit without sacrificing bandwidth or gain. This structural change also improves the noise figure and power consumption by allowing better current distribution and reducing the effective noise contribution from the feedback network.
