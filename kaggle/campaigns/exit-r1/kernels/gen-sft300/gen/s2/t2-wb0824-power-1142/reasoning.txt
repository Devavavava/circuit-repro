The binding constraint is the stability margin (mu_min_wide_0.01-50GHz), which is failing due to insufficient phase margin across the wideband operation. The original circuit uses a resistive shunt-feedback configuration with a shunt-peaked inductive load, which limits the stability margin due to the feedback topology and the lack of active stabilization mechanisms. This configuration struggles to maintain sufficient phase margin across the entire 0.01–50 GHz range, especially at the lower end of the band, leading to instability.

To address this, I replace the resistive shunt feedback with a two-stage topology that includes an additional NMOS transistor (NM2) and a PMOS transistor (PM1) in a common-gate configuration. This change introduces a second stage that provides additional phase compensation and enhances the stability margin. The addition of a capacitor (ZC1) between the output and the first stage's drain node helps to further stabilize the circuit by providing a low-impedance path for high-frequency signals, reducing phase lag. This structural change increases the phase margin across the wideband, directly addressing the failing stability constraint.
