The binding constraint is the stability factor `mu_min_wide_0.01-50GHz`, which measures the stability of the LNA across a wide frequency range. The failure indicates that the circuit is unstable at some frequencies within the 0.01–50 GHz band, as the achieved `mu_min` is 0.963768, below the required minimum of 1.0. This instability is likely due to the feedback paths in the current-reuse topology, particularly the shared DC path and the coupling between the input and output through the tuned load `L2+C2`. These elements can introduce resonances or parasitic feedback that reduce the stability margin, especially at higher frequencies.

To address this, I propose a structural change that isolates the output from the input and DC paths by introducing a separate DC path for the output. This is achieved by adding a capacitor `ZC1` from the output node `VOUT1` to the internal node `n3`, which connects to the drain of the NMOS transistor `NM1`. This capacitor blocks DC signals from propagating back through the output, reducing unwanted feedback and improving the stability margin. Additionally, I removed the capacitor `C3` that directly connects the output to the internal node `n2`, which was likely contributing to the instability by creating a low-impedance path for feedback at certain frequencies.

The netlist change ensures that the output is decoupled from the main signal path, reducing the risk of instability while maintaining the high-gain and low-noise characteristics of the LNA.
