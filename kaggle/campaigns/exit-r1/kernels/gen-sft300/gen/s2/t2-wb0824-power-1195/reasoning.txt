The binding constraint is the minimum stability factor (mu_min_wide_0.01-50GHz), which is failing due to insufficient phase margin and poor stability across the wideband frequency range. This is likely caused by the single-stage common-source (CS) topology with resistive shunt feedback and a shunt-peaked inductive load. The feedback network and load configuration may not provide sufficient phase compensation across the entire band, leading to instability at certain frequencies. The lack of a second stage or additional feedback paths limits the ability to control the phase and gain across the wideband, causing the stability factor to dip below the required threshold.

To address this, I introduce a second stage using a common-gate (CG) configuration in parallel with the original CS stage. This adds an additional feedback path and improves phase compensation. The CG stage (NMOS NM2) is connected to the output of the CS stage (n2) and provides a low-impedance path to ground through a capacitor (C2), which helps in stabilizing the output and improving phase margin. The addition of a capacitor (ZC1) between VDD and n3 and a resistor (ZR1) from n0 to VSS helps in shaping the frequency response and providing additional biasing stability without introducing DC sources. These changes enhance the stability factor across the wideband by improving phase margin and reducing feedback-induced oscillations.
