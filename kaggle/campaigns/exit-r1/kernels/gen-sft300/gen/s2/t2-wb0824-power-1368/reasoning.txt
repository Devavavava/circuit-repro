The failure is due to insufficient stability margin (mu_min_wide_0.01-50GHz) caused by the feedback topology and the shunt-peaked load configuration. The resistive shunt feedback and the inductive peaking introduce phase shifts that reduce the stability margin across the wideband frequency range, especially at the lower end of the band. This results in a negative margin for the stability factor, causing instability and failure to meet the specification.

To address this, I added a second NMOS transistor (NMOS ZN1) and a capacitor (C ZC1) to create a current-reuse topology with an additional feedback path. This enhances the stability by providing an alternative phase compensation mechanism, particularly at lower frequencies. The new NMOS transistor is connected to the output and the drain of NM1, while the capacitor connects to the output and a new internal node, creating a low-frequency feedback path that improves the phase margin and stabilizes the amplifier over the entire band.
