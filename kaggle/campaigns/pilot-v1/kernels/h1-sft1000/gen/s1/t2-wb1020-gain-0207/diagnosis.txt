The failure is due to the insufficient stability margin (`mu_min_wide_0.01-50GHz`) caused by the shared DC path and the lack of proper impedance transformation between the input and output. The current-reuse topology, while efficient, introduces parasitic feedback paths that reduce the stability margin at high frequencies, especially in a wideband context. The shared DC path between the NMOS and PMOS stages also causes poor impedance matching and increased sensitivity to process variations, which further degrades the stability.

To address this, I added a new NMOS transistor (ZN1) and a capacitor (ZC1) to the circuit. The NMOS transistor ZN1 is placed between VDD and the node n3, providing an additional impedance path and helping to isolate the output from the shared DC path. The capacitor ZC1 connects the internal node x1 (the drain of ZN1) to the input node n1, which helps to provide a more stable and predictable impedance transformation at the input, especially over a wide frequency range. This change reduces parasitic feedback and improves the stability margin at high frequencies.
