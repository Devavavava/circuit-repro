### DIAGNOSIS:
The circuit fails primarily due to **poor input matching (S11)**, which is the binding constraint with a margin of -0.98 dB. The S11_max_db is -0.306 dB, far below the required -15 dB. This indicates severe input reflection, likely caused by the **input impedance being too mismatched to 50 Ω**, especially at the target frequency (1.75 GHz). The structure uses a single NMOS transistor (NM1) with its source connected to a low-impedance node (n2), which is driven by an inductor (L1) from VIN1. This topology forms a **series inductor input matching network**, but the lack of a proper **source degeneration or impedance transformation element** prevents effective impedance transformation from the transistor’s low input impedance to 50 Ω.

Additionally, the presence of a **floating NMOS (NM2)** and **floating PMOS (PM2)** with both gate and source tied to the same node (n6 and n7 respectively) is problematic: they are essentially **unbiased and non-functional**, acting as parasitic devices or uncontrolled switches. These floating transistors likely introduce **unpredictable parasitic capacitances and noise**, degrading both S11 and NF. The current biasing network (R1, R2, R3, R4) is also non-standard and likely fails to properly set the DC operating points for the active devices.

The excessive current (4.85 mA) and high noise figure (4.56 dB) further suggest poor biasing and no deliberate feedback or stability mechanisms.
