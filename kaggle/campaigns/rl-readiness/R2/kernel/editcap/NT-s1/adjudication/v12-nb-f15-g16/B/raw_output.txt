### 1. Diagnosis of Failure

The failure is primarily due to the **insufficient gain** in the common-gate (CG) LNA configuration. The common-gate topology inherently provides a low input impedance and a high output impedance, which is useful for certain matching applications. However, the gain of a CG amplifier is typically lower compared to a common-source (CS) amplifier due to the transconductance being effectively bypassed by the input capacitor and the source inductor. Additionally, the drain load (L2) is reactive and cannot provide the necessary amplification at 1.6 GHz. The output is DC-blocked by C2, and the input is DC-blocked by C1, which may lead to a loss of signal amplitude across the frequency band.

The **s21_db** (forward gain) is the binding constraint, and it is failing by ~0.47 dB below the target of 16 dB. This indicates that the circuit is **not achieving sufficient amplification** at the center frequency of 1.58 GHz.

### 2. Structural Edits

#### **Edit 1: Convert Common-Gate to Common-Source with Source Degeneration for Gain and Stability**
This approach replaces the common-gate configuration with a common-source (CS) topology, which inherently provides higher gain. Source degeneration is added to stabilize the amplifier and improve linearity. The input is now at the gate, and the source is AC-grounded through a small capacitor. The output is still DC-blocked by C2.

```netlist
C C1 VIN1 n0
R Rsrc n0 n1
NMOS M1 n2 n1 VSS VSS
L L2 VDD n2
C C2 n2 VOUT1
R R1 VDD n3
NMOS NM3 n3 n3 VSS VSS
R R2 n3 n1
C Cbyp n1 VSS
```

**Predicted impact:**
- `s21_db` improves by **~3 dB** due to the higher gain of the CS configuration.
- `s11_db` may slightly worsen by **~1 dB**, depending on the input matching.
- `NF` may worsen slightly, but the trade-off is justified for increased gain.

---

#### **Edit 2: Add a Second Amplifier Stage in Cascade with a Common-Source Configuration**
This configuration adds a second amplifier stage in cascade to boost the gain further. The first stage is a common-gate (as in the original), and the second stage is a common-source. The second stage provides additional gain and helps compensate for the lower gain of the first stage.

```netlist
C C1 VIN1 n0
L L1 n0 VSS
NMOS M1 n2 n1 n0 VSS
L L2 VDD n2
C C2 n2 n3
C C3 n3 n4
NMOS M2 n4 n5 VSS VSS
L L3 VDD n4
C C4 n4 VOUT1
R R1 VDD n6
NMOS NM3 n6 n6 VSS VSS
R R2 n6 n1
C Cbyp n1 VSS
```

**Predicted impact:**
- `s21_db` improves by **~4 dB**, as the second stage adds significant gain.
- `s11_db` may worsen by **~1.5 dB** due to the second stage’s input impedance.
- `NF` may worsen slightly, but the gain increase is worth the trade-off.

---

#### **Edit 3: Use a Cascode Configuration with a Current Source Load for Higher Gain and Stability**
This configuration uses a cascode topology, which increases the output impedance and thus the gain. The drain load is now a current source formed by a NMOS transistor operating in the saturation region. This increases the effective transconductance and reduces the Miller effect, leading to better gain and stability.

```netlist
C C1 VIN1 n0
L L1 n0 VSS
NMOS M1 n2 n1 n0 VSS
NMOS M2 n2 n3 VSS VSS
L L2 VDD n3
C C2 n2 VOUT1
R R1 VDD n4
NMOS NM3 n4 n4 VSS VSS
R R2 n4 n1
C Cbyp n1 VSS
```

**Predicted impact:**
- `s21_db` improves by **~2.5 dB** due to the higher output impedance and transconductance.
- `s11_db` may worsen by **~1 dB**, as the input impedance becomes slightly less matched.
- `NF` may remain stable or slightly worsen, but the gain increase is worth it for meeting the s21 requirement.
