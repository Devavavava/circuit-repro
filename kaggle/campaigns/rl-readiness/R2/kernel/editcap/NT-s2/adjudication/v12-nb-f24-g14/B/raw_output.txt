### 1. Diagnosis of the Failure

The binding constraint is **s21_db**, which is the gain of the LNA. The achieved value is only **9.45 dB**, which is **0.325 dB below the required 14 dB**, causing the design to fail. The issue is likely due to the **common-gate topology**, which has **inherently limited gain**, especially when the input is DC-blocked and the source is the input terminal. The gain of a common-gate amplifier is typically **less than 1** in the absence of external inductive loading, and even with inductive loading (as in this case with **L1**), the gain is constrained by the **output impedance of the transistor and the load network**. Furthermore, the **source degeneration inductance** (L1) may be limiting the transconductance contribution to the gain, especially at the center frequency of 2.4 GHz.

### 2. Structural Edits (Topologies Only)

#### Edit 1: Convert to a Common-Source topology with inductive source degeneration
```netlist
C C1 VIN1 n0
L L1 n0 VSS
NMOS NM1 n2 n1 VSS VSS
L L2 n2 VDD
C C2 n2 VOUT1
R R1 VDD n3
NMOS NM3 n3 n3 VSS VSS
R R2 n3 n1
C Cbyp n1 VSS
```

**Predicted Effect**: s21_db improves by **~3–5 dB**, as the common-source configuration inherently has higher gain than common-gate.

#### Edit 2: Add a cascode stage to boost gain

L L1 n0 VSS

```

**Predicted Effect**: s21_db improves by **~4–6 dB**, as the cascode stage provides higher output impedance and boosts gain.

#### Edit 3: Add a source inductor and a load inductor with a resistor in parallel to increase bandwidth and gain

L L1 n0 VSS
NMOS NM1 n2 n1 VSS VSS
L L2 n2 n3
R Rload n3 VDD

```

**Predicted Effect**: s21_db improves by **~2–4 dB**, and the added inductors help increase the bandwidth and transconductance contribution, thereby improving gain.
